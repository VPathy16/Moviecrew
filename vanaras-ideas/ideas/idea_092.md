# Renewly — agents that own your household's paperwork, renewals and forms end to end
Lens: Wedge: life admin (paperwork, renewals, forms)
## Customer & pain
Paying: dual-income professionals aged 30-50 (US/UK/EU first, then India/SEA) and, later, their employers as a benefit. Pain: a steady drip of deadlines (passport, visa, vehicle registration, insurance, tax filings, school forms, warranty claims, subscription cancellations). Missing one costs fines, lapsed cover or lost refunds. Today it lives in email, photos of documents and one stressed partner's head. (Assumption: the average household has 30-60 recurring obligations per year; a guess.)
## Product
Phone vanara (Paperwork): scans a document with the camera and extracts the deadline, issuer and required steps. It reminds, calls or messages the counterparty, and asks for approval before anything is submitted or paid. Personal data (ID numbers, policy numbers) is held as reference tokens and only filled into forms on the device.
Cloud vanara (Case worker): runs a "case" for each obligation, for example a visa renewal. It watches portals and inboxes 24/7, drafts the forms, tracks the appointment slots and chases the status while the phone is off.
Desktop vanara (Filer): fills government and insurer web forms in the browser using the local tokens. It stops at every submit for approval.
Shared memory holds one household ledger of documents, deadlines, people and past cases. Agent-to-agent handover: phone captures the letter, cloud opens the case, desktop files, phone asks "approve GBP 85 fee?". A partner or an accountant can be added as a second approver.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
Wedge: "the renewal radar". Photograph or forward your documents and it builds a deadline calendar, then runs the first renewals (car insurance re-quote, passport, vehicle tax) with approvals. First 10 customers: expat and immigrant households, who face the most paperwork, found through expat forums and relocation consultants. Charge $15 a month from day one. Founder's own family is customer zero.
## Enterprise path
Sell "life admin as an employee benefit" to HR, particularly for relocation and immigration (mobility teams, global employers). Each employee gets a private team. The company sets the rules (approved spend caps, allowed services) and sees only aggregate usage, never content. It then expands to work-side admin (expense claims, compliance training, benefits enrolment) on the same platform.
## Business model & pricing
Consumer: $15/month household, or $99/year. BYO model key gives a cheaper $8 tier. Employer: $6-10 per employee per month, or a per-relocation fee of $300-500. Optional marketplace referral income (insurance re-quotes), kept off by default to protect trust.
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
Government and insurer forms are a long tail of country-specific messy workflows with real liability if an agent errs. Big platforms want horizontal assistants and avoid regulated, per-country case work. Apple/Google will not hold on-device tokenised identity data that the AI never sees on a rival's cloud. A neutral, cross-platform vendor with an audit trail is the plausible fit for employers.
## Biggest risk
Liability and trust: one wrongly filed form or missed deadline could destroy the brand. Also, portals change often and break automation (mitigated by human approval at every submit and by handing off to a human when a portal fails).
## Uses founder's existing assets
Scoped vanaras with locked tools (Paperwork, Filer); handover between agents; scheduled background jobs for the deadline radar; approvals and spend rules on the device; reference tokens for personal data; the MCP pipeline for email, calendar and storage; voice for calling offices; BYO model key. New work: document extraction, the case model, cloud runner, desktop form-filling.
