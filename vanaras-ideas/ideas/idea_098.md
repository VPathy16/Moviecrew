# Rota — a management sim where your real AI agent team is the game, and the game is the audit trail
Lens: Contrarian: games/gamified management of your vanara company
## Customer & pain
Payer first: prosumers and small-team leads (indie devs, consultants, ops leads) running 5-15 agents across phone, cloud and desktop. Later: the enterprise IT/risk buyer. Pain today: agent fleets are opaque walls of logs. People either over-trust (approve everything) or under-use (don't delegate). Nobody can see at a glance who is idle, blocked, overspending or waiting on them. Approval fatigue is real, and enterprises cannot get employees to review agent actions carefully.
## Product
Not a toy: a management-sim view (think Factorio or Two Point Hospital, flat 2D) of the REAL vanara company. Each vanara is a worker at a desk with its own tool locker (scoped tools). Phone vanaras run errands, cloud vanaras run project shifts overnight, desktop vanaras work the files. Handovers between agents are visible items moving on conveyor belts. Shared memory is the noticeboard/archive room. Spend rules are a budget meter; approvals are "manager desk" tickets you clear in swipes. Gameplay = real ops: promote an agent (widen its scope), hire one (new tool role), fix a jammed belt (failed handover), keep a "queue zero" streak. Agent-to-agent sync means the map is live, not simulated. Sandbox mode dry-runs a new org design before real tool access is granted. Approvals stay enforced on-device; the game layer only reads status events, and personal data appears as reference tokens.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
An Android "Office" tab plus a web view for UNO users: a live pixel-office showing agent state and a swipe-to-approve stack. Sell to 10 heavy UNO/agent power users found on r/LocalLLaMA, indie-hacker and Claude Code communities. Metric: approval accuracy and time-to-clear versus the plain list. Free tier, $9/mo for cloud and desktop vanaras.
## Enterprise path
Sell "Ops Floor" to IT/risk: an org-wide map where each employee's team is a department, with heatmaps of blocked handovers, policy violations and spend. Gamified compliance training (onboarding new employees by running a sandbox shift under company rules) replaces boring policy PDFs.
## Business model & pricing
Prosumer $9-15/mo. Team $25/seat/mo. Enterprise $40-60/seat/yr-style contracts with audit and sandbox training modules.
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
They build model-native agent UIs (chat, logs, dashboards) and each is tied to its own ecosystem. A cross-vendor, BYO-key, playful visual layer is off-brand for serious enterprise vendors and is not a platform priority.
## Biggest risk
Gamification could feel gimmicky or, worse, encourage speed over care (rewarding queue-zero leads to rubber-stamp approvals). Also, the visual layer is easy to copy; value depends on the underlying scoped-agent and event pipeline being deep. Mitigation: reward accuracy (caught-bad-action bonuses, seeded canary tasks), not speed.
## Uses founder's existing assets
Scoped vanaras and tool lockers map directly to desks; handover events drive the belts; scheduled jobs are shifts; on-device approvals and spend rules are the manager desk and budget meter; reference tokens keep the display private; the MCP role classification defines locker types; BYO key keeps costs off Rota.
