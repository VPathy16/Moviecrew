# Hearth — a household chief-of-staff: one agent team for the whole family, each member's private, all sharing one home memory
Lens: Families / household management as a paid plan
## Customer & pain
Dual-income parents (30-45, US/UK/EU/India diaspora) paying $20-40/month. The household is a small company with no ops staff: school forms, pediatric appointments, bills, subscriptions renewals, travel, carpools, insurance. One parent (usually the mother) carries the "mental load"; family calendars and shared lists only store information, nobody executes. Existing assistants are single-user and cannot hold a family's rules ("kids' data never leaves the house", "anything over $100 needs both parents").
## Product
- Phone: each family member gets their own vanara team (parent, teen, grandparent with voice-first mode). Agents handle messages, calls, reminders, errands within scoped tools.
- Cloud: a "Household" project runs 24/7: tracks renewals, school-portal deadlines, vaccination schedules, bill due dates, and prepares drafts overnight.
- Desktop: opens the school portal, insurer site, tax folders, fills forms, files receipts.
- Shared memory has two tiers: private per person, and a household layer (calendar, rules, contacts, preferences). Agent-to-agent sync: Dad's agent sees Mom's agent already booked the dentist and drops the duplicate; a teen's agent asks the parent's agent for spend approval; grandparent's agent hands off complex tasks to the cloud agent.
- Household rules engine: approval and spend thresholds, who can approve what, enforced on-device.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
"Paperwork & renewals agent for two-parent households": forward emails/photos of school notices and bills to Hearth; it extracts dates, creates events, drafts replies, and asks for one-tap approval from either parent. Launch to 10 couples from parenting groups and my own network via a private Android beta; free 60-day pilot, then $19/month.
## Enterprise path
Family plan is the consumer wedge; same architecture (per-person team, shared org layer, rules engine) becomes Vanaras for companies. Near-term B2B2C bridge: employers offer Hearth as a family-support benefit (childcare/eldercare benefits budget), giving HR an entry point and then upselling work-side teams under company policy.
## Business model & pricing
$19/month per household (up to 6 members), $39 "family plus" with concierge-grade cloud project hours and desktop tasks. BYO model key discount ($9). Employer benefit: $6-10 per employee per month. Guess: 70% gross margin after model cost with on-device tokenisation and small-model routing.
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
They build per-account assistants; family sharing today is billing and photos, not delegated agent authority between members with per-member privacy. Modelling multi-principal consent (kids, grandparents, ex-partners) is a liability and product-management swamp they avoid. Cross-ecosystem households (iPhone plus Android) are structurally awkward for Apple/Google. Model vendors avoid handling minors' data.
## Biggest risk
Trust and privacy: families hand over sensitive data (children, health, money). One incident kills the brand. Also low willingness to pay if value is felt only occasionally; retention depends on daily-usefulness.
## Uses founder's existing assets
UNO scoped vanaras (per-member agents with locked tools), handover between agents (parent/teen/cloud), scheduled background jobs (renewals, reminders), on-device approvals and spend rules (household rules engine), reference tokens keeping personal data from the model (kids' info), MCP tool-role classification (school, bank, health portals), voice (grandparents), BYO key.
