# Hush Desk — a voice-first agent team for field professionals whose hands and eyes are busy
Lens: Voice-first everything
## Customer & pain
Small specialty contractors and clinics-on-wheels (HVAC, electrical, mobile vets, home inspectors, physiotherapists visiting homes), 3-30 staff. The owner or tech spends 1-2 unpaid hours a day after work typing job notes, quotes, invoices, follow-ups and parts orders. On a job they have gloves, ladders or a patient; screens are unusable, but speech is free.
## Product
Phone: a push-to-talk / earbud vanara ("Scribe") hears the tech narrate the job ("compressor's shot, unit is a 2019 Carrier, quote 1,400 plus two days") and immediately files notes, photos and a draft quote. Other vanaras: "Dispatcher" calls and texts customers to confirm slots and chase approvals; "Bookkeeper" turns finished jobs into invoices in the accounting tool.
Cloud: overnight agents reconcile the day's jobs, order parts, chase unpaid invoices, and prepare tomorrow's route and briefing, read aloud at 7am.
Desktop: the office admin's machine gets the same team, entering data into legacy field-service or insurance portals through the browser when there is no API.
Shared memory + agent sync: what the tech said on the roof becomes the customer's quote, the parts order and the invoice with no re-entry. Device-enforced approvals mean the voice command "send it" is the only gate before money or customer messages leave, with spend caps.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
"Talk the job, get the quote": voice-to-quote-and-invoice on Android for solo HVAC/electrical/plumbing owners, $49/month. First 10 from trade Facebook groups, local supplier counters and a few personal-network contractors; founder rides along on 3 jobs to tune vocabulary. (Guess: quote and invoice pain is the strongest pull.)
## Enterprise path
Grow to multi-tech firms (dispatcher plus 10 techs, each with a team, shared job memory), then franchise networks and utilities' contractor programs. Company rules: pricing floors, discount approval limits, customer-data policy, audit trail of every voice-approved action, and on-device data handling for privacy-sensitive clinics.
## Business model & pricing
Per-seat: $39 field tech, $69 office seat with desktop agent; BYO model key option for cheaper tier; small usage fee on outbound calls. Target 200 firms x ~6 seats = ~$100K MRR-scale in years 2-3 (guess).
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
Horizontal assistants will get good voice, but trade-specific workflows, messy legacy portals, per-job vocabulary and thin-margin SMB support are unattractive to them. Vertical field-service incumbents (ServiceTitan, Jobber) are software vendors without agent handover across phone, cloud and desktop. Owning on-device approvals and spend rules is a trust feature generalists don't prioritise.
## Biggest risk
Noisy real-world audio (engines, wind, accents) making transcription unreliable, and Jobber/ServiceTitan adding a competent voice mode. Also trade-buyer sales cycles and low willingness to pay for "AI".
## Uses founder's existing assets
Scoped vanaras with locked tools (Scribe, Dispatcher, Bookkeeper); handover between agents; scheduled background jobs (overnight reconciliation, morning briefing); on-device approvals and spend rules; reference tokens keeping customer PII away from the model; MCP pipeline classifying accounting/calendar/messaging tools by role; existing voice stack; BYO model key.
