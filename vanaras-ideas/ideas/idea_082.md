# Tenlane — a telco-billed agent team for every small-business owner on the operator's network
Lens: Partnership with a telco
## Customer & pain
The telco pays (per-seat wholesale) and the owner of a 1-10 person business (clinic, garage, tutor, salon, tradesperson, small importer) uses it. They run the whole business from one phone: missed calls, unanswered messages, forgotten follow-ups, unpaid invoices. They will not adopt "an AI platform", but they already trust their operator and pay its bill. The telco's pain: SMB ARPU is flat, churn to MVNOs is high, and it has no value-added service that sits on top of the line itself.
## Product
Phone: a vanara team living on the SIM number. One answers and triages missed calls and messages, one chases quotes/invoices/reminders, one keeps the day's schedule. Each is locked to its own tools; spend and send-approvals are enforced on the device, so the owner taps yes/no. Cloud: vanaras keep following up overnight (payment nudges, appointment confirmations) while the phone is off. Desktop (later): a vanara fills the accountant's spreadsheet or portal from what happened on the phone. Shared memory means the customer who called at 9 is known to the invoicing vanara at 5; agent-to-agent handover replaces the owner acting as glue. Personal data stays on-device via reference tokens, which is the telco's privacy-compliance selling point.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
"Never miss a customer": missed-call-to-booked-job. Start with one mid-size or MVNO operator in a non-India market (guess: Africa/SE Asia/LatAm or a European MVNO, since small operators move faster than tier-1s). Pilot with 10 SMB owners handpicked by an operator's business-sales rep, billed as a Rs/$3-5 line add-on. Ship the Android app pre-configured through the operator's SMB store/portal. Founder does the pilot on evenings via the operator's innovation team (guess: 3-6 months to get a meeting; the $30K funds it).
## Enterprise path
Operators already sell to enterprises. Step up to "every employee's line comes with a team": field-sales and frontline staff on corporate plans, company rules (approvals, spend caps, allowed tools) pushed by the operator's device-management portal. The telco becomes the enterprise channel and billing entity; Vanaras is the agent layer.
## Business model & pricing
Wholesale $1-2/seat/month to the operator, who bundles it at $4-8 or zero-rates it in a higher plan. BYO model key for power users; otherwise inference at cost plus margin. Optional revenue share on carrier-billed transactions. Guess: 20-30% gross margin on inference at start, improving with on-device routing.
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
They sell to consumers or large enterprises directly and won't run SMB sales through hundreds of local operators, carrier billing, local languages and local store staff. Operators distrust handing their subscriber relationship to big tech, and a neutral, model-agnostic vendor is an easier yes.
## Biggest risk
Telco sales cycles: slow procurement, pilot purgatory, and a single-operator dependency. Second: operators may want exclusivity or white-label that strips the brand.
## Uses founder's existing assets
Android app with scoped vanaras, handover, scheduled background jobs, on-device approvals and spend rules, reference-token privacy (the compliance pitch), voice for calls, MCP tool-role pipeline (connects to local accounting/booking services), BYO key.
