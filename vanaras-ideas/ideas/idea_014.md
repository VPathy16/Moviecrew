# Chief of Staff Kit (working name: Aidewise) — a vanara team that multiplies the human executive assistant instead of replacing them
Lens: Executive assistants being augmented

## Customer & pain
Buyer: the executive (founder, partner, VP) who already pays a human EA $70-150K, and the EA herself, who is the real user. Today the EA drowns in inbox triage, calendar Tetris across time zones, expense reports, travel rebooking, meeting prep and chasing people. Existing AI schedulers (Motion, Reclaim, Clara-style) treat the EA as a competitor or ignore her. EAs distrust tools that read the exec's whole inbox and act unseen, and execs distrust handing private data to an AI vendor.

## Product
An EA gets a team of vanaras; the exec gets a thin phone companion.
- Phone (exec): a voice-first vanara that takes quick asks ("move Thursday's board prep, tell Priya"), holds approvals, and captures dictated context on the go.
- Cloud (EA's projects): vanaras run standing projects 24/7: board-meeting logistics, travel itineraries, hiring loops, offsite planning. Scheduled jobs chase replies and re-plan when something slips.
- Desktop (EA's work): a vanara drives the browser and apps (expense portals, airline sites, legacy calendar tools) and prepares drafts for the EA to approve.
- Shared memory holds the exec's preferences, relationships, "never before 9am", who matters, and what was promised. Agent-to-agent sync means the phone vanara's overheard ask becomes a cloud task and a desktop action without re-explaining. Each vanara is scoped to its own tools; spend and send rules are enforced on-device. Personal data is passed as reference tokens, so the model never sees raw contact or card details. The EA sees a single "what my team did / needs me" ledger, keeping her the accountable decision-maker.

## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
Start with one painful loop: calendar and meeting-prep vanara pair (phone ask + desktop execution + pre-read brief) sold to EAs directly at $49/month, BYO model key. Find the first 10 via EA communities (Chief of Staff Network, EA LinkedIn groups, EA Slack communities) and VC-backed startup founders whose EA supports 2-3 execs. Success metric: hours per week the EA gets back.

## Enterprise path
EAs cluster inside companies: 20-200 EAs at a mid-size firm. Sell an "EA pod" plan with admin-defined approval and spend policies, audit logs, and shared org memory (VIP lists, travel policy). Then extend to every employee getting their own team, with the EA function as the internal champion and template author.

## Business model & pricing
Per-seat: EA $49-99/month; exec companion seat $19; enterprise pod $150/EA with SSO, audit, policy engine. BYO key lowers COGS; managed-model option at a markup. Human EAs paying for tools out of pocket or expensing them make this a low-friction bottom-up sale.

## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
Their assistants serve the account owner, not a delegate acting across another person's accounts with scoped rights. The EA-plus-exec delegation model, per-agent tool lockdown, and cross-vendor tools (Google plus Outlook plus legacy portals) do not fit a single-ecosystem strategy. The niche is too small and too workflow-specific for them to prioritise.

## Biggest risk
Trust and delegation liability: one wrong email sent as the exec ends the account. Also, EAs may fear replacement and refuse adoption, and exec-account access (OAuth, delegated access) may be restricted by Google/Microsoft policies. Mitigation: draft-first defaults, approval rules, EA-controlled framing.

## Uses founder's existing assets
Scoped vanaras with locked tools, handover between agents, scheduled background jobs, on-device approvals and spend rules, reference-token privacy, the MCP pipeline for calendar/mail/travel/expense tools, voice for the exec's phone companion, and BYO model key. New: cloud runtime and desktop agent (guess: 4-6 months solo for a usable wedge).
