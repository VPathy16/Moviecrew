# Franchisee — a team of AI agents for every franchise owner, governed by the franchisor's rulebook
Lens: Franchise networks
## Customer & pain
The franchisor (HQ) pays; the franchisee (owner-operator of 1-10 units: gyms, auto service, home services, salons, clinics) uses it. Franchisees are solo-ish operators drowning in phone calls, bookings, local marketing, payroll admin, and monthly brand-compliance reporting. HQ pain: 200-2,000 units each running their own ad hoc tools; brand-standard drift; slow rollout of campaigns and SOPs; no visibility without invasive data collection that franchisees resist (and that privacy law complicates).
## Product
Each franchisee gets their own vanaras team. Phone: owner's daily ops (missed-call follow-up, shift swaps, supplier reminders, approvals). Cloud: 24/7 jobs such as local ad budget pacing, review replies, inventory reorders. Desktop: fills the HQ portal, POS exports, compliance forms. HQ gets a "franchisor vanara" that pushes playbooks (a new promo, SOP change, recall notice) as agent-to-agent handovers; each franchisee's agent adapts it to the local unit and asks the owner to approve. Brand rules (approved offers, discount caps, spend limits, tone) are enforced on-device as policy, not as suggestions. Shared memory holds each unit's context; HQ sees only tokenised, aggregated KPIs and compliance status, never customer PII.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
Missed-call and lead-recovery vanara plus monthly compliance auto-report, for one vertical (e.g. independent-brand auto service or fitness studios). Sell to 3-5 small franchisors (30-150 units) via franchise-association contacts, pilot in 10 units, HQ pays a pilot fee. Guess: this vertical has heavy phone-driven bookings.
## Enterprise path
Franchisor is already the enterprise: one contract, rules pushed to all units, SSO for HQ staff, audit log, per-region policy packs. Then move up to multi-brand franchise groups and private-equity roll-ups of franchise brands, who want standardised ops across acquisitions.
## Business model & pricing
Per-unit SaaS billed to franchisor or passed through: about $99-199/unit/month, plus HQ platform fee ($1-3K/month). Franchisee BYO model key optional to cut cost. 300 units at $150 is roughly $540K ARR (guess).
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
It is a vertical distribution and governance problem: franchise-by-franchise onboarding, franchise-law-aware data boundaries, and per-brand rulebooks are unglamorous, high-touch work. Horizontal assistants sell to individuals or IT departments, not to a franchisor whose customers are legally independent businesses. Copilot-style tools assume one employer owns all data; franchises do not.
## Biggest risk
Franchisor sales cycles are slow and franchisees may resist HQ-mandated tools. Also, integration with each brand's POS/booking software (guess: fragmented) could swamp a solo founder.
## Uses founder's existing assets
Scoped vanaras with locked tools (brand rulebook per agent), agent handover (HQ to franchisee to unit staff), on-device approvals and spend rules (discount caps, ad budgets), reference tokens (customer data stays local while HQ sees aggregates), scheduled background jobs, service-agnostic MCP pipeline (adapts to each brand's varied tools), voice and calls, BYO key.
