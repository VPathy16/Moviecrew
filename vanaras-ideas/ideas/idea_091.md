# Kinfolk — a team of agents that keeps your relationships alive across phone, cloud and desktop
Lens: Wedge: personal CRM (relationships maintained by agents)
## Customer & pain
Independent professionals whose income is relationships: freelance consultants, recruiters, boutique advisors, angel investors, realtors, coaches. They pay themselves. Today they use a spreadsheet, a stale HubSpot or Clay-style CRM they never update, and memory. Warm contacts go cold, follow-ups slip, and context (kid's name, last promise made) is scattered across WhatsApp, email, calls and LinkedIn. Existing CRMs are databases that need manual feeding; nothing actually does the maintaining.
## Product
Three vanaras share one relationship memory. Phone vanara (UNO base): sees calls, messages and calendar on-device, drafts check-ins in the user's voice, sets reminders, and asks approval before anything is sent. Cloud vanara: runs 24/7 and watches for triggers (job change, funding news, birthdays, a stalled thread), prepares a brief and queues a draft while the phone is off. Desktop vanara: does the laborious part in the browser and apps, such as updating LinkedIn notes, pulling email history, and filing meeting notes into the record. Agent-to-agent sync means a promise made on a call ("I'll intro you to Priya") is captured by phone, researched by cloud, and the intro email drafted on desktop, all from one memory. Reference tokens keep contact details from reaching the model.
## Wedge (first product, first 10 customers)
Android app: "never let a warm relationship go cold". Import contacts and call log, agents rank who to reach this week and draft the message. Price low, BYO key. First 10: recruiters and angel investors from founder's network and a few communities (indie hackers, operator Slack groups), offered white-glove onboarding.
## Enterprise path
Every salesperson, account manager and partner lead is a relationship worker. Move from personal to team: each employee keeps a private team of vanaras, with a company layer of rules (which contacts are company-owned, spend and send approvals, audit log). Shared "relationship graph" answers who in the company knows a prospect, without exposing private notes. On-device approvals and tokenised data are the compliance story versus CRMs that scrape inboxes.
## Business model & pricing
Personal: $19/month Pro (BYO key) or $39 with managed model. Team: $30-60 per seat per month plus admin/audit tier. Later, usage-based cloud agent hours.
## Why big tech (Google/Apple/Microsoft/OpenAI/Anthropic) won't just do it
Relationship maintenance is niche and cross-app and cross-platform, and it needs sending messages on the user's behalf on WhatsApp/SMS, which platform owners are cautious about. Microsoft and Google have Dynamics/contacts but optimise for company pipeline, not the individual's private network. Model labs build horizontal assistants, not opinionated vertical workflows with per-relationship memory. Guess: Apple could ship a "stay in touch" feature, but only shallowly.
## Biggest risk
Trust and permissions: reading messages and sending on the user's behalf. Also Android restrictions on call log/notification access, WhatsApp policy on automation, and users not paying for a "nice to have".
## Uses founder's existing assets
Scoped vanaras with own tools, handover between agents, scheduled background jobs, on-device approvals and spend rules, reference-token privacy, MCP pipeline for email/calendar/LinkedIn-type tools, voice for call-time capture, BYO key.
