# User feedback on proactive / agentic AI assistants (2025-2026): what users value and what drives them away

Evidence labels used throughout: **[M]** = measured or count-based data (still note the source quality), **[A]** = anecdotal (single user, reviewer, forum post, Trustpilot), **[V]** = vendor or company claim, **[P]** = press report of unpublished or internal data. Dates are the date of the event or publication. Research date: 2026-10-26. Several products in scope launched only weeks ago (Claude Duet 2026-10-21, OpenAI Dots ~2026-09-30/10-02, Meta Muse ~2026-09-08), so hands-on evidence for them is thin and mostly launch-week.

## 1. What do users praise most (features, moments of delight, time saved)?

### Takeaway
Users praise (a) a low-friction channel they already live in (texting/iMessage/Telegram, voice), (b) a short, finite, personalized digest that "already knows me", (c) agents that complete the boring last mile of a real task and stop at a clear confirm screen, and (d) natural, interruptible voice with camera/screen context. The praise is consistently for narrow, concrete wins; broad "do anything" agents get admiration at demo time but weak sustained use.

### Cited Findings
**Personalized, finite daily digest (ChatGPT Pulse, 2025-09 to 2026-06)**

- [A] On its retirement (2026-07-02) the same user wrote "It felt like magic," but admitted his own use had already decayed from reading it daily to skimming it every couple of days. — [Manton Reece, 2026-07-02](https://www.manton.org/2026/07/02/a-little-bummed-that-openai.html)
- [V] Pulse was deliberately designed as finite rather than an endless feed: cards disappear unless saved; steering via thumbs up/down and "Curate" requests (e.g., "tomorrow focus on tennis updates"). — [Axios, 2025-09-25](https://www.axios.com/2025/09/25/chatgpt-prompts-you-pulse-openai); [W&B summary](https://wandb.ai/byyoung3/ml-news/reports/Chatgpt-Pulse-enters-preview-A-daily-AI-assistant-that-works-proactively-for-you--VmlldzoxNDUzMTE4OQ)

**OpenClaw: what draws people in**
- [P] OpenClaw grew from a weekend project to ~100,000 GitHub stars; its default interface is Telegram rather than a web app; Telegram is the most mature, easiest channel and runs fine on a home machine behind a router. — [WPP Research](https://cms.research.wpp.com/?p=1355)
- [V/P] The "heartbeat" is what turns OpenClaw from a reactive chatbot into a proactive agent that surfaces issues and reminders on its own. — [Zilliz FAQ](https://zilliz.com/ai-faq/what-is-the-heartbeat-feature-in-openclawmoltbotclawdbot); [clawdocs heartbeat](https://clawdocs.org/architecture/heartbeat)

### Inferences
- For Boop, the two strongest "delight" patterns are (1) living in a channel users already use (a chat thread or voice call, not a dashboard) and (2) a short, steerable, finite morning brief. Both Poke and Pulse earned praise for these; Pulse's own users still drifted, so the brief must stay action-oriented (see Q3).
- "Last-mile" completion with a clean stop-at-confirm screen (TechRadar on Spark) is praised more than raw autonomy. For Swiggy/Zomato ordering, the delight moment is likely "cart built, offer applied, address chosen — tap to confirm," not fully autonomous ordering.
- Companion warmth is valued intensely by a minority (GPT-4o), which raises the stakes of persona changes and removals; a friendly persona should be stable and versioned, not silently swapped.

### Gaps
- No independent, quantified time-saved data for any proactive consumer agent was found; time-saved claims are vendor or anecdotal.
- No sizable body of OpenClaw user praise (e.g., HN/Reddit "this changed my life" threads) surfaced in searches; praise evidence for OpenClaw is mostly indirect (GitHub stars, Telegram convenience).
- Hands-on reviews of OpenAI Dots, Meta Muse and xAI Grok Bot beyond launch week were not found.

## 2. Top complaints: reliability, urgency, notification fatigue, cost/limits, latency, privacy, security, hallucinated actions, onboarding, regional availability

### Takeaway
The complaints that actually drove people away were: unreliable execution of basics (timers, reminders, scheduled tasks running at the wrong time), actions taken on overly broad permissions ("Allow Always"), opaque and runaway credit/token costs (especially background "heartbeat" checks), proactive features that rarely fire or surface trivial things, hallucinated facts in unsolicited summaries, and security exposure for self-hosted agents. Privacy concern is near-universal in surveys but does not stop use; concrete incidents do.

### Inferences
- The most damaging single failure mode for Boop would be a Muse-style "Allow Always" grant that silently covers a higher-stakes action (accepting a price, sharing an address, placing a trade). Scope approvals per action type and per amount, not per app.
- Background checks are where cost blows up (OpenClaw heartbeat, Duet's "every few hours" design choice). Boop should run cheap, local or rule-based triggers for "is anything new?", and only invoke a large model when there is a delta — and show the user what background work costs.
- "Proactive" fails in both directions: too quiet (Duet per Vo, Magic Cue) or too noisy/wrong (Apple summaries, Poke burying urgent mail). Ship with a small number of high-precision triggers and a visible "why am I seeing this" explanation.
- On Android specifically, users judge any assistant against Google Assistant's reliability at basics (timers, alarms, calls, reminders). Boop should delegate those to deterministic Android intents rather than LLM reasoning.
- For India: Swiggy MCP is gated and partially functional (COD-only in Jan 2026, Instamart checkout not exposed); Kite MCP is read-only plus GTT and requires daily re-login. Boop's food and investing flows must degrade gracefully to "hand off to the app with a prefilled cart / prefilled order" and never assume the API path will work.
- Self-hosting risk: an open-source, self-hosted Boop component (any server or gateway) must bind to localhost by default, never expose a control UI publicly, and store credentials outside the model context — OpenClaw's exposure numbers show many users never change defaults.

## 5. Lessons on trust and approvals: what approval UX users liked or found annoying

### Takeaway
Users and reviewers favor "show exactly what I'm about to do, then wait" for irreversible or money/identity actions, combined with read-only autonomy for research and drafting. Blanket grants ("Allow Always") cause the worst incidents; too many prompts cause rubber-stamping. The emerging consensus design is risk-tiered approvals, hard blocks on the riskiest actions, credentials never visible to the model, and clear labeling of agent-sent messages.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Claude Duet: reads and drafts on its own; before sending a message, making a payment, signing a document or deleting anything, it "shows you exactly what it's about to do and waits for your approval." Trading securities and moving money between accounts are off-limits even with approval. Never sees passwords or sign-in tokens; a classifier blocks sharing card numbers and government IDs; health and personal finance shared only on explicit instruction. Users set what they want to hear about and how often. — [Claude blog, 2026-10-21](https://claude.com/blog/claude-duet)
- [V] OpenAI Dots: per-app access; custom rules to allow, require approval for, or block actions; "sensitive actions, such as changing a password, remain with the user"; proactive research only with read-only tools when the user isn't engaging; saved passwords used without exposure to the model; separate cloud computer per dot. — [IT Brief, 2026-10-02](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/openai-launches-dots-ai-assistants-for-chatgpt-users)
- [V] OpenAI cited an outside test in which a dot prepared an overdue invoice and sent it only after user approval. — [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2613531)
- [V] Meta Muse: credentials in secure storage usable without Muse seeing them; per-app permissions; users can tell it to "forget" specific things; planned "Muse Confidential VM" encrypted with a user-held key. — [The Week, 2026-09-09](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/09/09/meta-says-its-muse-personal-ai-assistant-which-can-spend-your-money-is-safe-and-secure-is-it.html); [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/meta-muse-agent-review)
- [A] The Muse "Allow Always" incident shows a coarse grant silently extending to accepting offers and sharing a home address; the user's fix suggestions were per-action re-confirmation and a "Sent By Muse" label. — [Dexerto, 2026-09-30](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/meta-responds-after-muse-ai-gave-stranger-youtubers-home-address-on-facebook-marketplace-3414057/)
- [A] Liked: Gemini Spark pausing before booking and stopping at the submit page of a form for the user to confirm. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/i-tried-gemini-spark-in-chrome-and-its-the-perfect-ai-for-handling-all-the-boring-bits-online)

- [P, secondhand] Approval fatigue: a security vendor cites Anthropic (2026-03) that users approve 93% of Claude Code permission prompts, and (2026-02) that auto-approve use grows from ~20% of sessions for new users to over 40% by 750 sessions. — [NeuralTrust](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/human-in-the-loop-ai-agents) (original Anthropic sources not verified)

Simon Willison criticized reliance on constant supervision as the main defense. — [Simon Willison, 2025-10-21](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/21/introducing-chatgpt-atlas/); [Codeminer42](https://blog.codeminer42.com/how-to-use-chatgpt-atlas-agent-mode/)
- [V] Zerodha keeps Kite MCP read-only (except GTT orders) with login via Kite's 2FA, not via the AI — the Indian broker chose to withhold order placement from agents entirely. Claude Duet likewise blocks trading even with approval. — [Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/others-kite/articles/connect-zerodha-ai-assistant); [Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/claude-duet)
- [M, hands-on] Swiggy's initial MCP restricted AI-initiated food orders to cash on delivery (online payment blocked/failed), described by MediaNama as deliberate caution. — [MediaNama, 2026-01-28](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-ordering-chatgpt-swiggy-services-working/)
- [M, survey] Only 31% of respondents trust AI to make independent decisions, vs 66% comfortable using it. — [Let's Data Science (Ping Identity survey)](https://letsdatascience.com/news/survey-identifies-ai-trust-tipping-point-0fd45e17)

### Inferences
- Recommended approval tiers for Boop, derived from the above: (1) autonomous: reading, research, drafting, building a cart, preparing an order ticket; (2) one-tap confirm with an exact preview (item list, total, address, payment method; or symbol, qty, price, order type): sending messages, placing a Swiggy/Zomato order, booking; (3) confirm plus re-auth (biometric/UPI PIN in the native app): payments and any trade; (4) blocked by default: moving money between accounts, sharing address/phone with third parties, changing passwords.
- Never offer a single "Allow Always" that spans action types. If standing permissions exist, scope them narrowly (e.g., "reorder my usual breakfast under ₹300 from these two restaurants") and show them in a list that can be revoked.
- Keep approval counts low by batching (one confirm for a whole cart) and by making the default action reversible where possible; this targets approval fatigue without removing the gate.
- Label every agent-sent outbound message (the "Sent by Boop" idea from the Muse incident) and keep a human-readable activity log.
- For investing, mirror Zerodha's and Anthropic's conservatism: portfolio insight and alerts are low-risk value; order placement should hand off to Kite with a prefilled order and native 2FA rather than execute in the agent.
