# Session protocols for multi-agent ideation: order of steps, who sees what, alone versus building on each other

Notes compiled 19 November 2026. Questions 1 and 2 contain only human-group evidence. Questions 3, 4 and 5 contain only LLM-agent evidence. Question 6 compares the two and labels every recommendation with the evidence it rests on. Years are given for every study; human studies before about 2015 and LLM studies on 2023 to early-2024 models are marked "older". Where a number came from a secondary summary rather than the paper itself, it is marked "secondary".

## 1. Human research: group brainstorming versus nominal groups, blocking, apprehension, loafing, brainwriting, electronic brainstorming, exposure to others' ideas

### Takeaway
People who generate alone and pool afterwards (nominal groups) reliably out-produce the same number of people talking in a room, mainly because only one person can speak at a time (production blocking). Formats that remove turn-taking but keep some exposure to others' ideas (brainwriting, electronic brainstorming in larger groups, alternating solo and group phases) are the only ones with evidence of matching or beating nominal groups, and exposure helps or harms depending on what is shown.

### Cited Findings

#### Nominal versus interactive groups
- Older (1958 onward, tallied 1987): Taylor, Berry and Block (1958) found nominal groups produced "nearly twice as many different ideas as the real groups". Of 22 experiments tabulated by Diehl and Stroebe, 18 found nominal groups superior and 4 found no difference; all 4 exceptions used two-person groups. — [Diehl & Stroebe 1987, JPSP 53:497-509](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)
- Older (1987): the same review reports that the productivity gap between real and nominal groups widened as group size increased, which the authors read as consistent with blocking. — [Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)
- Older (1991): the Mullen, Johnson and Salas meta-analysis concluded that brainstorming groups are significantly less productive than nominal groups on both quantity and quality. Losses were larger in larger groups, when an experimenter was present, when ideas were spoken and tape-recorded instead of written, and when the comparison was people truly alone instead of people working separately in the same room. — [record of Mullen, Johnson & Salas 1991](https://www.scilit.net/publications/e7218c55746018a0c3a5fc3d0a54871a); [summary note](https://scrapbox.io/nishio-en/Productivity_Loss_in_Brainstorming_Groups:_A_Meta-Analytic_Integration)
- Secondary and unverified: one summary site gives the Mullen meta-analysis as 20 studies with d = 0.60 to 0.82 favouring nominal groups. I could not confirm these figures against the paper. — [Studies Suggest summary](https://studiessuggest.pages.dev/stories/brainstorming-groups-fewer-ideas-than-individuals.html)
- Older (1991): Stroebe and Diehl criticised the meta-analysis in the same journal issue, arguing its moderators were arbitrarily chosen and that real groups always work under blocking, which nominal groups do not. — [comment in Basic and Applied Social Psychology 12(1)](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15324834basp1201_2)

#### Production blocking, evaluation apprehension, free riding
- Older (1987): four experiments. Experiment 1: productivity was higher under personal than collective assessment, but real-versus-nominal still had "a major impact" when the temptation to free ride was removed. Experiment 2: inducing evaluation apprehension reduced productivity in individual brainstorming. Experiment 3: no interaction between apprehension and session type, which "raises doubts about evaluation apprehension as a major explanation". Experiment 4: introducing turn-taking into individual sessions showed that blocking "accounted for most of the productivity loss"; people free to voice ideas as they occurred produced "approximately twice as many ideas" as people who had to wait their turn. — [Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)
- Older (1987): in Experiment 4 the blocking effect was unaffected by whether participants could overhear the others, so the loss came from waiting, not from hearing content. — [Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)

#### Social loafing
- Older (1993): Karau and Williams meta-analysed 78 studies and described social loafing as moderate in size and general across tasks and populations. Loafing shrank when individual contributions were identifiable or evaluation potential was held constant, and on tasks people valued. — [Karau & Williams 1993 PDF copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/social-loafing-a-meta-analytic-review-and-theoretical-22g4ic0jro.pdf); [Simply Psychology overview](https://www.simplypsychology.org/social-loafing.html)
- Secondary and unverified: a blog gives the pooled loafing effect as about 0.44 standard deviations. — [secondary blog](https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/social-loafing-ringelmann-latane-group-productivity/)

#### Brainwriting, including 6-3-5
- Older (1968 or 1969): 6-3-5 brainwriting (Rohrbach) has six people each write three ideas in five minutes, then pass the sheet on so each row is built on five times. The often-quoted 108 ideas in 30 minutes is the arithmetic maximum (6 x 3 x 6), not a measured result. — [Wikipedia: 6-3-5 Brainwriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-3-5_Brainwriting)
- Older (2000): Paulus and Yang found that groups who wrote ideas and passed them round out-performed non-sharing (nominal) groups, both during the session and in a later solo session. This is one of the few results where an interacting format beats nominal groups. — [Paulus & Yang 2000 record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/901478178)
- Older (2009): Heslin's review treated brainwriting as promising but under-tested and asked for more research on its boundary conditions; later commentary notes only a small number of studies have examined it. — [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/forget-brainstorming-try-brainwriting)
- 2015: in a technology company, Paulus, Korde and colleagues found that alternating solo writing with a periodic review of the group's ideas beat continuous group brainwriting, reported as 0.50 versus 0.29 ideas per minute (about 71% more). The study was exploratory with a small sample. — [Association for Psychological Science summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/theres-a-better-way-to-brainstorm.html); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/forget-brainstorming-try-brainwriting); [publication record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/asynchronous-brainstorming-in-an-industrial-setting-exploratory-s)
- 2017: Korde and Paulus ran three experiments on alternating individual and group sessions. A combined analysis found the alternating format produced more ideas than group-only and individual-only formats, and idea output rose in the solo sessions that followed each group exchange. A later review notes the advantage was mainly in quantity, with no clear originality difference. — [Stanford d.school summary](https://medium.com/stanford-d-school/alternate-solo-team-work-eb7431893b42); [author publication page](https://scite.ai/authors/runa-m-korde-0a2kr9)

#### Electronic brainstorming
- Older (1992): Gallupe and colleagues compared electronic and spoken brainstorming at group sizes 2, 4 and 6 (one lab) and 6 and 12 (another). Larger groups produced more unique and higher-quality ideas electronically; differences were small for small groups. The authors attribute the gain to reduced blocking and apprehension. — [Gallupe et al. 1992 record](https://scilit.com/publications/9d4ac6467aaba6ce8aff99d4f31aeab9); [summary note](https://scrapbox.io/nishio-en/Electronic%20Brainstorming%20And%20Group%20Size)
- Older (meta-analysis, attribution uncertain between Dennis and Williams and the Gallupe group): electronic groups out-perform spoken groups from about four members and out-perform nominal groups from about ten members. — [IGI chapter on group size effects](https://www.igi-global.com/chapter/group-size-effects-electronic-brainstorming/12446)
- Older (1999): Pinsonneault and colleagues compared nominal, anonymous electronic, identified electronic and spoken brainstorming; nominal groups were at least as productive as electronic ones, and the authors argue the popularity of group formats rests on perceived, not actual, productivity. — [Pinsonneault et al. 1999 record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orisre/v10y1999i2p110-133.html)
- Older (2007): the DeRosa, Smith and Hantula meta-analysis of electronic brainstorming is cited by later authors as finding no overall difference between electronic and nominal groups. — [Sandia report citing it](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1146277)

#### Exposure to others' ideas: stimulation and fixation
- Older (2000): Dugosh, Paulus, Roland and Yang found that hearing others' ideas increased idea generation during and after exposure, and that the effect depended on whether people attended to the ideas and on what was shown (number of ideas, irrelevant material). — [Dugosh et al. 2000 record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/909565481)
- Older (2002): Nijstad, Stroebe and Lodewijkx found that seeing ideas from many semantic categories made people produce more diverse ideas, while seeing many ideas from a few categories made them produce more ideas within those categories. — [Nijstad et al. 2002 record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/914580572)
- Older (2005): Dugosh and Paulus found that exposure to a high number of ideas and to common ideas increased further generation. — [Dugosh & Paulus 2005 record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_38067175)
- Older (2011): Kohn and Smith paired participants with a confederate who fed in one to twenty typical ideas. Exposure produced conformity to the categories shown and narrowed breadth, without changing the number of ideas. A break reduced the usual decline in quantity and variety over time. — [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/stuck-your-ideas-fixation-group-brainstorms); [abstract](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/acp.1699)
- Older (2010): Girotra, Terwiesch and Ulrich found that ideas built on earlier ideas were no better than others, and teams that built on each other did not produce more ideas. — [Girotra et al. 2010](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/2010/idea-generation-and-the-quality-of-the-best-idea/)

### Inferences
- The human productivity loss is mostly a scheduling problem (waiting to speak), not a content problem. Formats that let everyone produce at once while still passing ideas around remove the main cost.
- Exposure has two separable effects in people: it raises output, and it steers content toward whatever was shown. Showing a spread of categories widens the pool; showing many examples of a few categories deepens those and narrows the rest.
- The best-supported human format is alternation: solo generation, a short look at others' ideas, then solo generation again.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the Mullen 1991 effect sizes from the paper; the only numbers found are from an unverified secondary site.
- I could not retrieve the abstract of DeRosa, Smith and Hantula (2007). My recollection, unverified here, is that it reports group size as a moderator, with large electronic groups beating nominal groups and small ones not. Treat as unconfirmed.
- No controlled study of 6-3-5 specifically against nominal groups was found; evidence is for brainwriting generally.
- The pooled effect size for social loafing was not confirmed against the 1993 paper.

## 2. Human research: separating divergent and convergent phases, quantity goals, time pressure, incubation, facilitator rules

### Takeaway
Generating more ideas does tend to yield more good ones, but people are poor at picking the best ideas afterwards, so selection needs its own designed step. Trained facilitation can close the gap with nominal groups, short breaks help a little, and the classic "no criticism" rule is not supported when tested against instructions to debate.

### Cited Findings

#### Divergent then convergent
- Older (2006): Rietzschel, Nijstad and Stroebe compared nominal and interactive groups with generation and selection either strictly separated or combined. Nominal groups generated more, but the quality of the ideas selected did not differ between conditions, and selection was not significantly better than chance. — [Rietzschel et al. 2006](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/productivity-is-not-enough-a-comparison-of-interactive-and-nomina/)
- Older (2010): Girotra, Terwiesch and Ulrich compared teams working together throughout with a hybrid in which individuals first worked alone and then met. Hybrid groups generated more ideas, better ideas, and were better at discerning quality. — [Girotra et al. 2010](https://repository.upenn.edu/oid_papers/191)
- Older (2010): press coverage of the same study reports that both structures did "significantly worse than hoped" at the evaluation stage, and that traditional teams showed more variance in idea quality. — [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/how-group-dynamics-may-be-killing-innovation/); [MIT Sloan Management Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=2997)

#### Quantity goals
- Older (2011): Paulus, Kohn and Arditti reportedly found that telling people to prioritise quantity produced both more ideas and more good ideas than quality instructions (secondary account; abstract not retrieved). — [secondary blog summary](https://generationbc.blogspot.com/2013/09/quantity-breeds-quality-when-it-comes.html)
- Older (2005): Adanez reported a correlation of r = .893 between the number of ideas produced and the number of good ideas. — [Spanish Journal of Psychology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/spanish-journal-of-psychology/article/abs/does-quantity-generate-quality-testing-the-fundamental-principle-of-brainstorming/5D8442A9F86CBB001D901C3F1262489C)
- Older (2008, 2009): Litchfield reframed brainstorming rules as assigned goals ("Brainstorming reconsidered: A goal-based view", 2008; "Brainstorming rules as assigned goals: Does brainstorming really improve idea quantity?", 2009); only the titles were retrieved, not the results. A 2011 paper notes that the standard rules contain only a vague quantity goal. — [Litchfield 2008 record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/886035336/1/3); [Motivation and Emotion 2011](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-011-9203-3)

#### Time pressure
- Mixed and thin (from a search summary; the match of each finding to each link was not individually verified): one Alternate Uses study found tighter time limits reduced average originality; a design-research review concluded time pressure usually raises productivity while creativity suffers with both too little and too much time; another study found no strong effect of time pressure on solution quality or originality. — [thesis on time pressure and originality](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/9056/1/17.05.2023%20PhD%20Thesis_Gina%20Palermo_FINAL.pdf); [design research review](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/28839/); [Creativity Research Journal study](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400410902855267)

#### Incubation and breaks
- Older (2009): Sio and Ormerod meta-analysed 117 studies and found a modest positive incubation effect (mean d = 0.29). The benefit was larger when the break was filled with an undemanding task than with a demanding task or rest, and longer preparation before the break increased the effect. — [Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://www.research.lancs.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-a-metaanalytic-review(ec023bd3-01e9-453e-854a-3dca00e2d146)/export.html)
- Older (2006): Paulus, Nakui, Putman and Brown found a small benefit from brief breaks in brainwriting sessions, reduced or absent in electronic brainstorming. — [Paulus et al. 2006 record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_614488031)
- Older (2011): in Kohn and Smith's fixation study a break moderated the decline in quantity and variety over time. — [abstract](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/acp.1699)

#### Facilitators and rules
- Older (2001): Kramer, Fleming and Mannis reported that face-to-face groups generate as many ideas as nominal groups when assisted by a trained facilitator. — [Kramer et al. 2001 record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/897307970)
- Older (1996): Offner, Kramer and Winter observed that almost all brainstorming research placed people in groups with no attempt to manage or facilitate them. — [article quoting Offner et al.](https://modernanalyst.com/Resources/Articles/tabid/115/ID/2967/Flawed-Studies-Brainstorming-Revisited.aspx)
- Older (2006): additional rules beyond Osborn's four showed clear benefits under several conditions; a facilitator enforcing them improved efficiency (fewer words per idea) but not the number of ideas. — [Paulus et al. 2006 record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_614488031)
- Older (2004): Nemeth, Personnaz, Personnaz and Goncalo compared the traditional "do not criticise" rule with instructions to debate and even criticise, and with no instructions, in the United States and France. Debate instructions were generally superior to the traditional rule. — [working paper](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4k70n7v8)
- Nemeth and co-authors (chapter and book coverage, as summarised in search results; not read in full): dissent increases divergent thinking even when it is wrong, but it must be authentic; role-played devil's advocacy is less effective and may backfire. — [chapter record: Better Than Individuals?](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........857ab7c3a6ee628b0132f079bd35a72f); [review of Nemeth's book](https://beforethemeeting.substack.com/p/conviction-by-proxy)

### Inferences
- In people, the generation step and the selection step fail in different ways. Generation is fixed by working alone and by quantity goals; selection is not fixed by any of these and performs near chance without extra structure.
- Individuals-first then group is supported for both more ideas and somewhat better judgement of them.
- The facilitator's measurable contribution is keeping people on the rules and off tangents, which matters because unmanaged groups were the norm in the studies that found large losses.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analysis of time pressure on brainstorming output; the evidence located is mixed single studies and one review.
- The original abstract of Paulus, Kohn and Arditti (2011) and of Oxley, Dzindolet and Paulus (1996) on facilitators could not be retrieved.
- I found no human study that isolates the effect of strictly separating divergent and convergent phases on the quality of the final chosen idea beyond Rietzschel (2006) and Girotra (2010).

## 3. LLM systems: how published multi-agent ideation systems organise the work, what they measured, what improved novelty or diversity

### Takeaway
Published systems do not rely on many identical agents receiving one brief. They vary what each generation call sees (different papers, methods, roles), remove near-duplicates by embedding similarity, rank by pairwise tournament, and feed winners and critiques back into further rounds. The measured diversity gains come from new external input, planned search, differentiated roles and iteration depth; automatic novelty and quality scores are mostly LLM-judged and their authors flag this.

### Cited Findings

#### Over-generate, de-duplicate, rank (Stanford, 2024)
- Si, Yang and Hashimoto generated 4,000 seed ideas per topic. Each call saw ten randomly chosen retrieved papers and six example ideas, and the titles of previous ideas were appended to discourage repeats. After de-duplication at cosine similarity 0.8, "there are only 200 non-duplicate unique ideas" out of 4,000 (about 5%), and the count of new unique ideas plateaued as generation continued. — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1)
- Ranking used a Swiss-system tournament of pairwise comparisons over five rounds. The ranker reached 71.4% accuracy at predicting which of two ICLR 2024 submissions was accepted. — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1)
- In blind review by over 100 researchers, AI ideas were rated more novel than expert ideas (5.64 versus 4.84 on a 10-point scale, p < 0.01) with no significant feasibility difference. — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1)
- LLM judges agreed poorly with human reviewers: the best evaluator reached 53.3% balanced accuracy against averaged human scores (chance 50%; human-human 56.1%). The authors conclude that LLMs "cannot evaluate ideas reliably yet". — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1)
- 2025 follow-up: 43 experts each spent over 100 hours executing randomly assigned ideas. After execution, scores of LLM-generated ideas fell significantly more than those of human ideas on novelty, excitement, effectiveness and overall (p < 0.05), closing the gap seen at the ideation stage. — [Si et al. 2025, The Ideation-Execution Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803v1)

#### Iterative planning and retrieval aimed at novelty (Nova, 2024; ACL Findings 2025)
- Nova starts with 15 seed ideas per input paper, drawn from related literature, ten named scientific discovery methods and the model's own knowledge. In each of three iterations the model plans what to search for in order to raise the novelty and diversity of the current idea set, generates ten new seeds per idea, and keeps three after self-reflection. Final seeds are clustered by k-means into 100 clusters and the cluster centres are expanded into proposals. — [Hu et al. 2024, Nova](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14255v2)
- Measured: share of non-duplicate ideas (threshold 0.8), novelty judged by an LLM against retrieved papers, and a five-round Swiss tournament. The number of unique novel ideas was "3.4 times higher" than without the framework, and over 80% of Nova's ideas were unique. — [Hu et al. 2024, Nova](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14255v2)
- Ablation: without planning, unique ideas barely grew between steps (42.4 to 44.1). Without planning and retrieval they plateaued near 31, which the authors attribute to "no external knowledge being introduced". — [Hu et al. 2024, Nova](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14255v2)

#### Generate, reflect, rank, evolve (Google AI co-scientist, 2025)
- A Supervisor turns the goal into a plan and assigns specialised agents to a worker queue, run asynchronously. Agents: Generation, Reflection, Ranking, Evolution, Proximity and Meta-review. — [Google Research blog 2025](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/); [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- Generation uses literature search, simulated multi-turn debates among experts (self-play), stepwise identification of testable assumptions, and "research expansion", in which the agent reviews existing hypotheses and the previous meta-review to pick unexplored directions. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- Ranking is an Elo tournament. New hypotheses start at 1200. Top-ranked hypotheses are compared through multi-turn debates and lower-ranked ones through single-turn comparisons. Matches are more likely between similar hypotheses, using the Proximity agent's similarity graph, and newer and top-ranked hypotheses are prioritised. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- Evolution strategies include grounding in literature, feasibility improvement, inspiration from top hypotheses, combination of several top hypotheses, simplification, and "out-of-box thinking" by moving away from a subset of hypotheses. The Evolution agent "generates new hypotheses; it doesn't modify or replace existing ones", so each variant must win its own tournament matches. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- Meta-review summarises recurring patterns from reviews and debates and appends them to other agents' prompts; the Generation agent uses this feedback "selectively to avoid over-fitting" to critiques. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- Measured: Elo over time across 203 research goals rose as compute increased, with "no evidence of performance saturation"; the paper states that Elo is auto-evaluated and "not based on independent ground truth". On 11 expert-rated goals the system received an average preference rank of 2.36 of 4 and novelty and impact ratings of 3.64 and 3.09 out of 5, ahead of the baseline models. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)

#### Team meetings (Virtual Lab, 2024 preprint; Nature 2025)
- A human sets the agenda. A Principal Investigator agent leads scientist agents in team meetings (all agents discuss) and individual meetings (one agent, optionally with a Scientific Critic agent that gives critical feedback). — [Swanson et al. 2024 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.11.623004.full.pdf); [project repository](https://github.com/zou-group/virtual-lab)
- The same meeting is run several times in parallel (five in the reported workflow) and the summaries are merged by a further step, to get more robust answers. — [Stanford Daily 2026](https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/06/researchers-develop-ai-discovery/); [Stanford lecture slides](https://web.stanford.edu/class/biods271/assets/lectures/L5.pdf)
- Outcome measured was downstream: 92 designed nanobodies were experimentally tested. Press coverage reports limitations including agents that miss context, suggest impractical experiments, or fail to challenge each other. — [project repository](https://github.com/zou-group/virtual-lab); [Stanford Daily 2026](https://stanforddaily.com/2026/05/06/researchers-develop-ai-discovery/)

#### Simulated research teams (VirSci, 2024; ACL 2025)
- Agents carry real scientists' profiles. Stages: collaborator selection, topic selection, idea generation by round-table discussion, novelty assessment, abstract generation. For novelty assessment, agents see the three top ideas with related papers and vote "without dialogue memory", to mimic blind review. An abstract too similar to past papers is revised once and then discarded. — [Su et al. 2024, VirSci](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09403v4)
- Novelty is measured by embedding distance to past and later papers and citation counts of similar later papers. Overall novelty peaked at a team size of 8 and at about 5 discussion turns, and was strongest when about 50% of members were new collaborators. Default model was LLaMA 3.1 8B. — [Su et al. 2024, VirSci](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09403v4)

#### Discussion with roles (LLM Discussion, 2024, older models)
- Three phases: initiation, several rounds in which each agent sees all others' previous responses and is told to diverge and build, and a final convergence round. Four agents, five rounds. Roles were generated by GPT-4 (for example Futurist, Environmentalist) and held fixed. — [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06373v3)
- On the Alternative Uses Test, originality scored by an LLM was 4.44 for the full framework against 3.73 for standard multi-agent debate. In the ablation, roles without discussion scored 4.58, and discussion without roles scored 3.95. — [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06373v3)

#### Ideation, critique, revision loops (Ueda et al., SIGDIAL 2025)
- Using GPT-4o-mini, the share of non-duplicate ideas rose from 0.69 (single pass) to 0.77 with one self-critique and revision, to 0.85 and 0.86 with three and four critique-revision rounds, and to 0.80 with three parallel critics. A persona on the proposer gave 0.81 diversity; a persona on the critic gave the best top-10 precision in an LLM-judged tournament (0.55) with slightly lower diversity (0.76). No human evaluation; no significance tests reported. — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1)

#### Other systems
- ResearchAgent (2024; NAACL 2025) refines ideas with reviewing agents whose criteria are aligned to human judgements; gains are reported to saturate after about three refinement iterations (secondary summary). — [Baek et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07738)
- The AI Scientist (2024) checks each idea for novelty by searching Semantic Scholar; a noted weakness is that idea generation "often produces very similar proposals across different runs and models" (secondary summary). — [Lu et al. 2024, The AI Scientist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06292)
- FunSearch and AlphaEvolve keep candidate solutions in separate "islands" that evolve independently, periodically replacing the worst islands with copies of the best, and AlphaEvolve prompts mix high performers with diversity representatives, to avoid premature convergence (secondary descriptions). — [overview of LLM algorithm discovery](https://richardcsuwandi.github.io/blog/2025/llm-algorithm-discovery); [FunSearch method notes](https://llm4ad-doc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/method/funsearch.html)

#### Engineering write-ups on orchestrator and worker systems
- Anthropic (2025): a lead agent with parallel subagents out-performed a single agent by 90.2% on an internal research evaluation, at about 15 times the tokens of a chat. Each subagent needs an objective, an output format, guidance on tools and sources, and clear task boundaries; with vague instructions subagents duplicated each other's work (in one example two subagents covered the same ground while a third went off-topic). Lead agents wait for each batch of subagents, and subagents can write results to files and return references. — [Anthropic: How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Anthropic (2024): describes parallelisation by "sectioning" (different sub-tasks) versus "voting" (same task several times), orchestrator-workers, and evaluator-optimizer loops, the last suited to cases with clear evaluation criteria where iteration adds measurable value. — [Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- Google Research (2025 paper, 2026 blog): across 180 configurations, independent parallel agents amplified errors up to 17.2 times, while a central orchestrator held this to 4.4 times; centralised coordination improved a parallelisable task by 80.9% and every multi-agent variant degraded a sequential task by 39 to 70%. These are task benchmarks, not ideation. — [Google Research: Towards a science of scaling agent systems](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)

### Inferences
- Every system that reports a diversity gain changes the inputs to generation between calls or rounds. None reports a gain from simply running more copies of one prompt; Si et al. show that approach saturating at about 5% unique.
- "Sectioning" is the pattern these systems use for generation, and "voting" (identical tasks) is the pattern the reader's two sessions used. In the write-ups, voting is recommended for confidence, not for breadth.
- A clustering or similarity step sits between generation and ranking in the stronger systems (Nova's k-means, the co-scientist's proximity graph). It serves both de-duplication and steering of the next round.
- Adding without replacing (co-scientist) and separate islands (FunSearch) are the two published devices for letting agents build on each other without losing the earlier spread.

### Gaps
- Almost all novelty and quality figures are LLM-judged or embedding-based. Only Si et al. (2024, 2025) and the co-scientist's small expert panel use human ratings, and the 2025 execution study suggests ideation-stage ratings overstate LLM ideas.
- No published system reports results for product or consumer ideation at the scale of tens of small-model agents; nearly all are research-idea systems on frontier or mid-size models.
- Details of the Virtual Lab's merge step, number of discussion rounds and sampling settings could not be retrieved from the paper itself.

## 4. LLM evidence: does seeing or debating each other's ideas raise or lower diversity and quality compared with independent generation? Conformity and echo effects

### Takeaway
Independent LLM agents are not independent in the human sense: they share one distribution and repeat each other without ever interacting. Unstructured interaction then narrows the pool further and can degrade correctness through conformity, while structured interaction (differentiated roles, critique and revision, partitioned groups) raises judged quality of individual ideas and, in some designs, diversity. The evidence on quality and on diversity points in different directions, and the two must be measured separately.

### Cited Findings

#### Independent generation is already homogeneous
- 2025: on 100 open-ended queries with 50 responses per model, "in 79% of cases, the average similarity exceeds 0.8" within one model at temperature 1.0. Average similarity between different models' responses was 71% to 82%. With temperature 2.0 and min-p decoding, 61.2% of response pairs still exceeded 0.8. The query set includes a "brainstorm & ideation" category. — [Jiang et al. 2025, Artificial Hivemind](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954v1)
- 2024 (older model, GPT-4): idea pools had within-pool cosine similarity of 0.255 to 0.432 across 35 prompts, against 0.243 for a pool of ideas from separate humans. The base prompt scored 0.377; a chain-of-thought prompt (list 100 short titles, revise to make them bolder and more different, then describe) scored 0.255; a Steve Jobs persona 0.368. — [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- Same study: the most common ideas "often occur early in a completion request"; the estimated number of unique ideas in the domain was about 3,700 for the base prompt and 4,700 for chain-of-thought; overlap between pools from different prompts was "relatively low", so the authors recommend generating smaller pools with different strategies and combining them. — [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- Same study: telling the model the cosine similarity of existing ideas gave the least diverse pool of all (0.432), and a simulated "hybrid brainstorm" in which groups of four sessions picked the best 10 of 120 ideas scored 0.393, less diverse than the base prompt. — [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- 2026: Deng, Brucks and Toubia report that independent LLM sessions give less diverse ideas than independent humans for two reasons: LLMs "aggregate knowledge into a unified distribution" where a human population is partitioned, and early outputs in a session constrain later ones (fixation). Personas of ordinary individuals improve partitioning but amplify fixation; chain-of-thought reduces fixation; combining both gave the highest diversity, "outperforming humans". — [Deng, Brucks & Toubia 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408)
- 2024: temperature is only weakly correlated with novelty and moderately with incoherence in story generation. — [Peeperkorn et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00492)
- 2025: asking a model to output several responses with stated probabilities ("verbalized sampling") raised diversity 1.6 to 2.1 times over direct prompting in creative writing, per its authors. — [Zhang et al. 2025, Verbalized Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)

#### Interaction and diversity
- 2026: across more than 10,000 proposals on 20 topics, Chen and colleagues found that "dense communication topologies accelerate premature convergence", that authority-driven structures suppress semantic diversity, and that collapse "arises primarily from the interaction structure rather than inherent model insufficiency". — [Chen et al. 2026, Diversity Collapse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18005)
- Same study: pool diversity (Vendi score) by structure was 8.08 for flat junior-peer groups, 6.93 leader-led, 6.08 mixed-seniority, 5.57 with no personas and 4.65 for interdisciplinary expert groups. LLM-judged quality ran the other way but in a narrow band, 7.88 (flat) to 8.50 (interdisciplinary); the authors note quality variation is "an order of magnitude smaller than diversity variation". Leader-led sessions opened with deference in about 61% of turns and showed pushback in under 1%. — [Chen et al. 2026, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)
- Same study, conflicting internal results: the main text says a nominal-group procedure (blind writing before discussion) starts with the highest diversity and that splitting agents into subgroups produces a mid-discussion rebound in diversity; an appendix ablation with three agents found the nominal-group procedure gave lower within-topic diversity than the recursive discussion under plain personas (2.669 versus 3.125, d = 2.21) and no difference under flat-peer personas. For one model (o1-mini), enforced subgroups reduced diversity. — [Chen et al. 2026, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)
- Same study: mixing models (DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4) raised diversity by 34% in interdisciplinary groups (p = 0.0003) and had no significant effect in flat groups. — [Chen et al. 2026, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)
- 2025: critique and revision loops raised the non-duplicate share from 0.69 to 0.86 (see question 3). — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1)
- 2024 (older models): the authors of LLM Discussion state that "the high homogeneity of LLMs renders it difficult for discussions to diverge" and that plain discussions "quickly converge"; roles were the main source of originality gains. — [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06373v3)

#### Interaction and judged quality
- 2025: structured discussion among three agents raised overall LLM-judged proposal quality by 0.44 to 0.46 points on a 10-point scale over a solitary agent, and humans preferred the multi-agent proposal in 35 of 40 pairs (87.5%). A designated leader raised quality further. Flat groups of junior personas performed at the solitary baseline (7.73 versus 7.75). — [Chen et al. 2025, Beyond Brainstorming](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04575)
- Taken with the 2026 paper from the same group, the same structures that scored highest on judged quality (leader-led, interdisciplinary, senior) scored lowest on pool diversity. — [Chen et al. 2026, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2); [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04575)

#### Conformity and echo effects (reasoning tasks with right answers)
- 2025: on the BenchForm benchmark, "all the evaluated LLMs show a tendency to conform". Smaller models conformed far more: Qwen2-7B followed a wrong majority 95.3% of the time and Llama3-8B 61.1%, against 9.2% for Llama3.1-70B and 24.4% for GPT-4o. Cutting the majority from six agents to three reduced one model's conformity from 69.9% to 32.6%; cutting rounds from five to one reduced it only to 62.7%. A single dissenter did not reduce conformity. A reflection step cut conformity roughly in half for one model and made another worse. — [Weng, Chen & Wang 2025, ICLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13381v2)
- 2025: with GPT-4o-mini, Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral 7B, majority accuracy after two debate rounds fell in 21 of 30 configuration-task comparisons. Agents "frequently shift from correct to incorrect answers in response to peer reasoning", weaker agents dragged groups down, and a prompt rewarding correctness did not fix it. — [Wynn, Satija & Hadfield 2025, Talk Isn't Always Cheap](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v2)
- 2026: a debate benchmark attributes 65% of debate errors to "collective delusion", agents reinforcing each other's wrong assumptions. — [M3MAD-Bench 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02854)
- 2024 (older models): multi-agent debate did "not reliably outperform" self-consistency and ensembling; a set-up with an "angel" and a "devil" agent often did worse because the devil disagreed even when the first answer was right. — [Smit et al. 2024, ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html)
- 2025: assigning agents different reasoning methods, not just different personas, out-performed standard debate in fewer rounds; the authors argue personas alone leave a "fixed mental set". — [Liu et al. 2025, DMAD, ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3de667dab3b3d812583abc0a786139a0-Abstract-Conference.html)
- 2026 preprint: plain debate often under-performs majority vote, and diversity of initial answers correlates with debate gains. — [Demystifying Multi-Agent Debate 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19921)
- 2023 (older models): Liang and colleagues found a single agent reflecting on its own answer cannot generate new thoughts once confident ("degeneration of thought"), and that debate needed an adaptive stopping rule and only a modest level of disagreement to help; an LLM judge favoured the debater built on its own model. — [Liang et al. 2023, EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19118v4)
- 2025: for answer aggregation, mixing different models often lowered average quality, and repeated sampling from the single best model beat a mixture by 6.6% on one benchmark; at fixed quality, more diversity helped. — [Li et al. 2025, Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)

### Inferences
- The reader's result (33 agents, about a dozen themes) matches the published pattern for identical prompts: Si et al. found 5% unique at the 0.8 threshold, and Meincke found the commonest ideas come first in each completion, so many short parallel runs each return the head of the same distribution.
- A flat "already found" list is weak on the evidence available: appending prior titles still left 95% duplicates in Si et al., and feeding similarity information back gave Meincke's least diverse pool. Neither study isolates the list as a variable, so this is suggestive only.
- Diversity and judged quality trade off across interaction structures. A session that wants a wide pool should use flat, exploratory, differentiated generators and delay authority and synthesis; a session that wants one polished proposal benefits from a leader and critics.
- Conformity findings come from tasks with right answers. Their relevance to ideation is that small models shown a majority view tend to adopt it, and that larger visible majorities matter more than more rounds.
- Selecting "the best" inside the generation loop reduces diversity (Meincke's hybrid result), which argues for keeping selection out of the divergent phase.

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly compares, for product or open-ended ideation, N independent LLM agents against the same N agents in a brainwriting-style rotation, with human-rated quality. The closest are Chen et al. (2025, 2026), which use research proposals and LLM judges, and whose nominal-group results conflict internally.
- No study isolates the effect of a negative "do not repeat these" list on diversity.
- Conformity benchmarks use multiple-choice reasoning; I found no conformity measurement on open-ended idea generation beyond the diversity metrics above.

## 5. LLM evidence on group size, number of rounds and role assignment

### Takeaway
Reported optima are small: three to eight agents per interacting group and three to five rounds, with diminishing or negative returns beyond. Role assignment matters more than headcount, but the type of role matters: generator-side personas and flat peer framing widen the pool, critic-side expertise and leaders raise judged quality, and scripted contrarians can hurt.

### Cited Findings

#### Group size
- Diversity per agent falls as groups grow: from three to seven agents, pool diversity rose only 7.4% (Vendi 3.09 to 3.32) while diversity per agent fell from 1.03 to 0.47. — [Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)
- Judged quality was flat with size and length: three agents and five rounds scored 7.96; five agents and twelve rounds 8.12. Longer discussions raised novelty and lowered workability. — [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04575)
- VirSci's novelty measure peaked at eight members; the authors attribute the decline beyond that to coordination problems. — [Su et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09403v4)
- LLM Discussion found four agents gave the best overall performance. — [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06373v3)
- Three parallel critics gave the best diversity (0.80), with four slightly lower (0.79). — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1)
- Conformity rises with the size of the visible majority (six versus three peers: 69.9% versus 32.6% for one model). — [Weng, Chen & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13381v2)
- Anthropic's guidance scales the number of subagents to task complexity (one agent for simple fact-finding, two to four for comparisons, more than ten for complex research with clearly divided responsibilities) and reports early failures such as spawning 50 subagents for simple queries. — [Anthropic 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

#### Rounds
- Critique and revision: gains continue to three rounds; "a fourth iteration offers negligible gains and sometimes hurts precision". — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1)
- LLM Discussion: originality rises and elaboration falls with more rounds; performance "does not consistently improve with more than 5 rounds". — [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06373v3)
- VirSci: novelty peaked at about five discussion turns. — [Su et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09403v4)
- Nova used three planning and search iterations, and unique ideas kept growing across them only when planning was enabled. — [Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14255v2)
- ResearchAgent's refinement gains saturate after about three iterations (secondary). — [Baek et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07738)
- Longer interaction raises conformity modestly (33.9% to 44.4% from one to five rounds for one model). — [Weng, Chen & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13381v2)
- The co-scientist is the exception: its Elo measure kept rising with compute with no saturation observed, in a design that adds new hypotheses to a tournament instead of extending one conversation. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- 2023 (older models): in multi-agent debate both more agents and more rounds improved accuracy, with main results at three agents and two rounds; long concatenated transcripts can exceed the context window. — [Du et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14325)

#### Roles
- Generators with personas: proposer-side personas gave the highest diversity among persona placements (0.81); roles without any discussion scored highest on originality in LLM Discussion's ablation (4.58). — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1); [Lu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06373v3)
- Persona type matters: flat junior-peer personas gave the most diverse pool (8.08) and interdisciplinary expert personas the least (4.65), below no personas at all (5.57). — [Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)
- Named-celebrity and "creative entrepreneur" personas moved GPT-4 pool similarity only slightly (0.348 to 0.385 against a base of 0.377). — [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- Critics: a specialised critic "delivers the strongest quality gains"; the Virtual Lab adds a Scientific Critic to any meeting; the co-scientist separates Reflection (review) from Generation. — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1); [Virtual Lab repository](https://github.com/zou-group/virtual-lab); [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- Combiners: the co-scientist's Evolution agent combines top hypotheses and adds the result as a new entrant; a simulated hybrid brainstorm in which GPT-4 merged four sessions and picked the best 10 ideas reduced diversity. — [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864); [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- Leaders: raise judged quality (+0.46 to +0.57 on a 10-point scale; the paper is unclear whether the comparison is the solitary agent or the leaderless group) and lower diversity (6.93 versus 8.08). — [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04575); [Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)
- Devil's advocates: a scripted "devil" agent lowered accuracy when first answers were right; a single dissenter did not reduce conformity. Different reasoning methods per agent worked better than different personas. — [Smit et al. 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html); [Weng, Chen & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13381v2); [Liu et al. 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3de667dab3b3d812583abc0a786139a0-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Orchestrator: a central orchestrator contained error amplification (4.4 times versus 17.2 times for independent agents) on task benchmarks. — [Google Research 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)
- Judges: LLM judges were near chance against human reviewers for research ideas (53.3%), and a judge favoured debaters built on its own model. — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1); [Liang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19118v4)

### Inferences
- "Group size" means two different things. The number of agents that see each other should stay small (three to eight). The total number of generators can be large only if they are partitioned into groups or given different inputs; otherwise each added agent contributes less than half the diversity of the first few.
- Three rounds of critique and revision is the most consistently reported stopping point for a single idea thread. Open-ended tournaments that add new entrants can run longer.
- For a wide pool, put differentiation on the generator side (personas, methods, source material) and expertise on the critic side.
- A contrarian by script is not supported. Structural disagreement (different methods, different inputs, separate subgroups) is.

### Gaps
- The optimal sizes come from different metrics (embedding novelty, LLM-judged quality, Vendi diversity) and different models; none was validated with human-rated idea quality at scale.
- No study tests more than about eight interacting agents for ideation, and none tests tens of partitioned small-model agents.
- I found no direct test of a "combiner" role's effect on pool diversity other than the co-scientist's design description and Meincke's selection result.

## 6. Where the human evidence does and does not transfer to parallel LLM agents, and why

### Takeaway
The human finding that people should generate alone first transfers only in form. Its cause (turn-taking) does not exist for parallel agents, and its hidden assumption (each person brings different knowledge) fails for copies of one model given one brief. What transfers better is the cognitive literature on exposure: what agents are shown steers what they produce, alternation beats continuous sharing, and selection is a separate problem that neither humans nor LLM judges solve well.

### Cited Findings

#### Does not transfer, or transfers only with changes
- [HUMAN] Production blocking, the main cause of group loss, is about waiting to speak and was unaffected by whether others' ideas were audible. — [Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)
- [LLM] Parallel agents have no turn-taking, but independent sessions of one model are far more alike than independent people: LLMs "aggregate knowledge into a unified distribution" where human populations are partitioned. — [Deng, Brucks & Toubia 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408)
- [LLM] In one study the human comparison pool that out-diversified GPT-4 was itself an aggregation of ideas from separate individuals, that is, a nominal group. — [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- [HUMAN] Social loafing falls when contributions are identifiable; evaluation apprehension is a weak cause of group loss. — [Karau & Williams 1993](https://typeset.io/pdf/social-loafing-a-meta-analytic-review-and-theoretical-22g4ic0jro.pdf); [Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)
- [LLM] The analogous social failure in agents is conformity to visible peers, strongest in small models and with larger visible majorities. — [Weng, Chen & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13381v2)
- [HUMAN] Incubation gives a modest benefit (d = 0.29), larger with an undemanding filler task. — [Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://www.research.lancs.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-a-metaanalytic-review(ec023bd3-01e9-453e-854a-3dca00e2d146)/export.html)
- [LLM] Within one LLM session early outputs constrain later ones, and the advantage of a better prompt disappears after about 750 to 800 ideas in a single session as the pool depletes. — [Deng, Brucks & Toubia 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408); [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- [HUMAN] Quantity instructions raise both the number of ideas and the number of good ideas. — [Adanez 2005](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/spanish-journal-of-psychology/article/abs/does-quantity-generate-quality-testing-the-fundamental-principle-of-brainstorming/5D8442A9F86CBB001D901C3F1262489C)
- [LLM] Raw quantity from one prompt saturates: about 5% unique among 4,000, and the unique count plateaus. — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1)
- [HUMAN] Authentic dissent helps and role-played devil's advocacy is weaker or backfires. — [Nemeth et al. 2004 working paper](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4k70n7v8)
- [LLM] A scripted devil agent lowered performance; all LLM dissent is role-played by construction. — [Smit et al. 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html)

#### Transfers in substance
- [HUMAN] Exposure to many categories widens output; exposure to many ideas in few categories narrows it; seeing typical ideas causes conformity to them. — [Nijstad et al. 2002](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/914580572); [Kohn & Smith 2011](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/acp.1699)
- [LLM] Dense sharing accelerates convergence; planned retrieval of new material and prompts that mix strong candidates with diversity representatives sustain it. — [Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18005); [Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14255v2); [overview of AlphaEvolve and FunSearch](https://richardcsuwandi.github.io/blog/2025/llm-algorithm-discovery)
- [HUMAN] Alternating solo and group phases beats either alone. — [Korde & Paulus 2017 summary](https://medium.com/stanford-d-school/alternate-solo-team-work-eb7431893b42)
- [LLM] Systems that alternate generation with review and re-generation report rising diversity or quality across about three rounds. — [Ueda et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1); [Gottweis et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)
- [HUMAN] People select ideas no better than chance without structure. — [Rietzschel et al. 2006](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/productivity-is-not-enough-a-comparison-of-interactive-and-nomina/)
- [LLM] LLM judges agree with human reviewers at about chance, and ideation-stage ratings of LLM ideas fell after execution. — [Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1); [Si et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803v1)
- [HUMAN] A trained facilitator brings interacting groups up to nominal-group output. — [Kramer et al. 2001 record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/897307970)
- [LLM] An orchestrator that gives each worker a distinct objective and boundaries prevents duplicated work and contains errors. — [Anthropic 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Google Research 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)
- [HUMAN] Larger groups lose more when interacting by voice and gain more when interacting electronically. — [Mullen et al. 1991 record](https://www.scilit.net/publications/e7218c55746018a0c3a5fc3d0a54871a); [Gallupe et al. 1992 record](https://scilit.com/publications/9d4ac6467aaba6ce8aff99d4f31aeab9)
- [LLM] Diversity per agent halves between three and seven interacting agents. — [Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005v2)

### Inferences
Each recommendation states its evidence base. "HUMAN" means it rests on the human-group studies in questions 1 and 2; "LLM" on the agent studies in questions 3 to 5; "BOTH" where the two agree. None has been tested on the reader's exact set-up.

- Do not give every agent the same brief. Split the problem space before generation and give each agent or small group a different slice: a different user segment, constraint, source document, ideation method or persona. Rests on LLM (Si et al. 5% unique; Nova's seeds from ten methods; Meincke's low overlap between prompt strategies; Anthropic's task boundaries). The human analogue, people bringing different knowledge, is what makes nominal groups work, so this restores the assumption the human finding depends on.
- Keep a first round that is independent, with no view of others' output. Rests on BOTH (human nominal-group and hybrid results; LLM conformity and dense-topology convergence), with the caveat that the one LLM test of a blind-writing first phase gave conflicting results.
- Have each agent produce a long list in steps (many short titles, then revise for difference, then describe) instead of a few ideas in one pass. Rests on LLM (Meincke chain-of-thought 0.255 versus 0.377; Deng et al. on chain-of-thought reducing fixation; commonest ideas appear first in a completion). The human quantity-goal finding points the same way but for a different reason.
- Between rounds, have the coordinator cluster the pool and send back a map of categories with instructions to work in empty or thin regions, plus a few deliberately varied examples, instead of a flat "already found" list. Rests on HUMAN for the mechanism (Nijstad et al. on category exposure; Kohn and Smith on fixation) and on LLM for the practice (Nova's clustering and planned search; co-scientist's proximity graph and "research expansion"; AlphaEvolve's diversity representatives). The weakness of flat negative lists is suggested, not proven, by Si et al. and Meincke.
- When agents do build on each other, do it in small partitioned groups of three to five, flat and without a leader, for the divergent phase. Rests on LLM (Chen et al. 2026 on topology, authority and per-agent returns; BenchForm on majority size). Human brainwriting evidence (Paulus and Yang) is consistent.
- Add variants as new entries; never overwrite the idea they came from. Rests on LLM (co-scientist Evolution agent; FunSearch islands).
- Limit critique and revision to about three rounds per idea thread. Rests on LLM (Ueda et al.; LLM Discussion; VirSci; ResearchAgent).
- Put expertise and personas on critics for quality, and use a leader or synthesiser only in the convergent phase. Rests on LLM (Ueda et al. critic personas; Chen et al. 2025 leader effect; Chen et al. 2026 diversity cost of authority).
- Do not use a scripted devil's advocate. Use agents with different methods or inputs to create disagreement. Rests on BOTH (Nemeth on role-played dissent; Smit et al. and DMAD).
- Separate selection from generation, use pairwise comparison instead of absolute scores, compare similar ideas against each other, and keep people in the final choice. Rests on BOTH (Rietzschel and Girotra on human selection; Si et al. on LLM judges; co-scientist and Nova tournament designs; Meincke's finding that in-loop "pick the best" reduced diversity).
- Consider mixing models for generators where cost allows, but expect a limited gain. Rests on LLM, with conflicting evidence (Chen et al. 2026: +34% in some structures, none in flat groups; Artificial Hivemind: 71 to 82% similarity across models; Self-MoA: mixing lowers average quality for aggregation tasks).
- With small models, be more cautious about showing agents each other's output, because small models conform most. Rests on LLM (BenchForm; Wynn et al.).
- Measure the pool, not only the best idea: report the non-duplicate share at a fixed similarity threshold and the number of clusters per round, so the next session can be compared with the last two. Rests on LLM (the metric used by Si et al., Nova, Ueda et al. and Meincke).

Why the human evidence fails to transfer where it does:
- Human group loss is caused by a shared channel (one speaker at a time). Parallel agents have separate channels, so removing interaction buys nothing by itself.
- Human nominal groups are diverse because people differ. Copies of one model given one prompt differ only by sampling noise, and sampling noise is small (79% of queries above 0.8 similarity).
- Human motivational effects (loafing, apprehension, goal-setting, time pressure) have no established agent counterpart; the agent counterpart of social influence is conformity to text in context.
- Incubation depends on a mind that persists across the break. An agent has no state between calls; the nearest equivalent is a fresh context that does not contain the earlier ideas, which removes within-session fixation but also returns the same common first ideas.
- Human dissent can be authentic. Agent dissent is always assigned, and assigned dissent has the weaker record in both literatures.

### Gaps
- No published experiment tests the full protocol sketched above (partitioned briefs, independent round, clustered feedback, small flat groups, tournament selection) against the reader's baseline of many identical small-model agents. The recommendations are assembled from separate studies with different tasks, models and metrics.
- No evidence was found on how many distinct slices a coordinator can usefully create before slices themselves become redundant.
- No evidence was found on whether incubation-like breaks, time limits or quantity targets have any effect on LLM agents beyond prompt length and list length.
- Human evidence on electronic brainstorming at large group sizes is old (1990s to 2007) and its meta-analytic figures could not be verified from primary sources here.
- The Diversity Collapse paper (2026) is the single most relevant LLM source and has internal inconsistencies (nominal-group procedure results; two different p-values for one comparison); its conclusions should be treated as provisional.
