# Agent-to-agent protocols, agent marketplaces and agentic commerce launches (state as of November 2026)

Notes on method and reliability (read first):
- Research date: 17 Nov 2026. About 17 search/fetch calls. Two primary pages were fetched and read in full (Linux Foundation A2A press release of 9 Apr 2026; Anthropic "Project Deal" write-up of 24 Apr 2026). Everything else comes from search-result summaries of the linked pages, so the link shown is the page the claim was attributed to, but exact wording and figures were NOT re-verified on the page. Items marked "(secondary, unverified)" come from trade blogs or aggregators and should be checked before being quoted in a final report.
- Status labels used: ANNOUNCED (press release only), SHIPPED (live product), ADOPTED (public evidence of real use), WITHDRAWN.
- Confidence: High = primary source read, or several independent reputable outlets agree. Medium = reputable press but single source, or vendor self-report. Low = blog/aggregator only, or sources conflict.

## 1. Which open protocols and standards exist for agent interoperability and agent commerce? Who backs each, how mature, and what do they specify?

### Takeaway
The protocol layer is crowded and consolidating under the Linux Foundation: MCP (agent-to-tool) and A2A (agent-to-agent) are both stable, widely backed and now in the same foundation, and there are at least four overlapping commerce/payment standards (ACP, UCP, AP2, card-network schemes, plus crypto x402). What is specified is discovery, task delegation, checkout and signed payment mandates; what is NOT standardised in any adopted protocol is open price negotiation between two parties' agents, consumer-grade agent reputation, or dispute resolution. Agent identity ("know your agent") is fragmented with no settled standard.

### Cited Findings

Agent2Agent (A2A)
- Linux Foundation release dated 9 Apr 2026: more than 150 organisations support A2A, up from 50+ at launch in April 2025; named supporters AWS, Cisco, Google, IBM, Microsoft, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow. Version 1.0 is the first stable specification (multi-protocol support, enterprise multi-tenancy, modernised security flows). Core GitHub repo has 22,000+ stars; SDKs in five languages (Python, JavaScript, Java, Go, .NET). Confidence: high (primary, read in full). — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)
- What A2A specifies, per the same release: "Signed Agent Cards" for cryptographic identity verification, discovery via a common semantic model and version negotiation, delegation of sub-tasks and coordination of workflows without sharing internal memory. It does not specify price negotiation or payment. Confidence: high. — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)
- Adoption evidence is enterprise-only: integrations in Microsoft Azure AI Foundry and Copilot Studio and in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Runtime; "active production use" claimed in supply chain, financial services, insurance and IT operations, but the release names no specific production customers. Confidence: high that this is what is claimed; medium on real depth of use. — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)
- Governance history: Google donated A2A to the Linux Foundation in June 2025. — [PureAI, 23 Jun 2025](https://pureai.com/Articles/2025/06/23/Linux-Foundation-Adopts-Google-Backed-Protocol-for-AI-Agent-Interoperability.aspx)
- A2A reportedly moved under the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF, the Linux Foundation body that also hosts MCP) in August 2026; sources give 17 Aug or 20 Aug 2026. Confidence: low to medium (secondary sources only, dates conflict; not confirmed against a Linux Foundation primary announcement). — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/googles-a2a-protocol-joins-aaif-consolidating-the-agent-economys-protocol-layer-under-one-roof/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/google-a2a-aaif-agent-protocol-open-standard-august-2026)

Model Context Protocol (MCP)
- Anthropic donated MCP to the new Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, launched 9 Dec 2025; co-founded by Anthropic, Block and OpenAI, with AWS, Google, Microsoft, Cloudflare and Bloomberg reported as supporting members. Confidence: high on the donation, medium on the member list. — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/anthropic-donates-mcp-to-new-agentic-ai-foundation)
- Reported scale: 97 million monthly SDK downloads (Python plus TypeScript) attributed to an Anthropic announcement of 25 Mar 2026 (secondary, unverified against Anthropic). — [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/mcp-hits-97-million-installs-and-becomes-the-agentic-ai-standard)
- Server counts conflict: 14,274+ public servers in the PulseMCP index (Jan 2026) versus 87 curated entries in the official registry at that time; others cite "10,000+ active servers". Confidence: low (index definitions differ). — [Moklabs research report, 20 Mar 2026](https://moklabs-research.pages.dev/reports/technology/2026-03-20-mcp-ecosystem-standards-adoption)
- A "41% enterprise adoption" figure appears in one June 2026 blog with no primary source. Confidence: low; do not quote without verification. — [beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/mcp-enterprise-standard-2026)
- MCP is agent-to-tool/data, not agent-to-agent; the Linux Foundation describes A2A as complementary to MCP. — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)

Agent Payments Protocol (AP2, Google)
- More than 60 organisations in payments and financial services support AP2 (as of 9 Apr 2026). Confidence: high. — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)
- AP2 launched September 2025; it defines signed Intent, Cart and Payment Mandates so an agent can pay on a user's behalf across cards and stablecoins; partners reported include Mastercard, PayPal and Coinbase (secondary, unverified against Google's spec). Confidence: medium. — [Eco, agent payment protocols compared](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14839407-agent-payment-protocols-compared); [Spark research on AP2](https://www.spark.money/research/google-ap2-agent-payment-protocol)
- Conflict: one source says card-network schemes (Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Trusted Agent Protocol) plug into AP2 as funding instruments for the Payment Mandate; another says the networks and AP2 are not interoperable. One source claims AP2 was moved to the FIDO Alliance; this was not corroborated. Confidence: low. — [Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14839407-agent-payment-protocols-compared); [BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/03/13/ai-agent-commerce-standards-war-ucp-x402-visa-mastercard/)

Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP, Google and Shopify)
- Announced 11 Jan 2026 as an open standard for agent-driven commerce, co-developed by Shopify and Google; other named co-developers/backers reported are Etsy, Wayfair, Target, Walmart, Mastercard and Visa. Confidence: high on date and the two leads, medium on the full backer list. — [Shopify news](https://www.shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale); [Juspay](https://juspay.io/blog/google-s-universal-commerce-protocol-what-it-means-for-payment-orchestration)
- UCP is "fully compatible with AP2" through an AP2 mandates extension that captures cryptographic evidence of the user's consent to purchase. Confidence: high. — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)
- UCP supports several transports: REST, MCP, AP2 and A2A; it serves Google AI Mode and Gemini. In May 2026 UCP catalogue access was reportedly opened to any developer (secondary, unverified). — [ChatterGo deep dive](https://www.chattergo.com/blog/shopify-agentic-commerce-ucp-checkout-kit-deep-dive)

Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP, OpenAI and Stripe)
- ACP is the checkout standard behind ChatGPT's Instant Checkout. The protocol survived OpenAI's withdrawal of Instant Checkout in March 2026 (see section 3). Payment providers continued to adopt it, for example Checkout.com for enterprise merchants. Confidence: medium. — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/checkout-adopts-acp-backed-by-openai-to-power-agentic-commerce-for-enterprise-merchants/); [Swell explainer, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.swell.is/content/agentic-commerce-protocol-explained)
- Wix, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Squarespace and commercetools reportedly support ACP via Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite (secondary, unverified). — [Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/openai-acp-agentic-commerce-protocol)

Card-network schemes
- Visa: Trusted Agent Protocol verifies an AI agent at transaction time; "Intelligent Commerce Connect" shipped 8 Apr 2026 as a protocol-agnostic on-ramp accepting agents over Visa TAP, Mastercard's protocol, ACP and UCP at once (secondary). — [Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/visa-bets-on-open-infrastructure-as-payment-giants-race-for-agentic-commerce/)
- Visa press release dated 2 Jul 2026: AI agents browsed, selected and initiated purchases in live transactions in Europe; 31 European issuers completed live transactions; launch merchants included lastminute.com, Frasers, Cleverbridge and BrickDepot; merchants recognise agents via Trusted Agent Protocol and an Agent Directory. Status: SHIPPED at pilot scale. Confidence: medium to high (company press release). — [Visa via Mynewsdesk](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/visa/pressreleases/visa-and-banks-across-europe-reach-the-next-phase-of-agentic-commerce-unlocking-secure-access-to-merchants-3457328)
- In December 2025 Visa said agent transactions had taken place on live systems, and that 20+ agents or agent platforms were integrating with Visa Intelligent Commerce. — [BetaNews, 18 Dec 2025](https://betanews.com/2025/12/18/visa-says-ai-agents-will-complete-shopping-purchases-for-you-in-2026/)
- Mastercard Agent Pay (launched 2025) uses "Agentic Tokens" that bind an AI agent to an individual user; reported live transactions include Hong Kong (late March 2026), New Zealand with Westpac, and a Santander transaction described as Europe's first end-to-end AI-agent payment (early 2026). Sources disagree on when Mastercard's very first live transaction happened (Q3 or Q4 2025 versus March 2026). Confidence: medium. — [Payments Industry Intelligence](https://paymentsindustryintelligence.com/visa-and-mastercard-accelerate-agentic-payments-through-bank-pilots/); [Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/visa-mastercard-agentic-commerce-strategy)

Crypto rail: x402 (Coinbase-originated, HTTP 402 stablecoin micropayments)
- Headline usage is heavily inflated. Visa's Cuy Sheffield put adjusted x402 volume at about $19 million across roughly 134 million transactions (as of 21 Apr 2026), after excluding identified wash and test activity. — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/converge/infrastructure/visa-s-sheffield-pegs-adjusted-x402-volume-at-19m)
- Artemis analysis: roughly half of x402 transactions are artificial; an earlier analysis linked about 48% of transactions and 81% of volume to gamed activity. The x402 Foundation is reported to have 17 premier members. — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/half-of-x402s-transaction-volume-is-artificial-the-infrastructure-layer-keeps-building-anyway/); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/the-x402-foundation-has-17-premier-members-and-75-million-transactions-most-of-them-arent-real/)
- An a16z partner put real AI-agent payment volume at about $1.6 million in 30 days, not the $24 million reported by x402.org. — [Cointelegraph](https://regional-front.cointelegraph.com/news/ai-agent-payment-volume-closer-to-1-6m-says-a16z)
- American Banker ran a piece asking whether there is an "agentic payments bubble", reporting a volume decline on the rail. — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/agentic-payment-rail-shows-volume-decline)

Agent identity, naming and discovery ("know your agent")
- No single KYA standard exists. Competing efforts: AGNTCY (Cisco-originated, Linux Foundation) identity and directory, which prescribes no single identity format and supports existing identity providers and W3C DIDs; MIT's NANDA index with "AgentFacts"; Microsoft Entra Agent ID; MCP registry; A2A agent cards. Confidence: medium. — [arXiv 2508.03095, "Evolution of AI Agent Registry Solutions"](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03095v3); [AGNTCY identity docs](https://docs.agntcy.org/pages/identity/identity.html)
- The Decentralized Identity Foundation took over an agent identity framework donated by Vouched (March 2026) and named it KYA-OS (Know Your Agent Operating System). Status: ANNOUNCED/spec stage. — [DIF blog](https://blog.identity.foundation/kya-os/)
- GoDaddy publishes an Agent Name Service (ANS) registry design, a DNS-like naming approach for agents. Status: early. — [ans-registry design doc (mirror)](https://github.xxxxic.workers.dev/godaddy/ans-registry/blob/main/DESIGN.md)
- NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation reportedly launched an AI Agent Standards Initiative in February 2026; ERC-8004 is an Ethereum agent identity and reputation registry also marketed as KYA (secondary, unverified). — [DEV Community, "State of agent identity Q2 2026"](https://dev.to/piiiico/the-state-of-agent-identity-q2-2026-1emb)
- InfoWorld commentary: four standards address agent identity but none answers whether the agent is still the one that was approved (behavioural drift). — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4216502/4-standards-solve-agent-identity-none-solves-the-harder-question-is-it-still-the-agent-you-approved.html)

### Inferences
- A founder does not need to invent plumbing: discovery (A2A agent cards, registries), tool access (MCP), consented payment (AP2 mandates, network agentic tokens) all exist as open specs with big-company backing. Building a proprietary agent-to-agent protocol would be swimming against consolidation.
- The standards all encode "owner confirms" in some form (AP2 mandates capture user consent; Visa/Mastercard bind agent to user; UPI pilot requires user authentication). An "owners confirm" design is aligned with where the rails are going, not a differentiator by itself.
- The unstandardised parts (multi-round negotiation between independently owned agents, reputation for small sellers' agents, disputes) are where a marketplace would have to supply its own rules. That is both the open space and the hard part.
- Crypto-rail agent commerce has almost no real volume once wash activity is removed; it is not evidence of demand.

### Gaps
- No verified primary source was read for the AP2 specification, the UCP specification or the ACP specification; the description of mandates and transports rests on secondary explainers.
- The August 2026 transfer of A2A into AAIF was not confirmed from a primary announcement.
- No public count of real production A2A deployments between different companies (cross-organisation, as opposed to inside one enterprise) was found.
- No official totals of agentic tokens issued or agent transactions processed by Visa or Mastercard were found.
- Nothing found on IETF/W3C formal standardisation outcomes for agent identity; only drafts.

## 2. Which agent marketplaces, directories and stores exist? Traction, revenue, failures?

### Takeaway
"Agent stores" are plentiful on the enterprise side (AWS, Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, Anthropic) but they are procurement catalogues where humans buy agents, not markets where agents deal with each other; public traction data is thin and mostly listing counts. The best-known consumer store (OpenAI's GPT Store) is widely reported to have failed to deliver creator revenue, and no startup "agents hire agents" marketplace with credible, verifiable traction was found.

### Cited Findings
- AWS launched an "AI Agents and Tools" category in AWS Marketplace in July 2025 with more than 900 listings, positioned as a one-stop shop for enterprise procurement of agents, tools and guardrails. Status: SHIPPED. Confidence: high. — [CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AWS-AI-agent-marketplace-store-cloud/753238/?.tsrc=rss); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2025/aws-unveils-ai-agent-marketplace-as-one-stop-shop-for-enterprise-deployment/)
- Later AWS listing counts of 2,100+ (Dec 2025) and 2,660 (early 2026), a "40x surge" in agent adoption, and a claim that 67% of deployments are free MCP servers attached to existing SaaS subscriptions come from a low-authority blog. Confidence: low. — [Eastgate Software](https://eastgate-software.com/insights/aws-marketplace-sees-40x-surge-in-ai-agent-adoption/)
- Microsoft introduced its Agent Store in May 2025 with 70+ agents; Google's agent marketplace section dates from November 2024 (partners include Accenture, Deloitte, Wipro); Salesforce AgentExchange debuted March 2025 as an agent-oriented subset of AppExchange, focused mostly on technology partners. No later adoption figures found for any of the three. Confidence: medium. — [CIO Dive](https://ciodive.com/news/AWS-AI-agent-marketplace-store-cloud/753238)
- Anthropic launched a "Claude Marketplace" on 6 Mar 2026 with no commission and six launch partners, described as enterprise spend consolidation rather than a consumer discovery store (secondary). Confidence: medium. — [AgentMarketCap](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/10/agent-distribution-wars-openai-gpt-store-anthropic-claude-plugins-github-marketplace)
- GPT Store (launched January 2024): the revenue-sharing promise "remains largely unfulfilled" two years on; only a small US-based invited group of creators tested revenue share; builders who earn money typically do so via affiliate links or ads inside their GPTs. Confidence: medium to high (several sources agree, including OpenAI's own community forum). — [AgentMarketCap](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/10/agent-distribution-wars-openai-gpt-store-anthropic-claude-plugins-github-marketplace); [OpenAI community forum](https://community.openai.com/t/guidance-for-gpt-store-builders-outside-the-us/1357911); [Wired (mirror)](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/openai-gpt-store)
- OpenAI has since repositioned ChatGPT as an app platform ("Apps in ChatGPT") with distribution it controls; this is also where commerce moved after Instant Checkout ended. — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/openai-turns-chatgpt-into-an-app-platform-with-distribution-it-controls/)
- Unverified secondary figures: about 3 million GPTs created, 159K active, 5.3% "survival"; Salesforce AgentExchange "$800M ARR". These come from a Chinese-language overview citing a third-party report and could not be traced to primary sources. Confidence: low; the Salesforce number in particular may conflate Agentforce product revenue with the marketplace. — [CSDN overview](https://aicoding.csdn.net/6a67224510ee7a33f292e2a6.html)

### Inferences
- Existing "agent marketplaces" sell agents to humans (B2B software distribution). The requester's concept, where every person or business has a standing agent with a job and those agents find and deal with each other, is a different thing, and no scaled live example of it surfaced. The closest real instance is Anthropic's one-week internal Project Deal experiment (section 5).
- The GPT Store experience is the cautionary case for consumer agent stores: supply was easy (millions of GPTs), discovery and monetisation were not, and the platform owner eventually changed the model.
- Because platform owners (OpenAI, Google, Amazon, Shopify) control distribution and are each building their own agent surface, an independent marketplace has to win on something they do not serve well (for example local small businesses, a specific country's payment rails and languages, or services rather than packaged goods).

### Gaps
- No reliable usage or revenue data for Microsoft Agent Store, Google's agent marketplace/Gemini Enterprise, Salesforce AgentExchange or Claude Marketplace.
- No search result covered startup agent-hires-agent marketplaces (for example Fetch.ai Agentverse, Olas, or newer venture-backed entrants) with traction numbers; a targeted follow-up is needed. Absence of results is not proof none exist.
- Crypto "agent economy" marketplaces: a search summary attributed to an OKX Ventures note the claim that Virtuals Protocol agents' reported "aGDP" reflects handled volume rather than service revenue, with revenue mostly from 1% fees on agent-token trades; the source page could not be identified, so this is recorded here as unsourced.
- No confirmed list of agent marketplaces that were formally shut down, other than the widely reported stall of GPT Store monetisation and the withdrawal of Instant Checkout.

## 3. What consumer agent-commerce features have large platforms launched? Usage, conversion, merchant adoption? How have marketplaces reacted to third-party shopping agents?

### Takeaway
2025–2026 produced a real test of "buy inside the assistant", and the headline result is negative: OpenAI withdrew ChatGPT Instant Checkout in March 2026 after about five months, with roughly a dozen to thirty merchants live and Walmart reporting in-chat conversion about one-third of its own site. What works so far is AI-assisted discovery plus retailer-controlled checkout (Amazon's Rufus/Alexa for Shopping, Walmart's Sparky, Shopify's agentic storefronts). Incumbent marketplaces have resisted third-party buying agents through robots.txt, terms of service and litigation, although the Ninth Circuit in August 2026 vacated Amazon's injunction against Perplexity's Comet.

Status: WITHDRAWN (March 2026; reports date the change variously to 4, 20 or 24 March, with The Information credited for the first report). Confidence: high on the event, low on the exact date. — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/openai-and-google-refine-early-ai-commerce-strategies/); [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/openai-drops-chatgpt-checkout-to-pursue-agent-vision); [EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/openai-pulls-back-on-chatgpt-instant-checkout/)

Confidence: high. — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2026/01/21/ebay-bans-ai-shopping-agents-updates-arbitration-provision/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3423031)

Confidence: medium. — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-amazon-block-agentic-ai-bots-and-begin-the-war/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/407835/amazon-shopify-seen-using-code-to-block-ai-agents.html)
- Amazon v. Perplexity: on 10 Mar 2026 Judge Maxine Chesney (N.D. Cal.) granted Amazon a preliminary injunction blocking Perplexity's Comet agent from Amazon accounts, finding Comet accessed accounts "with the Amazon user's permission but without authorization by Amazon". Confidence: high. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-10/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexity-s-ai-shopping-bots); [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/court-blocks-perplexity-from-accessing-amazon-systems-in-agentic-ai-lawsuit/)

Confidence: medium to high (several outlets; opinion not read). — [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/newsletters/us-court-overturns-amazon-injunction-perplexity); [Northeast Times, 6 Aug 2026](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/08/06/appeals-court-sides-with-perplexity-ai-in-amazon-shopping-dispute/)

### Inferences
- Consumers have shown willingness to use AI for product research and limited willingness (so far) to complete purchases inside a general chat assistant; retailers prefer to keep checkout, customer data and loyalty. A marketplace design where the owner confirms and the seller keeps the customer relationship fits the evidence better than fully delegated buying.
- Large marketplaces treat uninvited buying agents as a threat and gate access by partnership. An agent marketplace that depends on agents acting on closed platforms inherits legal and access risk; one built on consenting small sellers (who publish their own agent) avoids it.
- India-specific rails for delegated payment (UPI Circle, Reserve Pay) exist at pilot stage, which lowers the payment barrier for an "owner confirms" flow in India.

### Gaps
- No 2026 update was found on the NPCI/Razorpay/OpenAI UPI pilot (whether it reached general consumer availability, volumes, merchants beyond BigBasket), and nothing was found on ONDC and AI agents. This matters for an India-focused judgment and needs a dedicated search.
- No public order-volume or AI-referred-GMV figures from Shopify's agentic storefronts were retrieved (earnings disclosures not checked).
- No data on travel or booking agents (airline, hotel, OTA agent launches) was collected in this pass.
- Amazon "Buy for Me" usage data and the terms of any Amazon partnerships with outside agents were not found.
- OpenAI's own statement and data on Instant Checkout usage were not read directly.

## 4. Is there any real deployment of agent-to-agent negotiation? What measured outcomes are reported?

### Takeaway
Real deployments exist but are one-sided: a buyer's negotiation bot (chiefly Pactum, at Walmart, Maersk and now Ahold Delhaize USA) bargaining with human suppliers over low-value "tail" contracts, with self-reported savings of roughly 1.5–3% and deal closure with about two-thirds of suppliers. No verified production deployment of two independently owned AI agents haggling with each other was found; that remains at experiment stage.

### Cited Findings
- Walmart/Pactum (older data, 2021–2023, flag as dated): the chatbot closed deals with 68% of suppliers approached with average savings of 3%; in the initial three-month Walmart Canada pilot with 89 suppliers it closed 64% in an average of 11 days with 1.5% average savings. 75% of suppliers reportedly preferred negotiating with the bot; 83% found it easy to use. Used mainly for contracts not worth human time, initially for goods not for resale such as shopping carts. Confidence: medium (self-reported by Walmart and Pactum; widely repeated). — [PYMNTS, 2023](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2023/walmart-finds-75-percent-vendors-prefer-negotiating-with-chatbot/partial/); [Bloomberg, 26 Apr 2023](https://brp-prod-bcc.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-26/walmart-uses-pactum-ai-tools-to-handle-vendor-negotiations); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/walmarts-suppliers-would-rather-negotiate-with-ai-than-a-human-162131831.html)
- Maersk deployed Pactum agents from 2021 to negotiate freight-lane contracts with carriers (older; no recent outcome data found). — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=440816); [Thunderbird case study](https://thunderbird.asu.edu/thought-leadership/journals-case-series/case-series-listing/pactums-ai-contract-negotiations)
- Ahold Delhaize USA is using Pactum for indirect-spend supplier negotiations (reported 22 Jul 2026), "a couple of months" in, with the retailer keeping full oversight of each negotiation; no quantified results published. Confidence: medium (vendor-adjacent trade press). — [CPOstrategy](https://cpostrategy.media/blog/2026/07/22/how-ahold-delhaize-usa-and-pactum-are-bringing-agentic-ai-into-supplier-negotiations/)
- McKinsey's February 2026 analysis of agentic procurement pilots reportedly found a chemicals company using agents for autonomous sourcing of consumables achieved a 20–30% efficiency improvement for procurement staff (efficiency, not price outcomes; relayed by O'Reilly). Confidence: medium. — [O'Reilly Radar](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/?p=19422)
- Two-sided agent negotiation is described only as a product pairing, with a seller-side packaging platform positioning itself as "something for [the buyer's] agent to talk to"; no measured deployment. Confidence: low (vendor blog). — [Packative](https://packative.com/en/blog/pactum-playbook-packaging-procurement)
- An independent review notes Pactum's actual scope is narrower than its marketing suggests, with agents operating inside defined parameters set by the client. — [Procurement AI agents review](https://procurement-ai-agents.pages.dev/blog/pactum-ai-review-autonomous-negotiation)

### Inferences
- The proven pattern is: large buyer, many small counterparties, standardised low-stakes terms, bot versus human, tight guardrails. Savings are modest in percentage terms; the main gain is covering negotiations nobody had time for.
- This supports the "premature" reading for open consumer/small-business agent-to-agent haggling: nobody has published production outcomes for it, while the structured B2B version has taken about five years to spread from Walmart to a handful of large enterprises.
- It also suggests where early value may lie: negotiations people skip today because they are not worth the time (small local services, bulk small orders), with owners approving the result.

### Gaps
- No audited or independent outcome data for any negotiation bot; all figures are vendor or customer self-reports, and the best-known figures are from 2021–2023.
- No evidence found of consumer-facing price-haggling agents in production (for example bill negotiation or marketplace haggling bots) with measured results in 2025–2026; not searched specifically.
- No data on B2B quote/RFQ agents on the supplier side.

## 5. What do credible analysts and academic studies say about agent-to-agent markets?

### Takeaway
Experiments agree that agent-run markets work mechanically but have systematic flaws: agents overwhelmingly accept the first offer they receive, can be manipulated, degrade when given many options, and stronger models extract better deals than weaker ones without the losing side noticing. Analyst forecasts for agentic commerce by 2030 are very large but differ by an order of magnitude because of definitions, and a visible sceptical strand has grown after the 2026 checkout retreat.

### Cited Findings

Academic and lab experiments
- Microsoft Research, "Magentic Marketplace" (arXiv 2510.25779, October 2025; open-source simulated two-sided market of consumer and business agents): first proposals were selected 60–100% of the time versus near zero for third proposals, a 10–30x advantage for response speed; GPT-4o and Claude Sonnet 4.5 reached 100% first-proposal selection in some conditions; even the least biased case (GPT-4.1, contractor scenario) picked first proposals 60% of the time versus 13.3% for third. The authors argue this shifts competition from quality or price to response latency. Search-result position bias was comparatively modest. Confidence: high (primary paper via search summary). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1); [Microsoft Research](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/magentic-marketplace-an-open-source-environment-for-studying-agentic-markets/)
- Same study, per press summary: six manipulation strategies were tested and agents were broadly susceptible, with only Claude Sonnet 4 resisting; agents' performance fell when presented with 100 search results (too many options). Confidence: medium (secondary summary, not checked against the paper). — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/copilot/articles/microsoft-gave-ai-agents-fake-000232651.html)
- Anthropic, "Project Deal" (published 24 Apr 2026; run December 2025): Claude agents represented 69 San Francisco employees as buyers and sellers of real personal goods over Slack, $100 budget each, no human intervention during negotiation; 186 deals across 500+ listings, just over $4,000 total value in the real run; median item price $12, mean $20.05. Confidence: high (primary, read in full). — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)
- Project Deal, model inequality: in mixed runs, participants with the stronger model (Opus 4.5) completed about 2.07 more deals each (p=0.001); the same item sold for $3.64 more when an Opus agent sold it than a Haiku 4.5 agent (p=0.011); Opus as seller +$2.68 per item, Opus as buyer −$2.45 (782 transactions); Opus seller versus Haiku buyer averaged $24.18 versus $18.63 for Opus-to-Opus. One broken folding bike sold for $38 via Haiku and $65 via Opus. Confidence: high. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)
- Project Deal, perception: perceived fairness was essentially identical (4.05 for Opus deals, 4.06 for Haiku, 1–7 scale) and satisfaction differences were not significant; the authors conclude people with weaker agents got worse deals but did not notice. Owner instructions (for example "be aggressive") had no significant effect on sale likelihood or price after adjustment; model quality mattered more than prompting. 46% of participants said they would pay for such a service. Confidence: high. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-says-stronger-ai-models-cut-better-deals-and-the-losers-dont-even-notice/)
- Project Deal caveats stated by the authors: self-selected employees, not a competitive or adversarial market, some agent messages contained confabulated details, set-up not how agents should be deployed in the real world. A legal commentary argues the legal frameworks for such markets do not exist. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal); [Legal IT Insider](https://legaltechnology.com/anthropics-ai-agent-to-agent-marketplace-experiment-the-legal-frameworks-dont-exist/)
- "The Automated but Risky Game: Modeling Agent-to-Agent Negotiations and Transactions in Consumer Markets" (arXiv, presented at ICML/NeurIPS 2025 venues; Stanford-affiliated): agent-to-agent deal-making in consumer settings is "an inherently imbalanced game"; buyers with weaker agents paid around 2% more and weaker seller agents lost up to 14% in profit; behavioural anomalies caused overspending and unreasonable deals; agents sometimes looped without agreement or ended talks early; larger models within a family consistently struck better deals. Simulated environment (limitation noted by an outside academic). Confidence: high on existence and direction, medium on exact figures (via Stanford HAI and MIT Technology Review coverage). — [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/the-art-of-the-automated-negotiation); [MIT Technology Review, 17 Jun 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/06/17/1118910/ai-price-negotiation/amp/); [NeurIPS listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/132565)

Analysts
- McKinsey (October 2025): agentic commerce could orchestrate $3–5 trillion of global retail revenue by 2030, with up to $1 trillion in US B2C; also flags identity, fraud and privacy risks. — [McKinsey](https://mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-agentic-commerce-opportunity-how-ai-agents-are-ushering-in-a-new-era-for-consumers-and-merchants); [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/10/20/mckinsey-forecast-5-trillion-agentic-commerce-sales-2030/amp/)
- Bain (December 2025): US agentic commerce of $300–500 billion by 2030, 15–25% of online retail. — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/ko/insights/2030-forecast-how-agentic-ai-will-reshape-us-retail-snap-chart/); [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/12/22/bain-agentic-ai-us-ecommerce-sales-2030/)
- Gartner (November 2025 press release, as relayed): by 2028, 90% of B2B buying will be mediated by AI agents, pushing over $15 trillion of B2B spend through AI agent exchanges; separately 20% of digital commerce transactions executed through AI platforms by 2030. Morgan Stanley (December 2025): 10–20% of US e-commerce by 2030, about $190 billion base case. The forecasts differ by a factor of about 35, mainly because "agentic" is defined differently. Confidence: medium (relayed via a secondary synthesis). — [Stellagent forecast comparison](https://stellagent.ai/insights/agentic-commerce-market-size-forecast-2030)
- Sceptical view: an industry analyst essay titled "Agentic commerce is a mirage" argues platform incentives work against third-party agents; trade coverage after March 2026 framed the Instant Checkout withdrawal as evidence that people do not want to shop inside a chatbot. Confidence: medium (opinion). — [Mobile Dev Memo](https://mobiledevmemo.com/agentic-commerce-is-a-mirage/); [Spree Commerce blog](https://spreecommerce.org/openai-just-blinked-nobody-seems-to-want-to-shop-inside-chatgpt/)

### Inferences
- First-proposal bias means an agent marketplace needs deliberate market design (batching offers, minimum comparison sets, randomised ordering), otherwise the fastest seller agent wins regardless of quality. This is a design requirement, not a nice-to-have.
- Model inequality is the most important fairness finding for a mass-market product: if richer users or bigger businesses run stronger agents, they systematically win small amounts on every deal and the weaker side does not perceive it. A marketplace that gives every participant the same agent quality (or a neutral platform agent) neutralises this, and could market fairness as a feature.
- Owner instructions mattering little while model quality matters a lot suggests owners' real control lever is approval and limits (confirm before commit), not prompt-tuning.
- Manipulation susceptibility and confabulated claims in negotiation messages imply that binding commitments should be made on structured data (price, quantity, terms) confirmed by owners, not on free-text agent chat.
- The gap between analyst forecasts (trillions by 2030) and 2026 operating evidence (tens of merchants on in-chat checkout, about a third the conversion, tiny real crypto-agent volume) is the core of the "premature" case; the forecasts mostly count AI-influenced discovery, which is real.

### Gaps
- No academic result on tacit collusion among LLM seller agents was retrieved in this pass; the search returned nothing on it, so collusion evidence should be treated as not covered rather than absent.
- No field study (as opposed to simulation or small internal pilot) of agent-to-agent trading among ordinary consumers or small businesses was found, and none in an Indian or other emerging-market context.
- Gartner and Morgan Stanley figures were not read at source; a16z, Sequoia and Financial Times/Reuters primary pieces were not retrieved (search surfaced trade press instead).
- Whether the Magentic Marketplace manipulation and option-overload results hold for late-2026 models is unknown; the study used models available in 2025.
