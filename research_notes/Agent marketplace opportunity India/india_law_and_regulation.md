# India law and regulation for AI agents that make offers, negotiate and take/place orders (as of 17 November 2026)

Scope note. These are research notes, not legal advice. Research was done on 17 Nov 2026 by web search plus reading of a few primary texts (E-Commerce Rules 2020 gazette text; a Sept 2026 law-firm note on the 2026 amendment; a Sept 2026 law-firm AI regulatory tracker; a 2026 journal article). Where a point rests on my background knowledge of the statute and was not re-checked against a source in this session, it is placed under "Inferences" or "Gaps" and marked "not re-verified". Confidence labels: HIGH = primary text or several consistent reputable sources; MEDIUM = reputable secondary sources only, or some inconsistency; LOW = single weak source / press report / my own reasoning. "LAWYER" marks points where a written opinion from Indian counsel is needed before launch.

Product assumed: consumer app; (a) seller-side agent answers the seller's customers and records an order only after the seller confirms; (b) buyer-side agent asks for quotes and places an order only after the buyer confirms; (c) agents may talk to other agents; the app hosts public chat pages; the app does not handle money.

## 1. Contract formation: is an offer/acceptance made by an automated agent binding on the principal?

### Takeaway
Indian law has no "electronic agent" rule like the US UETA, and no reported Indian case on a contract concluded by a bot or AI was found; but the general tools (IT Act s.10A and s.11(c), Contract Act agency rules on actual authority, ostensible authority s.237, ratification ss.196-199, and restitution s.70) all point the same way: the person who deploys the agent is very likely bound, and the counterparty is protected (confidence: MEDIUM - consistent commentary, no case law). "Owner confirms each commitment" moves the contract-forming act back to a human, which removes most of the attribution doubt, but it does not remove liability for what the agent said before confirmation (misrepresentation, misleading statements).

### Cited Findings
- IT Act s.10A (inserted by the 2008 amendment) says that where a contract is formed through electronic means, communication of proposals, acceptances and revocations "shall not be deemed to be unenforceable solely on the ground that such electronic form or means was used". It is a non-discrimination rule; it says nothing on who the parties are or how software output is attributed. (HIGH) - [IJIRL article, Oct 2026, quoting the section](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- IT Act s.11(c): an electronic record is attributed to the originator if sent "by an information system programmed by or on behalf of the originator to operate automatically". The article's author states this "has, so far as I can find, never been the subject of a reported decision" and is barely discussed in commentaries. (HIGH for text; MEDIUM for the "no reported decision" claim - one author's search) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- Limits of s.11(c) noted by the same author: it is a rule about records, not contracts (whose message it is, not whether it was a valid acceptance); and "programmed ... to operate automatically" fits rule-based automation better than a goal-driven autonomous LLM agent. (MEDIUM - academic opinion) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- Supreme Court in Trimex International FZE v. Vedanta Aluminium (2010) 3 SCC 1: a contract concluded by exchange of emails is a contract like any other; no formal signed document needed. Bhagwandas Kedia (AIR 1966 SC 543) shows Indian courts adapt offer/acceptance rules to new communication modes. (HIGH that these cases exist and are cited for this; they are not AI cases) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- India did not enact an "electronic agent" provision. Contrast: US UETA ss.2(6) and 14 (contract may be formed by interaction of electronic agents even if no individual was aware); UN Electronic Communications Convention art.12; Singapore Electronic Transactions Act 2010 s.15. These "deem the automated system's act to be the act of the person who deployed it". (MEDIUM-HIGH) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- Three doctrinal routes an Indian court could use, per the article: (1) agent as instrument - the user assented in advance at deployment (s.10A + s.11(c) + Contract Act s.13 consent); (2) agency by analogy - Contract Act s.182 defines an agent as "a person", and software is not a person, but the rules that bind a principal to third parties (ss.186-188 actual/implied authority, s.237 ostensible authority, ss.196-199 ratification) look at the principal's conduct, not the agent's personhood; s.184 says a person without capacity can still be an agent as between principal and third parties; (3) if attribution fails, s.70 restitution - the user pays the value of the benefit enjoyed but is not bound by the contract terms. (MEDIUM - academic, low-tier journal, but reasoning tracks the statute) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- The article predicts s.237 (ostensible authority) "will end up deciding most of these cases": a counterparty who receives an order from an agent running on the user's authenticated account is entitled to assume it is authorised, so the user is bound even to terms outside the instruction. Ratification by conduct (e.g. accepting delivery) binds the user to the whole transaction (s.199). (MEDIUM) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- Because software cannot be sued for breach of warranty of authority (s.235), when attribution fails the loss falls on "the party who trusted the machine". Overall the article concludes the counterparty is protected under every route and the deploying user carries the risk. (MEDIUM) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- A Jan 2026 law-firm note (Ahuja Law Offices), as reported in the article, says s.184 governs the agent's responsibility to the principal and does not by itself decide validity of acts towards third parties, so lack of personhood "does not automatically render every AI-executed transaction void". (LOW-MEDIUM - cited second-hand; original not opened) - [Ahuja Law Offices, 4 Jan 2026](https://ahujalawoffices.com/alo/2026/01/04/ai-driven-transactions-and-the-indian-law-of-contract-and-agency/)
- A 1 Sept 2026 LiveLaw (law-school section, student author) article reaches the same conclusion: an AI agent cannot contract in its own right; its conduct is attributable to the person who authorised it; ss.227-228 (acts exceeding authority: principal bound only for the separable authorised part) and ss.196-199 (ratification) are the relevant tools; proposed factors for courts: the principal's directions, foreseeability, safeguards in place, and the third party's knowledge of the agent's limits. It finds existing law workable for now but uncertain for agent-to-agent negotiation. (MEDIUM-LOW - student article) - [LiveLaw, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/lawof-agency-ai-agent-autonomous-ai-agent-bind-humanprincipal-547909)
- Data-protection twist: the article argues consent "given" by a user's software agent to a privacy notice probably does not meet DPDP Act s.6 (free, specific, informed, unambiguous, clear affirmative action by the data principal); the only statutory channel for intermediated consent is a registered Consent Manager (s.6(7); Rule 4, in force 13 Nov 2026). So a buyer-side agent can probably bind its user to a purchase but cannot validly consent to data processing for them. (MEDIUM-LOW - one author's argument; untested) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- Global - UNCITRAL Model Law on Automated Contracting: adopted 11 July 2024; covers AI and smart contracts; sets rules for attribution of "outputs" of automated systems and an optional rule on "unexpected" outputs. (HIGH) - [UNCITRAL](https://uncitral.un.org/en/node/6355)
- MLAC art.7: parties may agree their own attribution rule (7(1)); default is attribution to the person who uses the system for that purpose (7(2)); the system itself cannot be the bearer of attribution. (MEDIUM - commentary) - [Kluwer Arbitration Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/arbitration-blog/uncitral-model-law-on-automated-contracting-and-the-road-to-automated-and-autonomous-arbitration/)
- Global - Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149 (British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal, Feb 2024): airline ordered to pay CAD 812.02 after its website chatbot wrongly said a bereavement fare could be claimed retroactively; tribunal rejected the argument that the chatbot was a separate entity; the company is responsible for all information on its site; basis was negligent misrepresentation. (HIGH) - [Promise Legal summary](https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/copilot-committed-ad-ai-agent-liability-agency-law/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/lawof-agency-ai-agent-autonomous-ai-agent-bind-humanprincipal-547909)
- Global - US: commentators treat UETA s.14 (with s.9 attribution) and E-SIGN s.101(h) as the anchor for AI-agent contracts; the "electronic agent" definition is read as wide enough for generative AI. (MEDIUM) - [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/copilot-committed-ad-ai-agent-liability-agency-law/); [McDermott, "Can chatbot interactions lead to enforceable contracts", May 2024](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Can-Chatbot-Interactions-Lead-To-Enforceable-Contracts.pdf)
- Global - Amazon.com Services v. Perplexity AI (9th Cir., 4 Aug 2026), as reported: court said "AI is a tool, not a person" and treated the user, not the agent vendor, as the one accessing Amazon's computers. Case said to be ongoing. (LOW-MEDIUM - reported only via a student article; verify) - [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/lawof-agency-ai-agent-autonomous-ai-agent-bind-humanprincipal-547909)
- Global - Singapore Quoine v. B2C2 [2020] SGCA(I) 2: for contracts between deterministic trading algorithms, the relevant state of mind for mistake is the programmer's at the time of programming. (HIGH that the case holds this) - [IJIRL article](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)

### Inferences
- With "owner confirms each commitment", the binding act (seller's acceptance of an order, buyer's placing of an order) is a human click, so the app sits in ordinary e-contract territory (s.10A; Trimex). The agent's messages before that are best framed in the UI and terms as "invitation to treat / draft quote, subject to seller confirmation". This cuts the risk of the agent accidentally concluding a contract, but only if the agent's wording does not itself read as a firm acceptance ("your order is confirmed") before the human acts. Design point: the agent must never say "confirmed" until the owner's confirmation is recorded; keep a time-stamped log of who confirmed what (also useful for s.11 attribution and evidence). (MEDIUM)
- Confirmation does not cure pre-contract statements. If the seller's agent states a wrong price, stock, delivery date or return policy and the customer relies on it, the seller is exposed under misrepresentation (Contract Act ss.18-19, not re-verified) and consumer law (section 2 below), following the Moffatt logic. The seller confirming an order that embeds the agent's wrong statement likely amounts to adopting it (ratification). (MEDIUM)
- The platform's own exposure is contractual and consumer-law based rather than as a "party" to the sale: it should not be the seller or buyer. Terms should say the seller/buyer is the principal, the agent is the principal's tool, each principal is responsible for the agent's configuration, and the platform gives no warranty of authority. LAWYER: whether such allocation holds against a consumer (unfair contract terms under CPA 2019 s.2(46), not re-verified) and how much liability can be disclaimed.
- Agent-to-agent negotiation (c): both sides' outputs are attributable to their own principals under any of the routes; with human confirmation on both sides the legal picture is two humans contracting via drafts prepared by tools. Without confirmation, it is the least-settled area (the LiveLaw piece flags it expressly). Recommend keeping two-sided human confirmation in v1. (MEDIUM)
- The buyer-side agent should not click through third-party terms or privacy consents on the buyer's behalf; surface them to the buyer at confirmation. (MEDIUM, based on the DPDP consent argument above)

### Gaps
- No Indian reported judgment on bot- or AI-concluded contracts was found (two 2026 commentaries say the same). Any view is prediction. LAWYER.
- EU position not researched in this session. From background knowledge (not re-verified): the EU has no single "electronic agent" contract rule; formation is national law; the E-Commerce Directive requires that contracts can be concluded electronically; the AI Act art.50(1) requires that people be told they are interacting with an AI system unless obvious, applicable from 2 Aug 2026 (check whether the 2025-26 "digital omnibus" changed that date). Treat as LOW confidence until checked.
- Whether an Indian court would apply s.11(c) to a goal-driven LLM agent (versus a scripted bot) is open.
- Stamp duty / writing requirements for particular contract types were not researched (irrelevant for small goods orders; relevant if agents ever touch leases, guarantees etc.).

## 2. Consumer protection: CPA 2019, E-Commerce Rules 2020, the 2026 amendment, dark patterns, misleading AI statements

### Takeaway
The definition of "e-commerce entity" is wide ("any person who owns, operates or manages digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce") and a "marketplace e-commerce entity" is one that "provides an information technology platform ... to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers", so an app that hosts public order-taking chat pages for many sellers and a buyer agent that finds sellers has a real risk of being classed as a marketplace e-commerce entity even though it never touches money (confidence: MEDIUM; LAWYER - this is the single most important classification question). The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules 2026 were notified on 9 Sept 2026 and take effect 1 Jan 2027, adding mandatory National Consumer Helpline convergence, annual dark-pattern self-audit with a displayed certificate, seller ID display and more.

### Cited Findings
- Scope (Rule 2): the Rules apply to all goods and services bought or sold over a digital or electronic network, all models of e-commerce including marketplace and inventory, and all forms of unfair trade practices across all models; they do not apply to "any activity of a natural person carried out in a personal capacity not being part of any professional or commercial activity undertaken on a regular or systematic basis"; they apply to entities outside India that systematically offer goods/services to consumers in India. (HIGH) - [Gazette text of E-Commerce Rules 2020, G.S.R. 462(E), 23 July 2020](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)
- Definitions (Rule 3): "e-commerce entity" = "any person who owns, operates or manages digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce, but does not include a seller offering his goods or services for sale on a marketplace e-commerce entity"; "marketplace e-commerce entity" = one "which provides an information technology platform on a digital or electronic network to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers"; "inventory e-commerce entity" = one that "owns the inventory of goods or services and sells such goods or services directly to the consumers"; "platform" = "an online interface in the form of any software including a website or a part thereof and applications including mobile applications". (HIGH) - [Gazette text](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)
- Duties of every e-commerce entity (Rule 4, 2020 text): appoint a nodal person / senior functionary resident in India; display legal name, address, website, contact details of customer care and grievance officer; no unfair trade practice; grievance officer must acknowledge a complaint within 48 hours and redress within one month; no cancellation charges on consumers unless the entity bears similar charges; record consent to purchase only through "explicit and affirmative action" (no pre-ticked boxes); process refunds within a reasonable time; no price manipulation or discrimination between consumers of the same class. (HIGH) - [Gazette text](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)
- Marketplace duties (Rule 5): a marketplace seeking s.79 IT Act safe harbour must comply with s.79(2),(3) and the intermediary rules; must take an undertaking from sellers that descriptions/images are accurate; must display seller details, return/refund/exchange, warranty, delivery, payment modes, grievance mechanism, ranking parameters; must give seller information to a consumer on written request after purchase; keep records to identify repeat-offender sellers. Seller duties (Rule 6): no unfair trade practice, no fake reviews, no refusal of returns for defective/late goods, a prior written contract with the e-commerce entity, own grievance officer (48 hours / one month), and must supply legal name, address, GSTIN/PAN, contact details and product information for display. (HIGH) - [Gazette text](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)
- 2026 amendment: notified 9 Sept 2026 (G.S.R. 789(E) per search result), effective 1 Jan 2027; reportedly notified without a prior public draft. (HIGH on dates; MEDIUM on "no draft") - [Khaitan & Co Ergo, 18 Sept 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf); [Internet Freedom Foundation](https://internetfreedom.in/a-certificate-is-not-compliance-and-the-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-forget-that/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/consumer-cases/government-amends-e-commerce-rules-to-strengthen-consumer-protection-550184)
- 2026 amendment contents (per Khaitan): (i) "prior price" = lowest price in preceding 30 days must be shown with any discount claim; (ii) all e-commerce entities must comply with the Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023, do a yearly self-audit and prominently display a certificate (method, auditor and consequences of a wrong certificate not specified); (iii) "ranking" now also covers prominence given to sellers; ranking parameters must be disclosed prominently in descending order of importance; paid vs organic placement must be distinguished; manipulative search results prohibited; (iv) entity must display legal name, principal address, website, customer care and grievance officer details at an "appropriate" place; importer and country of origin for imports; searchable country-of-origin filter from 1 July 2027; (v) expanded product information: best-before/use-by date, return/refund/exchange, warranty, delivery, cost of return shipping, payment modes; reference to Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011 mandatory declarations; (vi) marketplaces must obtain and prominently display sellers' government ID number (GSTIN or MSME/Udyam number), website and email; seller's name on invoices in same font size; (vii) National Consumer Helpline convergence becomes mandatory (registration form, MoU with the Department, IT integration, nodal officer, timelines); (viii) grievance officer must give the complainant a copy of the recorded complaint; (ix) marketplaces may not use consumer information for common-brand sales or to promote an associated seller without express affirmative consent; (x) no bundled fees for unrelated services. Contraventions attract CPA 2019 penalties. (HIGH for the summary as a law-firm reading; the gazette text itself was not opened) - [Khaitan & Co Ergo, 18 Sept 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)
- The Khaitan note does not mention any change to the definition of "e-commerce entity" and no provision on AI, chatbots or agents. (MEDIUM - absence in a 4-page summary) - [Khaitan & Co Ergo](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)
- Dark Patterns Guidelines: notified 30 Nov 2023 under CPA 2019 s.18; 13 listed patterns (false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, forced action, subscription trap, interface interference, bait and switch, drip pricing, disguised advertisement, nagging, trick question, SaaS billing, rogue malware); apply to platforms, advertisers and sellers. June 2025 CCPA advisory asked platforms to self-audit (reported 90 days) and file declarations. Enforcement has started: penalties on PhysicsWallah and McAfee India reported in mid-2026 (McAfee Rs 1 lakh, May 2026). (MEDIUM-HIGH) - [Gala Law blog on CCPA dark-pattern orders](https://blog.galalaw.com/post/102nqtf/grey-in-the-dark-indian-ccpas-dark-pattern-jurisprudence); [The Legal Quorum](https://thelegalquorum.com/designed-to-deceive-from-soft-regulation-to-active-enforcement-against-dark-patterns/); [Khaitan Ergo](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)
- No CCPA order or advisory naming chatbots/AI assistants, and no reported consumer-commission decision holding a chatbot operator liable, was found in search. ORF commentary argues current consumer law targets deception in commerce and does not squarely reach persuasion/dependence harms from AI companions. (MEDIUM - negative search result) - [ORF, 2026](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/the-next-frontier-of-dark-patterns-regulating-ai-companions-in-india)
- The 2021 draft amendments to the E-Commerce Rules (flash sales, fall-back liability, registration with DPIIT etc.) were floated in June 2021 and raised compliance concerns; the 2026 amendment is a different, narrower instrument. (MEDIUM) - [Business Standard, June 2021](https://business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/new-rules-for-e-commerce-companies-likely-to-raise-compliance-burden-121062300032_1.html)

### Inferences
- Classification. Three possible characterisations of the app: (1) pure software/SaaS tool used by each seller on the seller's own "storefront" (then each seller is itself an inventory e-commerce entity for its own chat page, and the app is a vendor outside the Rules); (2) marketplace e-commerce entity (an IT platform that "facilitates transactions between buyers and sellers"); (3) both, depending on feature. Features that push towards (2): a shared consumer-facing brand/domain for all seller pages; a buyer agent that discovers, compares or ranks sellers; a directory or search across sellers; order records kept by the app; the app's agent "speaking" for many sellers. Features that push towards (1): seller-branded pages on seller-chosen links, no cross-seller discovery, no ranking, seller is clearly named as the contracting party, payment and delivery entirely off-platform. The buyer-side quote-and-order agent (b) makes characterisation (2) hard to avoid. Not handling payments does not take a platform out of the definition - the definition has no payment element. (MEDIUM; LAWYER)
- Practical consequence if (2): from day one the app needs: displayed legal name/address/contacts; a grievance officer with 48-hour acknowledgement and one-month resolution; a nodal person resident in India; seller onboarding that collects and displays legal name, address, GSTIN or Udyam number, contact and grievance contact; seller undertaking on accuracy; explicit affirmative consent for each purchase (the "confirm" step fits well); and from 1 Jan 2027: NCH convergence, annual dark-pattern self-audit certificate, ranking-parameter disclosure and paid/organic separation (critical if the buyer agent recommends sellers), prior-price rule for discount claims made by a seller agent, copy of complaint to complainant. Many micro-sellers have no GSTIN; the 2026 text reportedly accepts an MSME (Udyam) number as an alternative. (MEDIUM)
- Very small sellers: the natural-person exclusion in Rule 2 only covers activity not done on a "regular or systematic basis"; a home baker selling every week is likely inside the Rules as a seller. (MEDIUM)
- Misleading statements by the AI: under CPA 2019 a false statement about price, quality, availability or terms is an "unfair trade practice"/"misleading advertisement" regardless of whether a human or software made it (statute not re-verified this session; consistent with Moffatt reasoning). Primary exposure sits with the seller whose agent spoke; the platform is exposed if it is an e-commerce entity that "adopts" the practice on its platform (Rule 4 bars unfair trade practices "in the course of business on its platform or otherwise") or if the error stems from the platform's model rather than the seller's data. Safeguards: ground agent answers only in seller-supplied catalogue/policy data; refuse to state prices/stock not in the data; no invented discounts, urgency or scarcity (false urgency is a listed dark pattern); show a final human-confirmed order summary with price, delivery charges and return terms (drip pricing is a listed dark pattern). (MEDIUM)
- A buyer agent that ranks sellers must be able to explain its main ranking parameters and must mark any paid placement; hidden paid preference would be both "disguised advertisement" and a breach of the 2026 ranking rule. (MEDIUM)
- CPA 2019 product liability (Chapter VI) can reach "product sellers" and e-commerce marketplaces in limited cases; exposure rises if the platform exercises control over the goods or vouches for them (not re-verified; LAWYER).

### Gaps
- No authoritative ruling or regulator FAQ was found on whether a provider of chat/agent software to sellers is itself an "e-commerce entity". LAWYER - ask for an opinion on characterisation under Rule 3(1)(b),(g) for each of features (a), (b), (c), and on ONDC-style "buyer app/seller app" precedents.
- Gazette text of the 2026 amendment (G.S.R. number, exact new rule numbers) was not opened; rule-number citations should be checked before quoting.
- No India-specific consumer rule requiring disclosure that a customer is talking to an AI was found (see section 8).
- BIS voluntary standard on e-commerce self-governance and the 2022 fake-reviews standard (IS 19000) were not researched.

## 3. Intermediary liability: IT Act s.79, IT Rules 2021 as amended to 2026, Digital India Act status

### Takeaway
A service that hosts public pages and chats where users (sellers, buyers) post content is an "intermediary" and keeps s.79 safe harbour for third-party content only if it follows the IT Rules 2021 due diligence; since 20 Feb 2026 those rules require removal within 3 hours of a government/court order (2 hours for certain sensitive content) and labelling of synthetic audio/visual content (confidence: HIGH on the rules; MEDIUM on how they apply to this app). The AI agent's own generated text is the hard part: safe harbour covers third-party information, and whether a platform's own LLM output is "third-party" is unresolved in India (LAWYER). The Digital India Act has still not been introduced.

### Cited Findings
- The IT Rules 2021 "prescribe due diligence obligations required to be complied with by intermediaries in order to avail safe harbour from liability arising from third-party information, data or communication links hosted, stored or transmitted through their platforms". (HIGH) - [AZB India AI Regulatory Tracker, 2nd ed., Sept 2026](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)
- IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules 2026: notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026 (an earlier draft was released 22 Oct 2025). They define "synthetically generated information" (Rule 2(1)(wa)) as audio, visual or audio-visual information that is artificially or algorithmically created or altered and appears authentic / indistinguishable from a real person or event; create a due-diligence framework (Rule 3(3)) for intermediaries whose computer resources enable creation or sharing of SGI; require reasonable technical measures against unlawful SGI and prominent labelling through metadata/identifiers; add user-declaration and verification duties for significant social media intermediaries; cut takedown time to 3 hours for government/court directions and 2 hours for certain sensitive content (deepfakes, non-consensual intimate imagery); non-compliance may cost safe harbour. (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/law-policy/centre-amends-it-rules-to-regulate-ai-generated-content-takedown-timelines-reduced)
- Previous timelines were 36 hours for takedown orders and 72 hours for certain grievance resolution; grievance resolution reportedly moved from 72 to 36 hours in the 2026 amendment. Removing synthetic content in compliance with the rules is stated not to breach s.79(2) conditions. (MEDIUM - press/NGO summary) - [Sabrang India](https://sabrangindia.in/synthetic-content-three-hour-compliance-and-the-risk-of-over-removal-analysing-the-it-rules-amendments/); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/law-policy/centre-amends-it-rules-to-regulate-ai-generated-content-takedown-timelines-reduced)
- Text-only AI output falls outside the SGI definition (which covers audio, visual, audio-visual). (MEDIUM - secondary source, consistent with the definition quoted by AZB) - [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/)

(HIGH on existence; not in force as of Sept 2026) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf); [Mondaq, MeitY's emerging framework for AI-generated content](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/new-technology/1779806/understanding-meitys-emerging-framework-for-ai-generated-content-in-india)

Status: advisory. (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)

(MEDIUM) - [Chambers AI 2026 India](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/india/trends-and-developments)

(MEDIUM-HIGH) - [Chambers AI 2026 India](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/india); [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/india-ai-governance-guidelines); [JD Supra / White & Case AI Watch India](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ai-watch-global-regulatory-tracker-4119458/)

### Inferences
- Baseline duties for an app that hosts public user pages/chats, from the IT Rules 2021 Rule 3 (structure from background knowledge, not re-verified line by line): publish terms, privacy policy and rules telling users not to post listed categories of unlawful content, and remind users periodically; act on court/government orders within the (now 3-hour) window; name a Grievance Officer with contact details, acknowledge complaints within 24 hours and resolve within 15 days (shorter windows for some categories - check current text after the 2026 amendment); retain removed content and registration data for 180 days; report incidents to CERT-In; give information to lawful agencies within 72 hours. The heavier "significant social media intermediary" duties (resident Chief Compliance Officer, nodal person, monthly reports) apply only above 50 lakh registered users in India and only to social media intermediaries. A commerce-chat app is arguably not a "social media intermediary" at all, but public pages with user interaction blur this. (MEDIUM; LAWYER on classification and on current complaint timelines)
- A 3-hour takedown window means a 24x7 monitored legal-order inbox and a kill switch for any public page from launch, even at small scale.
- The agent's generated replies: when the seller's agent speaks, the content is produced by the platform's system on the seller's instructions. Safe harbour protects only third-party information and is lost if the intermediary "initiates the transmission" or "selects or modifies" the information (s.79(2), not re-verified). Courts have denied safe harbour to "active" e-commerce platforms (Christian Louboutin v. Nakul Bajaj, Delhi HC 2018 - not re-verified). Assume no safe harbour for agent-generated statements and manage that risk by grounding, logging and seller sign-off rather than relying on s.79. (MEDIUM; LAWYER)
- The synthetic-content labelling rule bites if agents generate images, audio or voice (e.g. AI product photos, voice notes, AI voice calls). Text chat is outside the SGI definition, but the 2024 advisory still points to telling users the output is AI-generated and fallible. If product images are AI-generated or AI-edited to look real, label them and embed metadata. (MEDIUM)

## 4. Data protection: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified on 13/14 Nov 2025 with three stages: Board and definitions immediately; Consent Manager registration from 13/14 Nov 2026 (i.e. just commenced); and the main duties on data fiduciaries (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, children's data, rights) from 13/14 May 2027 (confidence: HIGH on the staging; MEDIUM that no acceleration has been notified). For a customer's name, address, phone and order details collected by a seller's agent, the seller is most naturally the data fiduciary and the app its data processor, but the app is itself a fiduciary for its own account data and for anything it decides to do with customer data (training, analytics, cross-seller profiles, buyer-side agent memory) (confidence: MEDIUM; LAWYER).

### Cited Findings
- Rules notified November 2025 (sources say 13 or 14 Nov 2025 - gazette dated 13 Nov, widely reported 14 Nov). Stage 1 (immediate): commencement provisions, definitions, Data Protection Board set-up. Stage 2 (12 months; 13/14 Nov 2026): Consent Manager registration framework (Rule 4). Stage 3 (18 months; 13/14 May 2027): substantive obligations and full enforcement/penalties. (HIGH) - [AZB update, Nov 2025](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/); [SCC Online, 26 Dec 2025](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-compliance-timeline-enforcement-2026-27-44740.html/)
- November 2026 "is not a universal compliance deadline for every Data Fiduciary"; it is the consent-manager milestone. (MEDIUM-HIGH) - [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-act-rules-2026-compliance-deadline/)
- Proposed acceleration: MeitY consulted on 22 Jan 2026 on cutting the 18-month window to 12 months (which would have made core duties bite in Nov 2026), bringing Rules 15 and 23 into effect immediately and Rule 8(3) within three months; feedback was due 4 Feb 2026; officials said no final decision; an April 2026 article still called it a plan. (HIGH that it was proposed; status of notification unconfirmed) - [S.S. Rana, Apr 2026](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm); [Takshashila, 26 Jan 2026](https://takshashila.org.in/content/blogs/20260126-meity-considers-reducing-dpdpa-compliance-timelines.html)
- As late as 18 Sept 2026 a major firm wrote that the DPDPA "comes into effect from May 2027", implying no acceleration had been notified by then. The same proposal included notifying cross-border restrictions for significant data fiduciaries. (MEDIUM) - [Khaitan Ergo, 18 Sept 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)
- Roles: a Data Fiduciary determines the purpose and means of processing; a Data Processor processes on the fiduciary's behalf; the fiduciary stays accountable even when processing is outsourced to a processor, cloud or technology vendor; the Act expects a valid contract with processors (s.8(2)) and the Rules require security provisions in fiduciary-processor contracts; a processor has no direct statutory duties but will face contractual ones. (HIGH) - [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-compliance-gdpr-comparison-45702.html); [Trilegal DPDPA note, Nov 2025](https://trilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/DPDPA.pdf); [Bar & Bench viewpoint](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/the-1-trillion-digital-ambition-hits-its-regulatory-clock-managing-enterprise-risk-under-indias-dpdp-regime)
- Consent standard (s.6): "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action", for the specified purpose, after a s.5 notice; consent may be managed through a registered Consent Manager (s.6(7)), which must be an Indian company meeting net-worth conditions (Rule 4, First Schedule). (HIGH) - [IJIRL article quoting the Act](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)
- E-commerce entities are treated as data fiduciaries, and consent for secondary uses of consumer data under the 2026 E-Commerce amendment must also meet the DPDP standard. (MEDIUM-HIGH) - [Khaitan Ergo](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)
- WhatsApp chatbots: 2026 Indian commentary treats opt-in for WhatsApp business bots as needing DPDP-grade consent and notice. (MEDIUM) - [SCC Online, 29 July 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/29/whatsapp-chatbot-opt-in-consent-dpdp-act-compliance/)

### Inferences
- Role map for the three features (MEDIUM; LAWYER):
  - (a) Seller agent takes a customer's name, address, phone, order: seller = data fiduciary (it decides why the data is collected: to fulfil the order); app = data processor under a written data-processing agreement. But micro-sellers cannot realistically write notices or handle rights requests, so the app must supply these as product features (standard notice shown in chat before details are collected, deletion and correction tools, retention limits, breach process) and take the processor obligations by contract. If the app uses the same data for its own purposes (model training, cross-seller customer profiles, marketing, fraud scoring), it becomes a fiduciary for that use and needs its own notice and lawful basis.
  - (b) Buyer agent: the app is a data fiduciary for the buyer's profile, addresses, preferences and chat history; when it passes buyer details to a seller, the seller becomes a separate fiduciary for the order.
  - (c) Agent-to-agent: every transfer of personal data between agents is a disclosure between fiduciaries; send the minimum needed (data minimisation is implied by purpose limitation).
- Lawful basis. Where a customer voluntarily gives name/address/phone to get an order delivered, DPDP s.7(a) ("legitimate use" for the specified purpose for which the data principal voluntarily provided the data, without having indicated non-consent) likely covers fulfilment without a formal consent flow; anything beyond fulfilment (marketing messages, reuse, training) needs s.6 consent with an itemised notice in plain language and available in English or the scheduled Indian languages. (MEDIUM - s.7(a) and the language requirement are from background knowledge, not re-verified)
- Other core duties from May 2027 (from background knowledge of the Act/Rules; not re-verified this session; treat as MEDIUM): reasonable security safeguards (encryption, access control, logs kept for one year); breach notification to the Board and to every affected individual without delay, with a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours; erase data when the purpose is served or consent withdrawn; publish contact of a person who answers data questions; grievance redress before a complaint can go to the Board; verifiable parental consent before processing data of anyone under 18, and no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising at children; cross-border transfer allowed except to countries the Government restricts (negative list; none confirmed as notified), with extra localisation possible for Significant Data Fiduciaries; penalties up to Rs 250 crore for security failures. An early-stage app is very unlikely to be designated a Significant Data Fiduciary (designation is by Government notification based on volume/sensitivity and risk).
- Children: a public chat page cannot know a customer's age. Practical safeguard: sellers' pages are for general commerce; ask the user to confirm they are 18+ at first contact or before collecting personal details; block categories aimed at children; do not profile. LAWYER on what age assurance is "verifiable" enough.
- LLM providers hosted abroad are sub-processors; sending chat content to them is a cross-border transfer that is currently permitted but should be disclosed in the notice and covered by contract. Hosting in India reduces exposure to any future restricted-country notification. (MEDIUM)
- Until May 2027 the older regime - IT Act s.43A and the 2011 Reasonable Security Practices (SPDI) Rules - still applies to "sensitive personal data" (passwords, financial information such as bank/UPI details, health data), with a duty of reasonable security and compensation for negligence (not re-verified).

### Gaps
- Whether MeitY notified the 12-month acceleration (or any other amendment to the DPDP Rules) between May and Nov 2026 could not be confirmed either way; check the MeitY/e-Gazette site. If it was notified, core duties may already apply now. HIGH-priority check.
- Exact Rule numbers for notice content, breach timelines and children's consent mechanics were not re-read this session.

## 5. Payments: RBI and NPCI

### Takeaway
RBI's Payment Aggregator Directions 2025 (15 Sept 2025) regulate entities that receive, pool and settle customers' money for merchants; an app that only shows the seller's own UPI ID/QR or opens a UPI intent link so that the buyer pays the seller directly from the buyer's own UPI app never holds or routes funds and so should fall outside the PA definition (confidence: MEDIUM - this is the standard reading, but no RBI FAQ confirming this exact pattern was found; LAWYER). NPCI stopped person-to-person "collect" requests from 1 Oct 2025; there is no RBI/NPCI rule yet for AI-agent-initiated payments, though NPCI is reported (July 2026) to be building a "Unified Agent Protocol".

### Cited Findings
- RBI issued the Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025 on 15 Sept 2025, effective immediately, consolidating rules for online, physical (offline) and cross-border PAs. Non-bank PAs must hold RBI authorisation; those without it had to apply by 31 Dec 2025 or wind down by 28 Feb 2026; banks need no separate authorisation. Escrow-account management is a core focus; net-worth thresholds reported at Rs 15 crore at application rising to Rs 25 crore. (HIGH on the instrument; MEDIUM on net-worth figures) - [RBI press release](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/rbi-issues-the-reserve-bank-of-india-regulation-of-payment-aggregators-directions-2025); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/rbi-issues-new-master-directions-for-payment-aggregators-rules-take-effect-immediately); [Vinod Kothari highlights](https://vinodkothari.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Key-Highlights-of-PA-2025-2.pdf); [AZB, 17 Sept 2025](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87411)
- NPCI circular dated 29 July 2025: "By October 1, 2025, UPI P2P Collect shall not be allowed to be processed in UPI"; banks and apps must not initiate, route or process P2P collect. Reason: fraud through deceptive pull requests. Merchant (P2M) collect requests remain operational. P2P collect had earlier been capped at about Rs 2,000 per transaction. (HIGH) - [Business Today, 13 Aug 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/banking/story/npci-to-tighten-upi-rules-peer-to-peer-collect-feature-to-end-in-october-489244-2025-08-13); [The Week / PTI, 14 Aug 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/14/dcm65-biz-npci-collect.html); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/npci-to-end-upi-p2p-collect-requests-from-october-1-to-reduce-fraud)
- NPCI operational circular OC 201 B (2025) enables delegated UPI payments from IoT devices and software and requires adherence to RBI turnaround-time and failed-transaction compensation rules. (MEDIUM - law-firm summary; circular not opened) - [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1709076/enabling-delegated-payments-on-upi-rails-implications-of-iot-and-software-integration-for-upi-payments-under-npci-oc-201-b)
- Agentic payments: per a Business Standard report relayed on 9 July 2026, NPCI is developing a Unified Agent Protocol in consultation with industry so AI agents can be registered, verified and authorised to transact on UPI; it would verify the agent's authority, define its limits and set accountability; low-value repetitive purchases expected first; chargebacks/dispute resolution "will need to evolve"; no launch date or limits stated. (MEDIUM - press report, not an NPCI circular) - [Outlook Business, 9 July 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/india-plans-ai-powered-upi-payments-framework-through-unified-agent-protocol)
- NPCI's chief has publicly called for a regulatory framework for fintech AI systems (May 2026). Other reported items: Razorpay/NPCI agentic UPI pilots with AI assistants (announced from Oct 2025 onward), Pine Labs "P3P" agentic protocol (June 2026); a reported cap of Rs 15,000 for agent payments is unconfirmed. (LOW-MEDIUM - press, search snippets only) - [Business Standard, 29 May 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/npci-chief-dilip-asbe-bats-for-regulatory-framework-for-fintech-ai-systems-126052901413_1.html); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/india-eyes-ai-driven-upi-payments-as-npci-weighs-agent-protocol/)
- RBI FREE-AI report (13 Aug 2025) sets out expectations for responsible AI in the financial sector (guidance for regulated entities). (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)
- No RBI circular specific to AI-agent-initiated payments was found. (MEDIUM - negative search result) - [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/india-plans-ai-powered-upi-payments-framework-through-unified-agent-protocol)

### Inferences
- What would trigger licensing if the app ever touched money: collecting buyers' payments into its own account and settling to sellers = payment aggregator (authorisation, net worth, escrow with a scheduled bank, merchant KYC, dispute handling); storing value in a wallet = prepaid payment instrument (PPI Master Directions); holding funds until delivery ("escrow" feature) = PA activity or needs a bank/PA partner; building a UPI payer app = needs NPCI TPAP approval via a sponsor bank. Staying out of the funds flow avoids all of these. (MEDIUM-HIGH on structure; from background knowledge)
- Safe pattern: show the seller's own UPI ID/QR, or a `upi://pay` intent link with the seller as payee, so the buyer's own UPI app does the payment and money moves buyer-bank to seller-bank. The app only passes text. Risks to manage: (1) do not generate "collect"/pull requests - P2P collect is banned and pull requests are a known fraud pattern; (2) a seller using a personal (P2P) VPA for business receipts is the seller's issue with its bank/NPCI, but the app should encourage merchant (P2M) QR/VPAs; (3) QR/UPI-ID substitution fraud - the agent must only ever show the UPI ID the verified seller entered, never one supplied in chat, never one generated or "remembered" by the model; lock it behind seller re-authentication; show the bank-registered payee name to the buyer; (4) the agent must not claim "payment received" unless the seller confirms - the app cannot see settlement; (5) never ask for UPI PIN, OTP or card data in chat. (MEDIUM; LAWYER to confirm the PA analysis and whether NPCI limits use of intent links/dynamic QR by non-TPAP, non-PA apps)
- If a paid subscription is charged to sellers, that is the app's own receivable collected through a licensed PA - no licence issue.
- Agent-initiated payment is not available to an unlicensed app today; plan for "human pays in own UPI app" and revisit when NPCI's protocol is published.

- No RBI FAQ or circular was found that says in terms "displaying a merchant's own QR/UPI ID is not payment aggregation"; the conclusion is by reading the PA definition (entity that receives and settles funds). LAWYER.
- NPCI rules on who may generate UPI intent links / dynamic QR for P2M versus P2P payees, and current P2P/P2M transaction limits, were not verified.
- Text of OC 201 B, UPI Circle (delegated payments) limits, and any 2026 NPCI circular on agent protocol not opened.
- RBI digital-payment authentication directions effective April 2026 (two-factor rules) not researched.

## 6. Messaging and telecom: TRAI/TCCCPR, AI voice calls, WhatsApp Business policy

(MEDIUM; structure from background knowledge)

## 7. Sector limits: categories an agent must not sell or must handle carefully

### Takeaway
Several everyday categories carry licence or disclosure duties that a seller's agent cannot waive and that a platform should gate at seller onboarding: medicines (no notified e-pharmacy rules; sales only by licensed chemists against prescription; court and regulator action against unlicensed online sale), food (seller's FSSAI licence/registration; best-before dates), packaged goods (legal metrology declarations), alcohol and tobacco (state law; mostly not sellable online), and financial products (regulated advice/distribution) (confidence: MEDIUM overall; only e-pharmacy and the e-commerce-rule cross-references were checked this session).

### Cited Findings
- E-pharmacy: India has no enacted e-pharmacy-specific regulation; the 2018 draft rules (registration of e-pharmacy portals, no narcotic/psychotropic sales) remain unnotified as of mid-2026; online sellers operate under state retail drug licences; the DCGI has cited a Dec 2018 Delhi High Court order against online sale without licence and sent show-cause notices to online pharmacies (Feb 2023); chemists held a nationwide strike on 20 May 2026 demanding tighter rules. (MEDIUM-HIGH) - [Vajiram & Ravi explainer](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/e-pharmacies-on-regulating-online-sale-of-drugs-in-india/); [Business Today, 20 May 2026](https://businesstoday.in/industry/pharma/story/chemists-protest-online-medicine-sales-seek-tighter-regulation-of-e-pharmacies-532550-2026-05-20); [Business Standard, Feb 2023](https://bsppd.business-standard.com/article/companies/india-s-drug-regulator-serves-show-cause-notice-to-online-pharmacies-123021000651_1.html)

(HIGH as a law-firm reading) - [Khaitan Ergo, 18 Sept 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)

(HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested category policy for agents (from background knowledge of Indian law; not re-verified this session; LAWYER to confirm a prohibited/restricted list):
  - Block: prescription and Schedule H/H1/X drugs, narcotics/psychotropics; alcohol (state excise laws; only a few states have permitted licensed home delivery); tobacco, e-cigarettes/vapes (banned outright by the 2019 Act); weapons; wildlife products; lottery/real-money gaming (the 2025 online gaming law bans online money games); adult services; counterfeit goods; hazardous chemicals, firecrackers; investment, insurance, loan and deposit products (need SEBI/IRDAI/RBI registration; an agent "recommending" them risks unregistered advice); legal and medical advice services.
  - Allow with gating: food (collect the seller's FSSAI licence/registration number and display it; home kitchens need at least basic FSSAI registration; show best-before info for packaged food; no health claims); cosmetics and Ayurvedic products (licence numbers; no cure claims - Drugs and Magic Remedies Act); packaged commodities (MRP, net quantity, manufacturer/packer/importer, country of origin, consumer-care contact); electronics/toys/helmets etc. under BIS compulsory certification; second-hand goods; services such as salons, tutoring, repairs (few licences but ordinary consumer law applies).
  - The agent must never state medical, safety or legal claims that are not in the seller's approved product data, and must never sell above MRP for packaged goods.
- GST: a platform that is an "electronic commerce operator" collecting consideration has TCS duties, and unregistered sellers face limits on selling through operators; since the app does not collect consideration, TCS under CGST s.52 should not arise, but this needs a tax adviser's confirmation. (LOW-MEDIUM; LAWYER/CA)

## 8. AI-specific governance in India; duty to disclose that the customer is talking to an AI

### Takeaway
India has no AI Act; the India AI Governance Guidelines (5 Nov 2025; presented again at the Feb 2026 AI Impact Summit) are voluntary and rely on existing laws and sector regulators, and the only binding AI-specific rules are the Feb 2026 IT Rules on synthetic audio/visual content (confidence: HIGH). No binding Indian rule was found that requires a text chatbot to say "you are talking to an AI", but the March 2024 MeitY advisory, the consumer-law ban on misleading practices, global norms (EU AI Act) and the 2026 draft making advisories part of due diligence all point to disclosing it clearly and always (confidence: MEDIUM).

### Cited Findings
- India AI Governance Guidelines released 5 Nov 2025 under the IndiaAI Mission (MeitY): principle-based; seven "sutras" - Trust, People First, Innovation over Restraint, Fairness and Equity, Accountability, Understandable by Design, Safety/Resilience/Sustainability - adapted from the RBI FREE-AI report; a mix of institutional design, voluntary measures and techno-legal tools; status "advisory". (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf); [PIB document, Feb 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/feb/doc2026215790801.pdf)
- The framework leverages existing statutes and sector regulators rather than a new all-encompassing AI law; the guidelines are voluntary - "not a law, and not a regulator with penalty power". (HIGH) - [JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/?p=55514); [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/india-ai-governance-guidelines)
- MeitY advisory of 15 Mar 2024 (revised from 1 Mar 2024): label "the possible inherent fallibility" of AI output and use a consent mechanism that "explicitly informs users that the content is derived from AI"; advisory, not a rule. (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)
- Binding AI content rule: IT Rules amendment in force 20 Feb 2026 on synthetically generated audio/visual information (labelling, metadata, technical measures). Draft Second Amendment (Mar/Apr 2026) would require continuous visible labels and would make MeitY advisories/SOPs part of safe-harbour due diligence. (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)
- Search found disclosure rules for AI-generated content but no rule requiring chatbots to tell users they are talking to an AI; IAPP notes India has been "weighing AI legislation" and signalling shifts in 2026, without an enacted law. (MEDIUM - negative result) - [IAPP, India weighs AI legislation](https://iapp.org/news/a/notes-from-the-asia-pacific-region-india-weighs-ai-legislation); [IAPP, India signals shift on AI regulation](https://iapp.org/news/a/notes-from-the-asia-pacific-region-india-signals-shift-on-ai-regulation)
- Sector regulators: SEBI proposes disclosure to clients when AI/ML is used in advisory or management services; CCI (Mar 2026) advises AI self-audits and transparency in AI-driven decisions; CERT-In issued 2026 AI security advisories and AI bill-of-materials guidance. (HIGH) - [AZB tracker](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)
- Liability: no AI-specific liability statute; commentary discusses whether s.79 immunity should apply to systems that generate or modify content rather than merely transmit it; unresolved. (MEDIUM) - [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/india-ai-governance-guidelines); [Chambers AI 2026 India](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/india/trends-and-developments)

### Inferences
- Disclose AI status as a hard product rule, even though not yet strictly mandated: (1) a visible "AI assistant for [seller name]" label on every public chat page and at the start of each conversation; (2) the agent must truthfully say it is an AI when asked and must never claim to be a human or the owner; (3) a line that answers may contain errors and that the order is final only when the seller confirms; (4) an easy route to a human (the seller) and to the grievance officer. Reasons: the 2024 advisory; letting a consumer believe they are dealing with a human when it affects their decision is arguably a misleading practice / "disguised" design under consumer law; agent-to-agent chats should also carry a machine-readable "I am an automated agent acting for X" marker so each side knows authority limits (this supports the s.237 analysis in section 1 - disclosed limits of authority reduce ostensible-authority exposure). (MEDIUM)
- Accountability under the guidelines' "sutras" maps to: logs of agent decisions, human confirmation for commitments, clear naming of the responsible principal, grievance redress. Building these now aligns with where binding rules are likely to go. (MEDIUM)
- Summary of safeguards implied across all sections (for the decision document): human confirmation on both sides before any commitment; agent never says "confirmed/paid" on its own; grounded answers only from seller data; AI label; seller identity and KYC-lite (name, address, GSTIN/Udyam or FSSAI where relevant); category blocklist; grievance officer with 48h/1-month (consumer) and IT-Rules timelines; 24x7 takedown channel; DPDP notice in chat before personal details, processor agreement with sellers, deletion tools, breach plan; UPI ID locked to verified seller, no collect requests, no handling of funds; customer-initiated messaging only; logs retained (IT Rules 180 days; DPDP one year for logs) and available as evidence.
