# Business model, pricing, unit economics and funding for a consumer AI assistant in India (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: Boop is an open-source, Android-first assistant for everyday errands. Today users bring their own key (e.g. OpenRouter). The plan is to sell prepaid credit packs (possibly per-user capped OpenRouter keys), add optional cloud runs billed per minute later, and build a company around it. Research date: 6 Nov 2026. Prices carry the date seen. Estimates are labelled **ESTIMATE** with their assumptions. Exchange rate used: **₹94.54 per US$** (open.er-api.com, updated 6 Nov 2026 00:02 UTC — [source](https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/USD)). Telecom bundles and distribution are covered in more depth in the sibling note `distribution.md`. This note covers only what bears on willingness to pay.

## 1. Willingness to pay: what Indians pay for AI and apps

### Takeaway
₹399 a month (GST included) is now the anchor price for "premium consumer AI" in India: ChatGPT Go and Google AI Plus both sit there, and Google also offers an ₹199 introductory price. Most heavy users have had a year or more of premium AI for free through OpenAI's own promotion or the Jio and Airtel telecom bundles. Indian app spending is growing fast (Sensor Tower: +35% YoY to a record $345M in Q2 2026), but each payer is worth far less than in the West (RevenueCat: about $14 year-one value per payer in India and Southeast Asia, against $32 in North America). Small-ticket pricing, ₹1 trials linked to UPI Autopay, and microtransactions (Pocket FM, Kuku FM, STAGE) are how Indian consumer apps actually get people to pay. UPI Autopay volumes are huge and still growing, but many mandates fail or are revoked because accounts run short of money.

**Payment costs that bite on small tickets**
- Razorpay standard pricing (page seen 6 Nov 2026): UPI is MDR-free by RBI policy, but Razorpay charges a **2% platform fee** on UPI, cards, netbanking and wallets, plus 18% GST on the fee. Recurring card payments cost 0.9% plus the platform fee. UPI recurring (Autopay) is priced "on request". — [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)
- Google Play billing in India (latest confirmed status, 2023–24): a 15% fee on the first $1M of annual revenue, a 15% standard fee on subscriptions, and 30% above $1M for one-off digital goods. User-choice billing is available to all India developers from 26 Apr 2023 at 4 points less (11%/26%). The CCI found UCB *prima facie* anti-competitive and opened an inquiry. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/cci-dismisses-pleas-of-indian-app-cos-against-googles-new-play-store-billing-policy); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/googles-10-30-service-fee-on-app-purchase-hindering-app-developers-cci-tells-nclat/)

### Inferences
- **The price ceiling is ₹399/month, and many users are used to ₹0.** Boop is an errand tool, not a chat tool, so it should not compete head-on at ₹399. Packs of **₹49–₹199** fit under the anchor. A **₹9–₹29 "try it" pack** (or a ₹1 trial) matches how Indians start paying for apps.
- **Prepaid packs suit India better than subscriptions at first.** Autopay mandates are cheap to set up, but they fail or get revoked when balances are low, and RBI is watching unintended debits. A prepaid pack bought by one-off UPI payment avoids failed collections, chargeback-style complaints and the "₹199 fear". A subscription (Autopay) can come later for loyal users.
- **The ChatGPT Go renewal wave (Nov 2026 onward) is a live test** of Indian willingness to pay ₹399 for AI. How many renew will be a strong signal. No data is public yet.
- **Fees on a ₹99 pack (ESTIMATE, my arithmetic, assuming GST is included in the price):** net of 18% GST = ₹83.90. After Razorpay's 2% + GST: ₹81.56. If sold through Google Play at 15%: ₹71.31. Through user-choice billing at 11% plus a 2% gateway: ₹72.33. Selling outside Play (website, UPI link, F-Droid build) keeps about ₹10 more per ₹99. The Play policy for an app on Play still has to be checked (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No reliable count of how many Indians pay for apps or subscriptions in 2026. No Redseer or Bain figure was found.
- No public conversion or renewal data for ChatGPT Go, Google AI Plus/Pro or Perplexity after the free periods.
- No consumer app pricing found for Indian AI assistants such as Sarvam's or Krutrim's apps.
- No 2026 NPCI series for Autopay mandate *execution* success rates. An undated "90.4% registration / 49.3% execution success" figure appeared in search results without a traceable source, so I have not used it.
- Current (2026) Google Play fee status in India after the CCI inquiry is not confirmed. Also unconfirmed: whether AI credits bought inside a Play-distributed app must use Play Billing or user-choice billing.

## 2. LLM cost per task: model prices, token use, caching, cost per errand and per voice hour

### Takeaway
Agent-capable models got much cheaper in 2026. Claude Haiku 5.5 is $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens, DeepSeek V4 Flash about $0.09/$0.18, and Qwen3.7 Flash $0.03/$0.13. Mid-tier Gemini 3.8 Flash ($0.75/$3.75) doubles in price on 1 Jan 2027. With prompt caching (cache reads at 0.05–0.1x the input price), a typical errand on a cheap model costs between a few paise and about ₹2. A heavy user's month costs roughly ₹5–₹40 on cheap models, but about ₹260 on a Sonnet-class model (ESTIMATE). A real-time voice hour costs about ₹80–₹130 on Gemini Live, or about ₹55–₹65 with Sarvam speech-to-text plus text-to-speech (ESTIMATE).

**Voice hour — ESTIMATE**
- Gemini 3.8 Live, 60 minutes of audio streamed in and 30 minutes spoken back: ~$0.84 (≈₹79). With 60 minutes each way: $1.38 (≈₹130). This ignores context re-billing per turn, reasoning tokens and tool calls, so it is a lower bound.
- Sarvam pipeline: speech-to-text for 60 minutes = ₹29.4, plus text-to-speech for 30 minutes of speech at ~900 characters/minute (150 words/min × ~6 characters, an assumption) = ₹27, giving ₹56 before GST (₹67 with GST). A cheap LLM adds roughly ₹2–₹10.

### Inferences
- **Cheap models make sachet packs workable.** A ₹99 pack that gives ~₹40 of inference (about $0.40) would cover a heavy month on Haiku 5.5, DeepSeek V4 Flash or Qwen Flash class models with large headroom. It would cover only a few food orders on Sonnet 5.5. Credits should therefore be metered by model tier (like Raycast's "each model spends credits at its own rate"), with cheap models as the default.
- **Caching matters as much as model choice.** It cuts costs by 2–2.7x in these estimates. Keep the system prompt and tool definitions stable and first in the prompt, and trim screen dumps.
- **Prices change in both directions within weeks** (e.g. Gemini 3.8 Flash doubling on 1 Jan 2027, and Haiku dropping from $1/$5 to $0.10/$0.50 between generations). Credit packs should be priced in Boop credits, not tokens, so Boop can re-route models without repricing.
- **Real-time voice is 50–100x more expensive per hour than text errands.** It fits a per-minute "cloud run / voice minutes" product, not the base pack.

### Gaps
- No measured token counts for Boop's own errands. The estimates above should be replaced with telemetry, e.g. OpenRouter key `usage` fields from a test cohort.
- No independent benchmark of how reliably the cheapest models (Qwen3.7 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Flash) handle multi-step Android UI agent tasks compared with Haiku 5.5 or Gemini Flash.
- Prices for Sarvam's consumer-facing or speech-to-speech models, and for per-minute OpenAI realtime, were seen only in secondary sources.

## 3. OpenRouter for resellers: per-user keys, terms and fees

### Takeaway
OpenRouter supports Boop's mechanism directly. A Management key can create, cap, reset (daily/weekly/monthly), disable and meter one API key per customer, and the docs name "SaaS: create unique API keys for each customer instance" as a use case. Costs: a **5.5% fee on credit purchases** (Standard plan; 8% Business), no markup on inference, and BYOK free up to $25K/month of usage, then 5%. The legal risk: the Terms forbid "reselling API access to Models", while allowing customers to use models through "your own products and services". Handing raw, general-purpose keys to users who can use them outside Boop is a grey area. Unused credits can expire after 365 days and can be refunded only within 24 hours.

### Cited Findings
- **Provisioning (Management) API:** `POST /api/v1/keys` creates a key with a `name` and optional credit `limit`. `PATCH` updates `name`, `disabled`, `include_byok_in_limit` and `limit_reset` (daily at midnight UTC; weekly and monthly also mentioned). Keys report `limit_remaining`, `usage`, `usage_daily/weekly/monthly` and BYOK usage. Management keys cannot call completion endpoints, and their expiry is fixed at creation. Named use cases: SaaS per-customer keys, key rotation, and usage monitoring with automatic disabling. The page does not state the unit of `limit`. — [OpenRouter docs: Provisioning API keys](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/provisioning-api-keys)
- **Fees (pricing page, seen 6 Nov 2026):** credit purchases carry a 5.5% platform fee on Standard pay-as-you-go and 8% on Business, with Enterprise discounted by contract. Payment is by card or USDC. BYOK: $25,000/month of list-price inference free, then 5% of the equivalent OpenRouter price. Free plan: 50 requests/day (the FAQ adds 20/min, and 1,000/day after buying $10 of credits), with no budgets, prompt caching or management keys. Standard plan: 600+ models, 80+ providers, budgets and spend controls, prompt caching, management API keys and data-policy routing. — [OpenRouter pricing](https://openrouter.ai/pricing); [OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)
- **No inference markup:** "no markup on inference pricing", so users pay the provider's listed rate. — [OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)
- **Refunds and expiry:** unused credits can be refunded within 24 hours of purchase, platform fees are non-refundable, and crypto payments are never refundable. Deleting an account forfeits its credits. "OpenRouter reserves the right to expire unused credits three hundred sixty-five (365) days after purchase" (§4.2). Credits are non-transferable (§4.4). — [OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq); [OpenRouter Terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)
- **Reselling clauses:** §7(4) bars "reselling API access to Models or otherwise developing a competing service". §7(14) bars selling or transferring "the access granted under these Terms". §5.1 extends model access to your customers "to the extent you incorporate the Service into your own products and services", and you must make sure they accept the Model Terms. §5.2 makes you responsible for your customers' acts. §5.5 lets OpenRouter suspend a customer's model access for Model Terms violations. §3.2: "You are responsible for all activity and charges under its account or API Credentials." — [OpenRouter Terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)
- OpenRouter's own model is itself a credits business: prepaid credits, a fee on purchase, and pass-through inference prices. — [OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)

### Inferences
- **Safest design:** Boop's backend (or a thin proxy) holds the per-user provisioned key, so the key is used only through Boop and the user never sees a raw key. Boop then sells "Boop credits" that fund the key's `limit`. That fits §5.1 (incorporated into Boop's product) rather than §7(4) (reselling API access). If the plan is to give users exportable keys, get written confirmation from OpenRouter first.
- **Unit-economics drag from OpenRouter (ESTIMATE):** the 5.5% fee plus GST and payment fees mean roughly 12–25% of a pack's sticker price never reaches inference, depending on whether Play billing is used (see section 1).
- **Policy for Boop's own credits:** match OpenRouter's 365-day expiry, don't promise refunds beyond what Boop can recover (OpenRouter refunds only within 24h), and plan for fraud (stolen UPI or cards buying credits) because Boop is liable for all usage on its keys (§3.2).
- **Lock-in hedge:** BYOK users keep using their own OpenRouter, Gemini or other keys at no cost to Boop. Paid packs could also route to Google or Anthropic directly at volume to avoid the 5.5% fee. Sarvam (₹-billed, India-hosted) is an alternative for Indic speech.

### Gaps
- The unit of `limit` on provisioned keys (presumably USD credits) is not stated on the docs page.
- No public statement from OpenRouter on whether per-user capped keys handed to end users count as "reselling".
- Whether OpenRouter accepts Indian payment methods (UPI/RuPay) for topping up, and the FX or GST treatment for an Indian company buying credits, is not confirmed.

## 4. Business models for open-source (and adjacent) consumer software

### Takeaway
What works for open-source consumer products is selling **convenience**: hosted sync, remote access, managed AI, hardware. The code itself is not what users pay for. Examples are Obsidian (free core, paid Sync/Publish, "100% user-supported"), Home Assistant / Nabu Casa (cloud subscription plus hardware, no outside investors) and Brave (ads, search API and premium, about 130M MAU). Credit-metered AI is now standard in paid tools: Raycast sells monthly AI credits with BYOK and top-ups, and OpenRouter takes a fee on prepaid credits. Donations alone (Signal) do not cover costs even at huge scale. For licences, the 2024–26 trend is toward AGPL, or source-available with delayed open-sourcing (FSL), to stop competitors cloning the hosted service. Hard relicensing (BSL/SSPL) brought backlash and forks.

### Cited Findings
- **Obsidian** (pricing page seen 6 Nov 2026): the core app is "Free without limits" with no sign-up. Sync costs $4/user/month billed annually ($5 monthly), Publish $8/site/month annually ($10 monthly), Catalyst is a $25 one-time supporter tier, and the Commercial licence is $50/user/year. Obsidian calls itself "100% user-supported", and Catalyst helps keep it "free from investor influence". Obsidian is closed source but local-first. — [Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing)
- **Raycast** (pricing seen 6 Nov 2026): Pro costs $10/month or $96/year with 500 AI credits a month and BYOK. Plus costs $20 with 3,000 credits and top-tier models, and Max $50 with 7,500. "Each model spends credits at its own rate". Monthly credits roll over one cycle (they expire after 60 days). Top-ups expire after 12 months and require an active subscription. Raycast is not open source. — [Raycast pricing](https://www.raycast.com/pricing)
- **Home Assistant / Nabu Casa:** no external investors. Its main income is Home Assistant Cloud subscriptions plus hardware, and the majority of profits go to the Open Home Foundation, which owns Home Assistant. — [Nabu Casa about](https://nabucasa.com/about/). The cloud service is being renamed "Home Assistant Link". — [HA community post](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/big-tech-ruined-the-cloud-so-we-re-renaming-ours/1027150). Home Assistant had about 2 million active installs ("2 million homes") in 2025. — [State of the Open Home 2025](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/2-million-homes-strong-state-of-the-open-home-2025/878546). Price is about $6.50/month or $65/year according to third-party sites only. — [Home Assistant Cloud page](https://www.home-assistant.io/cloud/)
- **Brave** (open-source browser): about 130.77M MAU in Oct 2026 (51M DAU in July 2026). — [PiunikaWeb, 3 Nov 2026](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/11/03/brave-130-million-monthly-active-users/); [PiunikaWeb, Jul 2026](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/07/02/brave-nears-121m-monthly-users-search-hits-new-all-time-high/). Revenue comes from the Search API (licensed to AI companies), search ads, browser ads (70% revenue share to users) and premium subscriptions (VPN, Search Premium), and Brave's business chief said it passed $100M annualised revenue in Q1 2025. These figures are not audited. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/brave-blocks-your-ads-and-sells-its-own-and-its-working/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/brave/)
- **Signal** (donations): costs were projected at about $50M/year by 2025, and SMS verification alone costs about $6M/year (Nov 2023). — [YourStory, Nov 2023](https://YourStory.com/2023/11/private-text-app-signal-meredith-whittaker-joshua-lund). FY2024 Form 990: revenue $29.4M against expenses $38.0M, with contributions and grants 74% of revenue. — [Philanthropy.org 990 report](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/824506840/signal-technology-foundation)
- **Licensing moves (open source vs hosted business):**
  - Grafana relicensed Loki and Tempo to AGPLv3. Users can still use, modify and redistribute, but must share changes when they offer the software. — [Grafana Labs](https://grafana.com/blog/grafana-loki-tempo-relicensing-to-agplv3)
  - Unleash moved its open-source core from Apache 2.0 to AGPLv3 and kept a commercial Enterprise edition. — [Unleash](https://www.getunleash.io/blog/unleash-moving-to-agplv3)
  - OpenObserve moved from Apache to AGPL. — [OpenObserve](https://openobserve.ai/blog/what-are-apache-gpl-and-agpl-licenses-and-why-openobserve-moved-from-apache-to-agpl/)
  - Sentry's FSL (Functional Source License) is source-available and converts to Apache 2.0 after two years. Keygen argues for "Fair Source" over AGPL (an opinion piece). — [Keygen](https://keygen.sh/blog/weaponized-open-source/)
  - Bear Blog moved from MIT to the source-available Elastic License (Sept 2025) to protect its hosted subscription. — [Fossdle link](https://link.fossdle.org/post/2533)
  - Elastic and Redis partly reversed their source-available moves by adding AGPL options. HashiCorp did not. MinIO stripped its community edition and archived the repo in April 2026. — [WZ-IT licence-change chronicle](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/open-source-licence-change-chronicle/)
  - OSI does not count BSL, ELv2, SSPL or FSL as open source. — [WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/open-source-licence-change-chronicle/)

### Inferences
- **The best-fitting model for Boop is "free app + BYOK forever; pay for convenience".** Paid items: prepaid credits (no key setup), cloud runs (phone-off errands), possibly sync/backup and premium voice. This mirrors Obsidian (paid Sync), Nabu Casa (paid remote access and voice) and Raycast (credits plus BYOK). BYOK should remain a first-class, unpaid path, because that is what keeps the open-source community on board.
- **Licence choice:** Apache-2.0/MIT maximises contributors and OEM or partner adoption, but lets anyone clone the hosted credits service. AGPL on the client does not protect a server Boop doesn't publish. The protection needed is for Boop's *cloud-run* service, so a common pattern is: client app Apache-2.0 (or GPL), server components AGPL or FSL, and the Boop name and trademark kept by the company. Changing licence later causes backlash (Elastic, Redis, HashiCorp, Bear), so choose before launch.
- **Donations won't fund a company** (Signal runs a deficit with tens of millions of users). At most, a "supporter" badge like Obsidian Catalyst is a small extra.

### Gaps
- No revenue or subscriber numbers for Obsidian or Nabu Casa. Brave's revenue figures are unaudited estimates.
- No examples were found of an India-focused open-source consumer app monetising with credits.
- Current Nabu Casa pricing was not confirmed from the official page (only third-party sites).

## 6. Benchmarks for a viable company: CAC, LTV, retention and small-ticket winners

### Takeaway
Globally, AI apps convert and earn better than other subscription apps but churn faster: 12-month retention on monthly plans is 6.1% for AI apps against 9.5% for non-AI apps (RevenueCat 2026). India's year-one value per payer is about $14 (~₹1,320), so Boop's paid CAC has to stay at a few hundred rupees per *payer*. The median paid install in India costs about ₹20 and a signup about ₹48 (vendor data). At a 2–2.5% download-to-paid rate, that implies roughly ₹800–₹1,000 of paid acquisition per payer (ESTIMATE). Paid acquisition alone barely breaks even, so growth must come mostly from organic and open-source channels. The Indian small-ticket winners (Pocket FM, Kuku FM, Pratilipi, STAGE) monetise through microtransactions, ₹1 trials and very cheap entry plans, and some still lose money on heavy ad spend.

### Cited Findings
- **RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026** (published 14 Mar 2026, 2025 data, 115K+ apps, $16B revenue):
  - AI apps: year-one realised LTV per payer $30.16 vs $21.37 for non-AI apps. Median trial-start rate 8.5% vs 5.6%. Median download-to-paid conversion **2.4% vs 2.0%**.
  - Monthly-plan 12-month retention **6.1% vs 9.5%**. Annual-plan retention **21.1% vs 30.7%**. Median refund rate 4.2% vs 3.5%.
  - Hard paywalls convert 10.7% by Day 35 vs 2.1% for freemium, but both retain similarly after a year (27% vs 28% on annual plans). Trials of 17+ days convert 42.5% vs 25.5% for shorter trials. Android trial-to-paid is 32.5%.
  - Median prices: $10 monthly, $34.80 yearly. **Year-one value per payer: India and Southeast Asia $14.**

### Inferences
- **Back-of-envelope viability (ESTIMATE):**
  - Assumptions: Boop credit packs average ₹99 net-of-GST ₹84, with ~₹40 going to inference. Gross margin is about ₹40 per pack (~48%) on Razorpay, or ~₹30 on Play billing. A paying user buys 1 pack/month, and 25% are still buying after 12 months, roughly between RevenueCat's monthly and annual AI retention.
  - Result: year-one gross profit per payer ≈ ₹40 × ~4.5 average months paid ≈ ₹180–₹250. Paid CAC per payer at ₹20/install and 2–2.5% conversion is ₹800–₹1,000.
  - Implication: **paid acquisition does not pay back in year one.** Growth has to be organic (open-source community, word of mouth, Play Store search, creator content, partner integrations), with paid spend only on proven high-intent cohorts. Raising pack value (₹199) or attaching cloud-run minutes improves this.
- **Model the business on "payers × packs", not subscriptions.** Track pack repurchase rate, the time to second purchase, and gross margin per credit after model routing. These are the numbers Surge-stage investors will ask about.
- **Lessons from small-ticket winners:** keep the first payment tiny (₹1–₹29), don't show the full price next to the trial, send reminders before charging, pay per unit of value (Pocket FM's per-episode ≈ Boop's per-errand), and unlock instantly by UPI.
