# Regulatory and Platform-Policy Requirements for a Consumer AI Assistant in India (as of 6 November 2026)

Scope: Boop is an open-source, Android-first assistant. It reads notifications, contacts and calendar on the phone and sends user text to a cloud AI provider the user picks (for example through OpenRouter). It orders food and groceries through Swiggy, hands UPI payments to the user's own UPI app, and plans to sell prepaid AI credit packs and possibly run tasks on its own cloud servers. The notes cover India only. Primary sources (Gazette text, Google Play policy pages) were used where they could be fetched. Pending or proposed rules are flagged as such.

## 1. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: what is in force when, and what it requires

### Takeaway
On 6 Nov 2026 the DPDP Rules are only partly in force. The Board provisions run from 13 Nov 2025 and Consent Manager registration starts on 13 Nov 2026. The core duties (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, children, data rights, cross-border transfers) and all penalties start on 13 May 2027. MeitY proposed moving that date to 13 Nov 2026, but no Gazette amendment had been found by early October 2026. If Boop keeps data on the phone, its exposure is small. If it hosts user data or credentials on its own servers, it becomes a full Data Fiduciary, with duties for security, one-year log retention, breach reporting and age-gating, and penalties of up to ₹250 crore.

### Cited Findings

**Notification and phased commencement**
- The DPDP Rules, 2025 were issued as G.S.R. 846(E) and dated "New Delhi, the 13th November, 2025". Rule 1(2) brings Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 (definitions and the Data Protection Board) into force on publication. Rule 1(3) brings Rule 4 (Consent Managers) into force "one year after the date of publication". Rule 1(4) brings "Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23" into force "eighteen months after the date of publication" — [DPDP Rules 2025, Gazette text (MeitY)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- PIB's explainer gives the notification date as 14 November 2025, a one-day gap from the Gazette's 13 November. It says 6,915 consultation inputs were received and describes "an eighteen-month period for phased compliance" — [PIB explainer, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Most trackers use these dates: 13 Nov 2025 (Board), 13 Nov 2026 (Rule 4 and the First Schedule, Consent Managers) and 13 May 2027 (Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23: fiduciary duties, rights, appeals, information calls) — [Legismith timeline, reviewed 30 Sep 2026](https://legismith.com/dpdp-rules-2025-timeline/)
- On 13 Nov 2026 only Rule 4 and Sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) of the Act start, all concerning Consent Managers, so "ordinary businesses have no new legal obligations on that date". Penalties under Sections 33–34 and the Schedule start on 13 May 2027 — [The Privacy Labs, 8 Oct 2026](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts)
- **Proposed acceleration (pending, not law):** On 22–23 Jan 2026 MeitY proposed cutting the 18-month runway to 12 months, which would move the date to 13 Nov 2026, with comments due by 4 Feb 2026. It also floated immediate effect for SDF notification (Section 10(1)), cross-border restrictions (Rules 13(4) and 15) and state exemptions (Section 17(2)), and Rule 8(3) log retention within 90 days of the amendment — [S.S. Rana & Co., 13 Feb 2026](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm)
- As of 8 Oct 2026, "no amendment to Rule 1 had been published in the Gazette". Reports differ on whether the proposal covered all Data Fiduciaries or only SDFs — [The Privacy Labs](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts). A separate September 2026 compliance tracker also treats 13 May 2027 as the main deadline — [MirvoLegal (as at Sep 2026)](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said on 17 Nov 2025 that the government intended to shorten the timeline for large businesses — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)

**Data Protection Board**
- The Board works as a "digital office" (Rule 20) and has four members. Complaints are filed and tracked online, and appeals go to TDSAT — [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf); [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- No Chairperson or Members had been appointed as of 1 Aug 2026 (per LiveLaw), and no Gazette notice of appointments had been found by 8 Oct 2026 — [The Privacy Labs](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts)

**Applicability and the personal-use carve-out**
- The Act does not apply to "personal data processed by an individual for any personal or domestic purpose" (Section 3(c)(i)) — [DPDP Act 2023 (MeitY)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- A Data Fiduciary "alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing". A Data Processor processes "on behalf of" a fiduciary, and the fiduciary stays responsible for processing by its processors — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- **Startup exemption power:** Section 17(3) lets the Government notify Data Fiduciaries, "including startups", to whom Section 5 (notice), Section 8(3) and 8(7) (accuracy and erasure) and Sections 10 (SDF) and 11 (right to information) do not apply. A "startup" here means an Indian private limited company, partnership or LLP recognised under DPIIT criteria — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)

**Notice and consent (from 13 May 2027)**
- Under Rule 3 the notice must stand on its own, separate from other information. In "clear and plain language" it must give an itemised description of the personal data, the specified purposes, and a specific description of the goods or services or uses that the processing enables. It must also give a link and other means to withdraw consent "with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given", to exercise rights, and to complain to the Board — [DPDP Rules, Rule 3](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Consent must be "free, specific, informed" and so on (Section 6(1)). A fiduciary must set up an effective grievance mechanism (Section 8(10)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Each fiduciary must publish a contact for processing queries, either the DPO or a responsible person (Rule 9). It must publish how to exercise rights and respond to grievances within "a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days" (Rule 14(3)). Users may nominate others to exercise their rights (Rule 14(4)) — [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

**Consent Managers (from 13 Nov 2026)**
- A Consent Manager must be a company incorporated in India with net worth "not less than two crore rupees" and sound technical, operational and financial capacity (First Schedule, Part A). It acts in a fiduciary capacity toward Data Principals, must avoid conflicts of interest with Data Fiduciaries, and may not sub-contract its obligations (Part B) — [DPDP Rules, First Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Consent Managers must register with the Board (Section 6(9)) and are "accountable to the Data Principal" (Section 6(8)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)

**Security safeguards and log retention**
- Rule 6 sets minimum safeguards: encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; access controls; logging, monitoring and review to detect unauthorised access; backups for continuity; retention of "such logs and personal data for a period of one year"; security clauses in processor contracts; and technical and organisational measures — [DPDP Rules, Rule 6](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 8(3) requires a fiduciary to retain "personal data, associated traffic data and other logs of the processing for a minimum period of one year from the date of such processing" for Seventh Schedule purposes (state access), including data handled by its processors, such as a cloud host. The e-book illustration says logs must be kept "even if X deletes her account" — [DPDP Rules, Rule 8(3)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 8(1)–(2) and the Third Schedule apply auto-erasure after three years of user inactivity only to e-commerce entities with at least 2 crore registered users in India, online gaming intermediaries with at least 50 lakh, and social media intermediaries with at least 2 crore. Users must be warned at least 48 hours before erasure — [DPDP Rules, Rule 8 and Third Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

**Breach notification**
- Rule 7(1) requires notice to each affected Data Principal "without delay", through their account or registered contact. The notice must cover the nature, extent and timing of the breach, likely consequences, mitigation, steps the user can take, and a contact person — [DPDP Rules, Rule 7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Under Rule 7(2) the Board gets an initial description "without delay". A detailed report follows "within seventy-two hours of becoming aware", or a longer period if the Board allows. It covers facts, causes, mitigation, findings about the person who caused the breach, remedial measures, and a report on the user notices — [DPDP Rules, Rule 7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- The 6-hour clock belongs to CERT-In, not to DPDP. Both apply — [The Privacy Labs](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts) (CERT-In details are in Section 2 below)

**Children's data (age under 18)**
- A "child" is someone under 18 (Section 2(f)). A fiduciary must obtain "verifiable consent of the parent" before processing a child's data (Section 9(1)). It must not do processing likely to harm a child's well-being (Section 9(2)), and "shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children" (Section 9(3)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Rule 10 requires checking that the parent is an identifiable adult (18 or older). This can use identity and age details the fiduciary already holds reliably, or details the person provides voluntarily or through a "virtual token" from an authorised entity, including a DigiLocker service provider. Four illustrations cover parent-is-user and parent-is-not-user cases — [DPDP Rules, Rule 10](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Exemptions are listed in the Fourth Schedule. Part A covers classes of fiduciary such as clinical establishments, educational institutions and childcare. Part B covers purposes, among them email-only account creation, real-time location for safety, blocking harmful content or ads, and "confirmation by the Data Fiduciary that the Data Principal is not a child" — [DPDP Rules, Rule 12 and Fourth Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- PIB's explainer says parental consent is needed unless processing relates to "essential services such as healthcare, education or real-time safety" — [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)

**Cross-border transfers**
- Section 16(1) is a negative list: the Government may "by notification, restrict the transfer" to specified countries. Stricter sectoral laws, such as RBI payment-data localisation, still apply (Section 16(2)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Rule 15 allows transfer outside India, subject to requirements the Government may set by general or special order about making data available "to any foreign State, or to any person or entity under the control of or any agency of such a State" — [DPDP Rules, Rule 15](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

**Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDFs)**
- The Government designates SDFs under Section 10(1) based on volume and sensitivity of data, risk to Data Principals, sovereignty, electoral democracy, security and public order. Commentators expect AI system operators, search engines, e-commerce marketplaces and social media could be included, but no classification has been notified — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)
- Rule 13 obligations: an annual DPIA and audit, with significant observations reported to the Board; due diligence that "algorithmic software" does not risk Data Principals' rights; and localisation of personal data and its traffic data that a government committee specifies — [DPDP Rules, Rule 13](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf). Under the Act SDFs must also appoint an India-based DPO and an independent data auditor — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)
- No SDF duties had been notified as of Oct 2026 — [The Privacy Labs](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts)

**Penalties (Schedule to the Act, Section 33)**
- Failure to take reasonable security safeguards (Section 8(5)): up to ₹250 crore. Failure to notify the Board or users of a breach (Section 8(6)): up to ₹200 crore. Breach of children's obligations (Section 9): up to ₹200 crore. Breach of SDF obligations (Section 10): up to ₹150 crore. Breach of a Data Principal's duties (Section 15): up to ₹10,000. Breach of a voluntary undertaking: up to the penalty for the original breach. Any other breach: up to ₹50 crore — [DPDP Act 2023, Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)

### Inferences
- **On-device-only architecture (lowest exposure).** Suppose the user installs the app, enters their own OpenRouter or provider key, and the Boop company never receives notifications, contacts or messages. The processing then arguably sits with the user (Section 3(c)(i), personal or domestic purpose) and with the AI provider the user chose. The Boop company would not be a fiduciary for that content. It would still be a fiduciary for any data it collects itself: crash reports, analytics, accounts, payment records for credit packs. This is untested, and the "determines purpose and means" test could catch an app developer whose code decides what is sent where. Get legal advice.
- **Boop-hosted architecture (full exposure).** If Boop servers relay prompts, store credentials such as Swiggy session tokens or provider keys, or run cloud tasks, Boop becomes a Data Fiduciary from 13 May 2027 (or earlier if the acceleration is notified). It would then need a Rule 3 itemised notice and consent flow, Rule 6 encryption and access logging, Rule 8(3) retention of personal data, traffic data and logs for at least one year (this conflicts with a "we store nothing" promise), a 72-hour Board report and immediate user notices on breach, and CERT-In 6-hour reporting with 180-day logs. The AI provider (OpenRouter and the model labs) would be Boop's Data Processor, so Rule 6(1)(f) contract clauses are needed.
- **Third-party data.** Notifications and contacts contain personal data of people who are not Boop users, such as message senders and contact names. If Boop's servers process them, Boop processes data about people who never consented, and the Section 7 "legitimate uses" are narrow. Processing them on-device, under the user's personal-use carve-out, is the safer design.
- **Children.** No children's exemption covers a general assistant, and Section 9(3) bans "behavioural monitoring" of children, which an assistant reading notifications could arguably do. The practical route is an 18+ age gate (Fourth Schedule Part B item 6 permits processing to confirm a user is not a child) or a Rule 10 parental-consent flow, for example using a DigiLocker age token.
- **Cross-border.** Sending prompts to US or EU model providers is allowed today. Watch for a Section 16 negative-list notification and for any Rule 15 order about foreign-state access.
- **Consent Managers** are optional for Boop. Boop does not need to become one, since that requires ₹2 crore net worth and an Indian company, but it may later want to accept consent through registered Consent Managers.

### Gaps
- No Gazette amendment to Rule 1 (the 12-month acceleration) was found as of early Oct 2026. I could not confirm whether anything was notified between 8 Oct and 6 Nov 2026. Check egazette.gov.in and meity.gov.in directly.
- I found no notification under Section 17(3) exempting startups.
- I found no notified Section 16 negative list and no Rule 15 order.
- Whether the Board has been constituted as of Nov 2026 is unconfirmed (last data point: no members as of Aug 2026).
- I found no official guidance on whether an open-source app publisher whose app sends data straight to a user-chosen third-party API counts as a Data Fiduciary.

## 2. IT Act and IT Rules: intermediary duties, the 2026 synthetic-content rules, AI advisories, grievance officer, CERT-In

### Takeaway
The IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 were notified on 10 Feb 2026 and took effect on 20 Feb 2026. They require labelling and provenance metadata for AI-generated audio, visual and audio-visual content, cut takedown windows to 3 hours (36 hours before), and cut the grievance resolution period to 7 days (15 before). A text-only assistant is largely outside the "synthetically generated information" (SGI) definition, but any image or voice generation would bring the labelling duties in. India's broader AI policy (the India AI Governance Guidelines, Nov 2025) is voluntary. CERT-In's 6-hour incident reporting and 180-day log retention apply to any Boop servers.

### Cited Findings
- MeitY notified the amendments on 10 Feb 2026, effective 20 Feb 2026, which gave a 10-day window — [MediaNama, Feb 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/); [Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content)
- **SGI definition:** "audio, visual or audio-visual information" that is artificially or algorithmically created or altered and likely to be perceived as indistinguishable from a natural person or real-world event. The test is whether it appears real, not merely whether AI was used — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/). Good-faith technical edits, documents and templates, and accessibility or translation uses are excluded — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content). One secondary source said SGI covers text, which conflicts with the notified wording — [Global Law Experts (via search summary)](https://globallawexperts.com/news/it-amendment-rules-india)
- **Duties for intermediaries offering SGI tools:** Rule 3(3)(a)(i) requires technical measures to prevent unlawful SGI such as CSAM, NCII and false documents. Rule 3(3)(a)(ii) requires labelling permitted SGI "in a manner that is easily noticeable and adequately perceivable", with a prefixed audio disclosure for audio, permanent metadata or provenance "where technically feasible", and a unique identifier of the generating resource. Rule 3(3)(b) forbids enabling removal of labels or metadata — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/)
- **SSMIs** (more than 50 lakh registered users in India) must collect user declarations on SGI, verify them with automated tools, and label confirmed SGI (Rule 4(1A)) — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content)
- **Timelines:** Rule 3(1)(d) takedown on government or court order went from 36 hours to 3 hours. Rule 3(2)(b) went from 24 hours to 2 hours; the source does not state its subject, but in the 2021 Rules this clause covers complaints about intimate or impersonating content. Grievance resolution under Rule 3(2)(a)(i) went from 15 days to 7 days, and the proviso timeline from 72 hours to 36 hours — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/)
- **User notices:** intermediaries must tell users "at least once every three months" about content removal, account termination, liability and mandatory reporting (Rule 3(1)(c)). Tools offering SGI must also give an SGI misuse warning (Rule 3(1)(ca)) — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/)
- **Safe harbour:** proactive or automated removal done under the Rules does not breach Section 79 safe-harbour conditions — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/)
- The draft amendment was released for consultation in October 2025, with comments open until 13 Nov 2025 — [S.S. Rana on AI Governance Guidelines](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines/)
- **India AI Governance Guidelines** (released 5 Nov 2025) are voluntary. They set out seven "sutras" and six pillars and create an AI Governance Group, a Technology & Policy Expert Committee and the AI Safety Institute. Regulation is left to sector regulators, and India does not plan a standalone AI statute — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines/)
- **CERT-In Directions (28 Apr 2022, under Section 70B(6) of the IT Act, effective 27 Jun 2022):** report cyber incidents to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing them; keep ICT logs securely for a rolling 180 days in India; sync clocks to NIC/NPL NTP. These apply to service providers, intermediaries, data centres and body corporates — [Mondaq: 6 hours to report](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1270332/6-hours-to-report-cyber-incidents-to-cert-in); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/cert-in-issues-directions-relating-information-security-practices.html)
- Critics such as the Internet Freedom Foundation argue the 2026 amendments contradict their own purpose — [IFF](https://internetfreedom.in/it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026-contradict-their-purpose/)

### Inferences
- **Is Boop an intermediary?** An app that only runs on the phone and calls the user's chosen API is arguably not an intermediary for third-party content. Once Boop servers store or transmit user content (cloud tasks, shared rooms, synced chats), Boop likely becomes an intermediary under the IT Act. It would then need published terms and privacy policy, a Grievance Officer whose contact is published, acknowledgement and resolution within the new 7-day limit, 3-hour compliance with government or court orders, and quarterly user notices.
- **SGI labelling:** Boop's text chat is outside the SGI definition. Features that generate images, video or realistic synthetic voice, including TTS that sounds like a real person, would trigger Rule 3(3): a visible label, an audio prefix disclosure, provenance metadata and abuse filters.
- **CERT-In:** this is the most immediate cyber duty for any Boop server. A 6-hour report runs alongside the DPDP 72-hour Board report and the user notices.

### Gaps
- Secondary summaries give the 2026 amendment's notification number as G.S.R. 120(E). I did not verify it against the Gazette.
- I could not fetch the text of the March 2024 MeitY AI advisories (on under-tested models and labelling) or confirm their current status. They appear to be superseded in practice by the 2026 Rules and the 2025 Guidelines, but I found no source saying so.
- The current acknowledgement timeline for the Grievance Officer (24 hours under the 2021 Rules) was not confirmed as unchanged in the 2026 text.
- The proposed Digital India Act status in 2026 was not researched.

## 3. Payments: RBI rules for prepaid credits, payment aggregators, GST/OIDAR, TDS/TCS

### Takeaway
Credits that can only be spent on Boop's own AI service are a closed-system PPI and need no RBI authorisation under the current Master Directions. RBI's draft PPI Directions, 2026 (22 Apr 2026) keep that exemption for non-marketplace issuers but would end it for marketplaces. So Boop credits must never become spendable on third-party goods such as Swiggy orders. Boop needs no payment-aggregator licence if it collects only its own revenue through a licensed PA, and handing UPI off to the user's UPI app keeps Boop out of the funds flow. Through Google Play, Google handles GST on in-app purchases. Direct sales by an Indian Boop entity carry GST, and imports of foreign AI API services fall under OIDAR and reverse-charge rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Closed-system PPIs (current Master Directions):** issued "for facilitating the purchase of goods and services from that entity only and do not permit cash withdrawal"; they are outside RBI authorisation — [RBI Master Directions on PPIs](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12156) (via search summary)
- **Draft RBI (Prepaid Payment Instruments) Directions, 2026** (issued 22 Apr 2026; comments until 22 May 2026; still a draft):
  - drops the formal "Closed System PPI" category but keeps the authorisation exemption for PPIs issued by entities "other than a marketplace". "Marketplace" follows the PA Directions 2025 definition, and PPIs used on marketplaces must come from a licensed issuer.
  - authorised issuers need net worth of ₹5 crore at application, rising to ₹15 crore by year 3.
  - Gift PPIs, renamed Special Purpose PPIs, get a one-year maximum validity and cannot be bought with cash, and issuers "will no longer earn breakage revenue from unspent balances".
  - small PPIs get a two-year maximum validity; full-KYC PPIs are limited to one per holder with a ₹2 lakh monthly debit cap.
  - no transition period is proposed — [Legal500 / "Holding All The Cards"](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/finance-and-banking/holding-all-the-cards-rbi-issues-draft-prepaid-payment-instruments-directions)
  - one source says the 2021 Master Directions stay in force until final Directions are notified — [search summary of Legal500 and others](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/finance-and-banking/holding-all-the-cards-rbi-issues-draft-prepaid-payment-instruments-directions)
- **Payment Aggregator Directions, 2025** (RBI, 15 Sep 2025): non-bank PAs need RBI authorisation, net worth of ₹15 crore at application and ₹25 crore by the end of the third year. Banks need no separate authorisation — [MediaNama explainer, Sep 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-explained-rbi-master-direction-payment-aggregators/); [Saraf & Partners](https://sarafpartners.com/rbi-issues-the-reserve-bank-of-india-regulation-of-payment-aggregators-directions-2025/)
- **RBI and Google Play billing:** in March 2024 RBI began examining, after a complaint by Hoichoi, whether Google Play's billing system breaches the Payment and Settlement Systems Act. Google said "The Google Play billing system is not a payment system". No outcome has been reported — [MediaNama, Nov 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/)
- **GST on Play purchases:** Google says it "is responsible for setting, charging, and remitting GST to the appropriate authority for all paid app and in-app purchases made by customers in India" — [Google Payments Center, region guidelines](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7161449). Google's India tax page says Google handles applicable TDS and GST TCS on paid apps and in-app purchases. For alternative-billing purchases, Google collects the applicable GST from developers — [Google India tax information (German version)](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/15152449?hl=de)
- **OIDAR:** since 1 Oct 2023, foreign suppliers of online information and database access or retrieval (OIDAR) services to individuals and government in India must register and pay GST. Business recipients pay under reverse charge — [CA Sahuja, OIDAR guide (2024)](https://www.casahuja.com/2024/07/guide-to-oidar-services-under-gst.html?m=1)
- **PA/PG fees:** 18% GST under SAC 998433, per a vendor blog. This is a secondary source and needs verifying — [IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/payment-gateway-compliance-rbi-gst-requirements)
- **TDS under Section 194-O (Income-tax Act):** e-commerce operators deduct 0.1% (cut from 1%) on gross sales they facilitate for participants, from 1 Oct 2024 — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/budget-2024-tds-rate-for-ecommerce-operators-cut-to-0-1-from-1/amp); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-194o)
- **GST TCS under Section 52 (CGST Act):** 0.5% of net taxable value (0.25% CGST plus 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST) from 10 Jul 2024, under Notification 15/2024-Central Tax. It is deposited within 10 days after month-end — [TaxManagementIndia (via search summary)](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/detail_manual.asp?ID=1919)
- **Equalisation Levy:** the 2% levy on e-commerce supply was withdrawn for consideration received from 1 Aug 2024 — [Xflowpay](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/equalisation-levy). The 6% levy on online ads was abolished from 1 Apr 2025 through the Finance Act 2025 — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/india-equalisation-levy-removed-tax-impact-2025-125040100810_1.html); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/03/equalisation-levy-online-advertisements-abolished-google-meta-x-ads)

### Inferences
- **Credit packs:** design them as prepaid access to Boop's own service. They should be non-transferable, non-withdrawable, not redeemable for cash and usable only for Boop AI usage. That keeps them in the closed-system exemption now and in the "non-marketplace issuer" exemption under the draft. Do not let credits pay for Swiggy orders or for third-party sellers on any Boop marketplace. Doing so would risk a semi-closed PPI needing authorisation (₹5 crore rising to ₹15 crore net worth under the draft).
- **Expiry and breakage:** the draft's removal of breakage applies to regulated PPIs. Whether it reaches exempt closed-loop credits is unclear. Conservative design: long or no expiry, and clear refund terms for unused credits.
- **Collecting money directly** (web checkout, or UCB in-app): use an RBI-authorised PA (bank or licensed non-bank PA). Boop itself does not need PA authorisation when it collects only its own revenue.
- **UPI handoff:** using a UPI intent or deep link so the user's UPI app pays Swiggy or the merchant keeps Boop out of the funds flow. Then neither PA nor PPI rules apply, and Boop is not the "e-commerce operator" collecting consideration, so Section 194-O and Section 52 TCS should not apply. This is a design-dependent inference to confirm with a tax adviser.
- **GST on direct sales:** an Indian Boop entity selling credits to Indian consumers outside Play would charge GST on the service. 18% is the usual rate for IT and digital services, but I did not find a primary source in this pass. Registration thresholds and a CA's view are needed.
- **AI API costs:** if Boop pays OpenRouter or US model labs as a GST-registered Indian business, the imported services are likely subject to reverse-charge GST. If users bring their own keys and pay the provider themselves, that tax sits between the user and the provider.

### Gaps
- No final RBI PPI Directions 2026 were found. Status after the comment period (May 2026) is unknown.
- I did not find a primary CBIC source for the GST rate on prepaid AI credits, or on when GST is triggered (at sale or at redemption, and whether voucher rules apply).
- I did not locate RBI guidance specifically on in-app credits for digital services.
- Whether Google's alternative-billing GST collection applies to the service fee or the transaction was not clarified.

## 4. Google Play billing in India: requirement, fees, User Choice Billing, CCI orders and 2026 status

### Takeaway
Selling AI credits inside a Play-distributed app almost certainly counts as selling digital goods. Google's Payments policy lists "virtual currencies" and "cloud software and services", so Boop must use Google Play Billing or India's User Choice Billing (UCB). Food and grocery orders are physical goods and are exempt. India is still on the old fee schedule: 15% on the first $1M a year, 30% above, 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions, and 4 percentage points less under UCB. The new 10%+5% structure has so far reached only the EEA, UK, US, Australia and Japan. The legal fight is unresolved. The NCLAT upheld CCI's abuse finding in March 2025 and cut the fine to ₹216.69 crore. The Supreme Court heard developers' appeals on 3 Nov 2026 and next hears them on 1 Dec 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Payments policy:** in-app payments for "features, services, or content" must use Play Billing unless an exception applies. Examples: "virtual currencies, extra lives, additional playtime"; "subscription services"; "cloud software and services (such as data storage services, business productivity software"; and "app functionality or content". Virtual currencies "must only be used within the app or game title for which they were purchased". Exceptions include "physical goods", physical services and peer-to-peer payments. Section 8 allows in-app alternative billing in eligible countries after enrolment. Steering users to other payment methods is barred unless an exception applies — [Google Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)
- **Fees:** for "all other markets (until the updated fees roll out globally)", which includes India: "15% for the first $1M (USD) revenue earned by the developer each year", 30% above $1M, and 15% for auto-renewing subscriptions. In the EEA, UK and US (rollout date 30 Jun 2026) and Australia and Japan (30 Sep 2026), a new structure applies: 10% plus a 5% billing fee for subscriptions; 20% (new installs) or 25% (existing installs) plus 5% for other transactions; lower rates with external links. For India and South Korea alternative billing, the fee is the normal fee "minus 4%" — [Google Play service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622)
- **India UCB programme:** open to apps and games with users in India. The fee is "reduced by 4%", which is 4 percentage points, giving roughly 11% and 26%. Developers must enrol in Play Console, certify PCI DSS compliance, give users a way to report fraudulent transactions, and report transactions through the alternative billing APIs within 24 hours (or self-report monthly by the 5th business day). Google renders the choice screens. Web payments in an embedded webview are allowed, and prices may differ from Play's — [Google Play Help: India UCB](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13306652); [Alternative billing APIs migration page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247); fee range "11% to 26%" per [MediaNama, Nov 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/)
- **CCI and the courts:**
  - The CCI's October 2022 order fined Google ₹936.44 crore for abuse of dominance through Play billing and directed it to allow third-party billing. Google introduced UCB in India in January 2023, and it was expanded to all developers effective 26 Apr 2023 — [MediaNama, Nov 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/); [search summary of LiveLaw/Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/india-news/sc-questions-app-developers-on-opting-out-of-google-s-play-billing-system-126110301862_1.html)
  - In March 2025 the NCLAT upheld the abuse finding and the third-party billing direction but cut the penalty to ₹216.69 crore — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/)
  - The Madras High Court (single judge, then a division bench on 19 Jan 2024) dismissed developers' civil suits against Play Billing and UCB. The Supreme Court admitted appeals in Feb 2024 but refused interim relief. In March 2024 Google removed apps including Matrimony.com, Shaadi.com, Naukri and Kuku FM for non-compliance — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/); [TechCrunch, 29 Feb 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/29/google-calls-out-10-indian-companies-for-evading-play-store-fees-warns-of-app-removal/amp)
  - On 3 Nov 2026 the Supreme Court asked developers whether their contracts let them opt out of Google's payment system. The dispute centres on the 3 Oct 2022 "Authorised Provider" term and Clause 15.3. The next hearing is on 1 Dec 2026 before Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe. The court must also decide whether a civil court or the CCI is the right forum. Google's counsel argued the CCI had examined the fees under Section 27 and found them "neither unreasonable nor excessive" — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/); [Business Standard, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/india-news/sc-questions-app-developers-on-opting-out-of-google-s-play-billing-system-126110301862_1.html); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/competition-law/supreme-court/google-play-billing-case-supreme-court-questions-app-developers-if-they-can-opt-out-of-googles-payment-system-557100)

### Inferences
- **AI credits are digital goods.** Prepaid AI usage is consumed inside the app and sits squarely within "virtual currencies" and "cloud software and services". The Play build must sell them through Play Billing (15%) or UCB (about 11% plus PA fees of roughly 2%, GST handled as above). Linking out to a web checkout from inside the app is not allowed in India; outside-the-app link programmes exist only in specific regions, not India.
- **The 15% tier:** enrol in the 15% tier. Small developers stay under $1M a year, so the effective rate is 15% on consumables (11% under UCB).
- **Bring-your-own-key** (the user pays OpenRouter directly) involves no in-app purchase. That should sit outside Play Billing, but Boop must not steer users to buy credits elsewhere from within the app.
- **Swiggy orders and UPI handoff** are physical goods and services and are exempt from Play Billing.
- **Open-source distribution** (F-Droid, GitHub APK) is outside Play billing rules. A dual-distribution model is possible: Play build with Play Billing or UCB, and a sideload build with direct web purchase. Any future Android developer-verification requirements for sideloaded apps (see Section 5) should be checked.
- **Watch** the Supreme Court hearing on 1 Dec 2026 and any global rollout of the new fee structure to India.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 Google announcement that brings the new 10%+5% fee structure to India, or a date for it.
- I found no Google statement that classifies "AI credits" or "tokens" by name. The classification is by analogy.
- The status of the Supreme Court appeals against the NCLAT order (Google's and CCI's cross-appeals) was not separately confirmed.
- Whether the separate CCI investigation into UCB's fee level (opened in 2024 after developer complaints) has concluded is unclear. Google's counsel's reference to a CCI Section 27 finding is the only data point.

## 5. Google Play policies that apply to an assistant app

### Takeaway
The biggest platform risk for Boop is the Accessibility API. Google says general assistants are not accessibility tools, and it "strictly prohibit[s]" using the API so an app can "autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions". An LLM agent that drives the Swiggy UI through Accessibility would therefore break Play policy. The sanctioned agent route is Android AppFunctions, whose policy compliance date is 14 Apr 2027. A new Contacts Permissions policy (deadline 27 Jan 2027) pushes apps toward the Contact Picker. Every app must complete the Financial features declaration, and AI chat apps must offer in-app reporting of AI content.

### Cited Findings
- **Accessibility API:** eligibility for `isAccessibilityTool` is limited to services "designed to help people with disabilities access their device". The listed non-eligible examples include "automation tools, assistants". "A general assistant that is voice-activated" would not qualify even if it helps some users with disabilities — [Google Play AccessibilityService API policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- **Autonomy ban:** use that "enables an app to autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions or decisions is strictly prohibited" for non-tool apps. "Deterministic, rule-based automation, where behavior follows a static, human-defined script" is allowed — [Google Play AccessibilityService API policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- **Non-tool apps using Accessibility** must show an in-app prominent disclosure during normal use. It must describe the data accessed and how it is used or shared, require an affirmative action, be separate from other disclosures, and not sit only in the privacy policy. They must also file a Play Console declaration with the purpose, data collection and sharing details, and a video of the disclosure flow. Deceptive or undeclared use can lead to suspension or account termination — [Google Play AccessibilityService API policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)
- **AppFunctions API** (agentic integration): announced 29 Oct 2026 with a compliance date of 14 Apr 2027. AppFunctions are "strictly prohibited from deceiving or manipulating AI agents", bypassing security controls, or interfering with other apps — [Play Policy Deadlines](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780?hl=en)
- **Contacts:** a new Contacts Permissions policy for broad contacts access (deadline 27 Jan 2027, announced 15 Apr 2026). Apps that don't need broad access should use the Android Contact Picker. The Manage Contacts permission is restricted to core functionality and needs a Play Console declaration (deadline 25 Aug 2027) — [Play Policy Deadlines](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780?hl=en)
- **Other dated items:** `READ_CALL_LOG` can no longer be used for phone-call verification (27 Jan 2027). Subscriptions with eligibility-based discounts must disclose criteria and post-eligibility pricing (27 Jan 2027). Apps had to be registered in Play Console for Android developer verification by 30 Sep 2026 to avoid "global removal from Google Play" — [Play Policy Deadlines](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/12921780?hl=en)
- **AI-Generated Content policy:** covers text-to-text chatbots where "interacting with the chatbot is a central feature of the app", plus AI image and video. Apps must prevent restricted content such as child exploitation and deceptive behaviour, and "must contain in-app user reporting or flagging features" that work without leaving the app — [Google Play AI-Generated Content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936)
- **Financial features declaration:** "All developers that have an app published on Google Play must complete" it, including testing tracks; apps with no financial features certify none. The categories include "Mobile payments and digital wallets", "Money transfer and wire services", and "Rewards, points…and other incentives". Personal-loan features need extra documentation. It has been mandatory since 31 Aug 2023 — [Play Console Help: Financial features declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271)
- **Sensitive permissions** such as SMS, Call Log and Accessibility need a Permissions Declaration Form, a demo video and a privacy policy naming the data. This is a secondary source — [Forasoft 2026 checklist](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/how-to-get-you-app-approved-on-google-play-and-app-store)

### Inferences
- **Swiggy integration:** use Swiggy's official APIs or partner services (as the plan states) or AppFunctions or intents. Do not use Accessibility-driven UI automation planned by an LLM, which is "strictly prohibited". A deterministic, user-scripted macro could be allowed, but it is still not an accessibility tool, so the prominent disclosure, declaration and video apply.
- **Notifications:** reading them via NotificationListenerService is a user-granted special access. No specific Play policy page was found, but the User Data policy (prominent disclosure, privacy policy, limited use) presumably applies, because notification content is personal and sensitive data. Send-to-cloud behaviour must be disclosed in-app and in the Data safety form.
- **Contacts:** from 27 Jan 2027, broad READ_CONTACTS use will need justification under the new policy. Consider the Contact Picker for "message X" style tasks.
- **Financial features declaration:** Boop must file it. If it only launches the user's UPI app, "My app doesn't provide any financial features" or "Mobile payments" might fit. Google's guidance doesn't say which, so ask Play support.
- **AI content:** add an in-chat "report this response" control for Play compliance.

### Gaps
- I found no Play policy page specific to NotificationListenerService or to the default assistant (ROLE_ASSISTANT) or home role. Whether Google requires a declaration for becoming the default digital assistant is unconfirmed.
- The date Google added the Accessibility autonomy prohibition was not captured.
- I did not research whether Play requires generative-AI apps to restrict minors or apply age ratings specific to AI chat.
- Details of Android developer verification for sideloaded or F-Droid apps outside Play were not researched.

## 6. Consumer protection: dark patterns, subscriptions and credits, refunds and grievances

### Takeaway
The CCPA's Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 (30 Nov 2023, 13 listed patterns including subscription traps, drip pricing and basket sneaking) are being enforced. Nine platforms, among them Zepto, IndiGo and BookMyShow, were fined in August 2026. The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026 take effect on 1 Jan 2027. They make compliance with the guidelines a rule (Rule 4(15)) with a yearly self-audit and a displayed compliance certificate, and require complaint acknowledgement in 48 hours and resolution within a month. Credit packs need clear pricing with no hidden charges, an easy cancel or refund path, and honest auto-renewal.

### Cited Findings
- The CCPA issued the Dark Patterns Guidelines on 30 Nov 2023 under Section 18 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. They list 13 categories, including false urgency, basket sneaking, subscription traps (making cancellation hard or hiding the cancel option), confirm-shaming, drip pricing and trick questions — [K&S Partners newsletter](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/safeguarding-consumer-rights-dark-patterns-guidelines-2023/); [Guidelines text (copy)](https://www.nls.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Dark-Patterns.pdf); [DecodeCivils explainer](https://currentaffairs.decodecivils.com/current-affairs/explainers/dark-patterns-and-drip-pricing-the-ccpas-2023-guidelines-XwpE6tp9542xhdjS6V7TR3we/)
- In June 2025 the CCPA asked e-commerce platforms to self-audit for dark patterns — [DecodeCivils (via search summary)](https://currentaffairs.decodecivils.com/current-affairs/explainers/dark-patterns-and-drip-pricing-the-ccpas-2023-guidelines-XwpE6tp9542xhdjS6V7TR3we/)
- **Enforcement, 6 Aug 2026:** the CCPA fined nine platforms including IndiGo, Zepto and BookMyShow. Reported penalties total about ₹20 lakh. Zepto's was the highest at ₹7 lakh, for drip pricing and basket sneaking, and FirstCry's was ₹2 lakh. Companies were told to stop hidden charges, forced subscriptions, misleading countdown timers and manipulative renewal prompts — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/ccpa-fines-indigo-zepto-bookmyshow-dark-patterns-quick-commerce-penalty-126080600580_1.html); [ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/govt-says-ccpa-imposes-rs-20-lakh-for-dark-patterns-steps-up-action-against-deceptive-online-practices20260806161831/); [Channel IAM](https://en.channeliam.com/2026/08/06/ccpa-action-dark-patterns-digital-platforms-india/). The per-company figures come from news reports and were not reconciled with official orders.
- **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026**, effective 1 Jan 2027 — [S.S. Rana & Co., 17 Sep 2026](https://ssrana.in/articles/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-recalibrating-e-commerce-compliance-across-the-indian-data-privacy-framework/); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/consumer/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/pdf/):
  - Rule 4(4): display the legal name, addresses, and customer-care and grievance-officer contacts (email, landline, mobile).
  - Rule 4(5): acknowledge complaints within 48 hours, give the complainant a copy of the recorded complaint, and resolve within one month.
  - Rule 4(15): comply with the Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023, run a yearly self-audit, and "prominently display a compliance certificate".
- **Penalties:** under Section 20 of the CPA the CCPA can order an end to unfair practices. Under Section 88, failing to comply can bring up to 6 months' imprisonment, a fine of up to ₹20 lakh, or both — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-recalibrating-e-commerce-compliance-across-the-indian-data-privacy-framework/)
- One source said the 2026 dark-pattern provisions were only proposed. Most later sources treat them as notified with effect from 1 Jan 2027 — [search summary; see SuperKalam, 12 Sep 2026](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/12-09-2026/e-commerce-firms-brought-under-tighter-regulation-pg10-5d95a8a1-9126-40f6-8871-0bf96e450c04)

### Inferences
- **Coverage:** Boop's web or in-app sale of credit packs is likely "e-commerce" (selling digital products or services over a digital network). From 1 Jan 2027 Boop would need the Rule 4(4) disclosures, a Grievance Officer with the 48-hour and 1-month timelines, and a yearly dark-pattern self-audit with a displayed certificate.
- **Credit and subscription design:** show the all-in price including GST up front, to avoid drip pricing. Do not pre-select larger packs (basket sneaking or interface interference). Do not use countdown pressure (false urgency). If plans auto-renew, cancellation must be as easy as sign-up (subscription trap). Disclose expiry of credits before purchase. Do not use guilt copy on cancellation (confirm-shaming). From my recollection, the guidelines' 13 patterns also include "SaaS billing" (silent recurring charges, including trial-to-paid conversion), which bears directly on auto-top-ups. This needs verifying against the Annexure.
- **Refunds:** no statutory refund mandate specific to unused digital credits was found. Play purchases follow Google's refund processes. For direct sales, a published refund policy for unused credits plus the E-Commerce Rules grievance timelines is the practical baseline.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the full list of the 13 dark patterns from the primary PDF in this pass. "SaaS billing" and "rogue malware" are recalled, not verified.
- The notification date and Gazette number of the E-Commerce (Amendment) Rules 2026 were not found. The effective date of 1 Jan 2027 comes from secondary sources.
- I found no CCPA guidance specific to AI-credit or token pricing, and no specific statutory refund rule for digital goods.
- Consumer Protection Act Section 21 penalties for misleading ads (₹10 lakh, or ₹50 lakh for repeat offences, from memory) were not re-verified.
