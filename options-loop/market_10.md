## New facts that bear on v9
- **NPCI unveiled the Unified Agent Protocol (UAP) at GFF, 8–11 Sep 2026.**
  - Agents can pay without per-transaction approval, within delegated caps. It builds on UPI Circle limits (₹5k per transaction, ₹15k per month, which may change).
  - It is **not live yet**: RBI approval and the liability framework are still pending.
  - NPCI also launched **AtOM**, which gives UPI agent flows signed, machine-readable audit trails.
  - Sources: [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/npci-to-launch-agentic-payments-on-upi-report/), [MediaNama GFF](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026/), [explainer](https://www.indianpaycalculator.in/govt-news/ai-agents-upi-payments-npci-unified-agent-protocol-2026)
- **Others at GFF are shipping agent spend limits themselves.** Amazon Pay's Smart Wallet has agents paying on UPI with biometrics, and Pine Labs lets agents book within user-set spend limits. [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026/)

## What changes (3 lines)
1. **Drop the payment spend-limit gate from the action gate.** In India, payment spend-limits and audit are being built into the rails (Reserve Pay, UAP, AtOM) and by Amazon Pay and Pine Labs. The gate's defensible part is the **non-payment actions**: send, share PII, delete, cart/address changes. Also include the "what happens before the payment" review of the merchant's MCP (Swiggy/Zepto/CRED) against UAP delegation. Pitch reviews as **"UAP-readiness for your MCP"**.
2. **Lower the runway-engine rate assumption to $35–50/h.** At ~2.5 days/week that is still about ₹2.5–3L/month, so the 31 Dec floor test of ≥₹1.5L/month stands. Everything else in v9 checks out: PolicyLayer, prices, free scanners, YC 2 Nov, EF terms and the DMA dates.
3. **Warm buyers:** Swiggy, Zepto, Razorpay and CRED have real action-capable MCP servers, but PhonePe has only a docs MCP, so drop it from the list. Only audit their *open-source* repos (Razorpay, Swiggy manifest) or bug-bounty scopes. EF requires a Delaware C-Corp to invest, which is fine to note but not a blocker.
