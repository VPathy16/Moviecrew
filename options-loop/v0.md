# "We're out of options" — v0 (the founder's thesis, to be attacked)

## The thesis (founder's words)
"I don't think there is anything we can do. We cannot focus on India, we cannot get into a crowded market,
we cannot fight the tech companies. We are literally out of options except for a miracle."

## Constraints the founder has stated
1. Not India-only (market judged too hard to monetise / too slow).
2. No crowded markets (food delivery assistants, dating apps, job sites, group planning all rejected as crowded).
3. No head-on fight with big tech (Google, Apple, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, and funded startups like Instinct).

## What the founder has (assets)
- A working Android assistant app (UNO), Kotlin/Compose, solo founder + AI coding help, no funding mentioned.
- Real, tested tech:
  - Privacy architecture: the AI plans, the phone executes; personal data never reaches the AI
    (references like HOME_1); approvals and spending rules enforced on the device.
  - A service-agnostic MCP pipeline: any MCP server's tools are classified by role (order, book, cancel,
    payment, personal data…), hidden/gated appropriately, with a per-service privacy gateway.
  - Background "vanaras": scoped sub-agents each locked to their own tools, with schedules, jobs, handover.
  - Voice (Gemini Live), BYO AI key (Gemini/Claude/OpenAI), Swiggy MCP integration, on-device UPI flow.
- Ideas explored and dropped: UNO on WhatsApp (Meta bans general AI assistants on the Business API),
  person-to-person agents / group plans (Instinct, Meta Muse, ChatGPT Groups exist), agent dating, agent job matching.

## Question for the loop
Is the thesis true? If not, what specific, non-crowded, non-India-dependent opportunity — one that big tech
structurally won't or can't do — fits these assets? Each iteration should replace this file's "best answer"
with a sharper, more defensible one, or conclude honestly that the thesis holds.
