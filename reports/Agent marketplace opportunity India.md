# Build the seller's agent, defer the marketplace

**Short answer: there is a real product opportunity, but it is not an "agent marketplace" yet.** The part with evidence behind it is a seller's agent for Indian small businesses: it answers enquiries at once, quotes from the owner's own price list, and records an order only after the owner confirms. The part without evidence is the marketplace, where independently owned agents discover and bargain with each other. No scaled live example of that exists anywhere; the closest real instance is a one-week experiment among 69 employees of one company ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)). The best-known attempt to let people buy inside a general assistant was withdrawn after about five months ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/openai-and-google-refine-early-ai-commerce-strategies/)). In India the behaviour is not in doubt (customers message small sellers and expect a chat reply), but the size of the pain and sellers' willingness to pay are unproven, the last wave of small-seller software mostly failed on exactly that point, and Meta launched its own AI agent inside the WhatsApp Business app in May 2026 ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses-in-india/)). The recommendation is to add a seller's agent on the pages your assistant already builds, reached by link or QR, for sellers who quote prices (local services and made-to-order goods), with owner confirmation, no handling of money and no commission. Add a "get me three quotes" buyer feature inside your own app only once enough sellers in one city and category are reachable. Confidence: high that the open marketplace is premature; medium that the seller's agent is a workable wedge; low on what sellers will pay.

A note on the evidence. Five researchers produced the notes behind this report on 17 November 2026. Most figures come from search summaries of the linked pages, and only a handful of primary documents were read in full. Where a number rests on a vendor, on Meta describing its own product, or on a search summary, the text says so. Passages marked "Inference" are reasoning by the researchers or by this report, not sourced fact. Nothing here is legal advice.

## The plumbing exists; the marketplace itself does not

**Protocols and standards (confidence: high that they exist, medium on the detail).** A founder does not need to invent a way for agents to talk. Agent2Agent (A2A), the open standard for one agent to find and hand work to another, reached a stable version 1.0 and **more than 150 supporting organisations** by April 2026, including AWS, Google, Microsoft, Salesforce and SAP ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)). It covers signed identity cards, discovery and task delegation. It does not cover price negotiation or payment, and the adoption it reports is inside large enterprises, with no named production customers ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)). The Model Context Protocol (MCP), which connects an agent to tools and data, was donated to a Linux Foundation body in December 2025 ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/anthropic-donates-mcp-to-new-agentic-ai-foundation)).

For buying and paying there are at least four overlapping standards: OpenAI and Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol, Google and Shopify's Universal Commerce Protocol announced in January 2026 ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/ai-commerce-at-scale)), Google's Agent Payments Protocol with more than 60 payments firms behind it ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)), and the card networks' own schemes. Visa reports live agent purchases in Europe with 31 issuers, at pilot scale ([Visa](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/visa/pressreleases/visa-and-banks-across-europe-reach-the-next-phase-of-agentic-commerce-unlocking-secure-access-to-merchants-3457328)). The researchers did not read any of these specifications at source, and sources disagree on whether the card schemes interoperate with Google's protocol and on who now governs it ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14839407-agent-payment-protocols-compared); [BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/03/13/ai-agent-commerce-standards-war-ucp-x402-visa-mastercard/)). Crypto payment rails for agents are not evidence of demand: after removing wash and test activity, Visa's own analyst put real volume on the main one at about $19 million ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/converge/infrastructure/visa-s-sheffield-pegs-adjusted-x402-volume-at-19m)). Agent identity ("know your agent") has several competing efforts and no settled standard ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03095v3)).

Inference: every payment standard builds in some form of owner consent, so "the owner confirms" matches where the rails are heading and is not a differentiator. What no adopted standard covers is multi-round bargaining between two parties' agents, reputation for small sellers' agents, and disputes. A marketplace would have to write those rules itself.

**Agent stores and directories (confidence: medium).** The stores that exist are catalogues where people buy agents, not markets where agents deal with each other. AWS launched an agents category with more than 900 listings in July 2025; Microsoft, Google and Salesforce have equivalents, with no later usage figures found ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AWS-AI-agent-marketplace-store-cloud/753238/?.tsrc=rss)). The consumer cautionary tale is OpenAI's GPT Store, where the promised revenue share reached only a small invited group of US creators ([OpenAI community forum](https://community.openai.com/t/guidance-for-gpt-store-builders-outside-the-us/1357911)). The researchers did not search specifically for startup "agents hire agents" marketplaces, so their absence here is a gap, not proof that none exist.

**Consumer launches by large platforms (confidence: high on the direction).** The clearest market test was negative. OpenAI ended ChatGPT's Instant Checkout in March 2026 and moved purchases into retailers' own apps inside ChatGPT ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/openai-drops-chatgpt-checkout-to-pursue-agent-vision)). Merchant counts conflict, from about 12 to about 30 to 47 depending on the source, but all agree it was tens and not thousands ([Naturaily](https://naturaily.com/blog/agentic-commerce-shopify-guide); [Ryze](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/agentic-commerce-protocol-after-instant-checkout-what-survived)). Walmart said checkout inside the chat converted at **about one-third** the rate of its own website, then put its own assistant inside ChatGPT instead ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/walmarts-chatgpt-checkout-flopped-heres-what-comes-next/)). What does work is AI-assisted discovery with the retailer keeping checkout: Amazon reports hundreds of millions of users of its shopping assistant (company figures relayed by trade press, and the numbers differ by date) ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-ai-shopping-assistants--what-s-driving-adoption-how-brands-win-visibility)).

Large marketplaces resist outside buying agents. Amazon blocks them in its site rules ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-expands-its-fight-to-keep-ai-bots-off-its-e-commerce-site/)), and eBay's user agreement bars agents that place orders without human review unless approved ([EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2026/01/21/ebay-bans-ai-shopping-agents-updates-arbitration-provision/)). Amazon won a court order against Perplexity's shopping agent in March 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-10/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexity-s-ai-shopping-bots)), which an appeals court vacated in August 2026 ([Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/newsletters/us-court-overturns-amazon-injunction-perplexity)). Inference: a network built on sellers who choose to publish their own agent avoids this fight; one that sends agents into closed platforms inherits it.

**India's rails (confidence: medium to high).** The Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC), a government-backed network built on the Beckn protocol in which separate buyer apps and seller apps interoperate, is alive but is not where small retail sellers are winning. Volume was about 9 million transactions a month in June 2026 and over 500 million in total by July 2026, driven by mobility, ticketing and logistics ([Cross-Border Magazine](https://cross-border-magazine.com/ondc-500-million-transactions-india/)). A second report gives 1.2 crore orders a month on an unclear basis ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/ondc-onboards-over-6-lakh-sellers-enables-1-2-crore-orders-per-month-3090094)). Retail orders peaked at 6.5 million a month in October 2024 and fell to 4.6 million by February 2025 after incentives were cut; PhonePe's buyer app left the network and the chief executive departed ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=509526)). No retail-only figures for 2026 were found. Beckn's authors argue it can be the contracting layer when two agents talk, and an open-source implementation with Google's agent kit exists, but these are statements and demos, with no official agent standard and no usage data ([Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/when-two-ai-agents-communicate-beckn-can-be-the-contracting-infrastructure/); [Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-11-03-Beckn-Labs-and-Google-Cloud-Partner-to-Accelerate-the-Adoption-of-Open-Networks-Worldwide-with-Beckn-Onix)). Inference: ONDC is a possible distribution channel later, through an existing seller app, and not a source of customers now.

UPI is the strong rail. It processed **24.51 billion transactions in August 2026** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/upi-mdr-regime-explained-heres-what-will-be-charged-after-oct-15/)). A framework announced in September 2026 puts a 0.4% merchant fee on UPI payments above ₹2,000, while keeping payments of ₹2,000 or less, and small vendors receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month, at zero ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/upi-mdr-rules-rs12-on-rs3000-rs200-on-rs50000-and-rs300-cap-on-rs75000-payments-check-faqs-555724-2026-09-15)). Whether it is in force is unverified: a headline reports the 15 October start was deferred until after Diwali ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/npci-defers-upi-mdr-rollout-amid-festive-season-select-merchant-payments-to-attract-charges-postdiwali/81931.html)). Payment started by an agent is arriving but early: Razorpay and the payments body NPCI launched agent payments on Claude for Zomato, Swiggy and Zepto in February 2026 ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/newsroom/razorpay-npci-launch-agentic-payments-on-claude-powering-zomato-swiggy-zepto-at-the-india-ai-impact-summit/)), and NPCI is reported to be preparing its own agent protocol, with no confirmed launch, limits or liability rules ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/india-plans-ai-powered-upi-payments-framework-through-unified-agent-protocol)).

WhatsApp is both the main channel and the main competitor. Meta launched "Business AI" inside the WhatsApp Business app for Indian small businesses in May 2026 (the notes give both 7 and 14 May): it answers customers around the clock from the profile and catalogue, captures leads, books appointments, hands over to the owner and works in Indian languages; eligibility and price were not stated, and UPI payment in the chat was promised "soon" ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses-in-india/)). There is no independent data on its reach or quality.

## Demand is real for answering, unproven for delegated buying and bargaining

**What supports demand.** About 15 million small businesses in India use the WhatsApp Business app, a Meta-originated and undated figure ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/how-whatsapp-business-is-bringing-indias-smes-to-the-digital-fold/)). Sellers already pay real money for enquiries: IndiaMART has about 2.2 lakh paying suppliers at roughly **₹67,000 a year each** ([Quartr](https://quartr.com/events/indiamart-intermesh-limited-indiamart-q4-25-26_3PjDiEE6)), and Justdial had 631,530 active paid campaigns at the end of its 2025-26 financial year ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/just-dial-q4-results-operating-revenue-hits-307-cr-profit-dips-on-treasury-drag)). Negotiation bots work in one narrow form: a large buyer's bot bargaining with human suppliers over small contracts. Walmart's supplier bot closed deals with 68% of suppliers at average savings of 3%, on self-reported figures from 2021 to 2023 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2023/walmart-finds-75-percent-vendors-prefer-negotiating-with-chatbot/partial/)). In Anthropic's experiment, 46% of participants said they would pay for an agent that trades for them ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)).

Surveys show Indians are unusually open to AI in shopping, but they measure stated intent on online panels and are not comparable with each other. Adobe reports 58% of Indian consumers comfortable with agent-to-agent interactions ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/65-of-indian-shoppers-trust-ai-recommendations-highest-in-asia-pacific-adobe-report-4061340)). Against that, Ipsos found that globally only **9% of consumers let an AI make purchases on its own** ([Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2026-07/ipsos-views-shopping-with-AI.pdf)). Inference: authority to check out is the trust bottleneck, which favours keeping the human as the one who confirms.

**What failed or stalled, and why.** The pattern across failures is consistent. In-chat checkout stalled because people researched but did not buy there, and retailers wanted to keep checkout and customer data (Walmart's one-third conversion, above). Consumer agent stores stalled because supply was easy and discovery and payment to creators were not (GPT Store, above). ONDC retail stalled because demand had been bought with incentives, quick-commerce firms took the grocery use case, and low commissions left buyer apps without a business ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=509526); [Finshots](https://finshots.in/archive/has-the-ondc-experiment-failed-nrai-rapido-t-koshy-swiggy-zomato-food-delivery/)).

India's 2020-2022 wave of small-seller software failed on willingness to pay. Dukaan moved from kirana shops to brands "willing to pay for software tools" and cut about 30% of staff ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/exclusive-lightspeed-backed-dukaan-lays-off-30-workforce)). Bikayi's revenue fell from about ₹1.9 crore to under ₹10 lakh a month within three months of 2022, amid allegations that salespeople forged signatures to enrol shop owners in instalment plans; one executive said no more than 20,000 of India's small businesses can run online stores ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/bikayi-in-disarray-startup-hit-by-fraud-allegations-seller-exodus/)). Khatabook shut its store builder ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/khatabook-fires-over-40-employees-restructuring-exercise/amp)). Inference: micro sellers will not pay a recurring software fee sold by a field team; anything for them needs near-zero acquisition cost and a price tied to money earned.

**What experiments say about agent-run markets (confidence: high on direction, low on magnitudes).** All of this evidence is from simulations or one small pilot. Microsoft's simulated market found agents picked the first offer they received 60-100% of the time, so speed of reply beat price and quality ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1)). In Anthropic's pilot, 186 real deals worth just over $4,000 were struck; the same item sold for $3.64 more when the stronger model sold it, and people represented by the weaker model rated their deals as equally fair, so they did not notice ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)). Owner instructions such as "be aggressive" had no significant effect ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)). A Stanford-affiliated simulation found buyers with weaker agents paid around 2% more ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/the-art-of-the-automated-negotiation)); one researcher's note also records seller losses of up to 14%, which another could not verify. Pricing agents told only to maximise profit drifted to above-competitive prices on their own ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00806v4)), though a consultancy reports smaller experiments pointing the other way ([Keystone](https://www.keystone.com/news-publications/policing-an-invisible-handshake)).

Analyst forecasts for 2030 run from Bain's $300-500 billion for the US ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/ko/insights/2030-forecast-how-agentic-ai-will-reshape-us-retail-snap-chart/)) to McKinsey's $3-5 trillion globally ([McKinsey](https://mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-agentic-commerce-opportunity-how-ai-agents-are-ushering-in-a-new-era-for-consumers-and-merchants)). They differ mainly in what counts as "agentic", and mostly count AI-influenced discovery. Inference: the gap between these forecasts and 2026 operating results is the core of the case that an open agent marketplace is premature.

## Small sellers sell through chat, and their willingness to pay is thin

Chat is the default sales channel. A Meta-commissioned Bain survey of 7,800 Indian users found about 60% message a business account every week; a later Kantar study cited by Meta says 91% of online adults do. The two differ in sample, wording and year, and both are Meta-linked ([Meta 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/05/genai-powered-conversational-commerce-to-fuel-business-growth-in-india/); [Meta 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses-in-india/)). The segment is huge by count and thin by ability to pay. India has **7.34 crore informal enterprises, of which only 26.7% use the internet for business** ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/economy/news/employment-up-10-unincorporated-establishments-rise-12-28-govt-survey-124122400965_1.html); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/annual-survey-of-unincorporated-sector-enterprises-2023-24)). Internet use ranges from 40.6% among urban male-owned establishments to 9.4% among rural female-owned ones ([CEDA, Ashoka University](https://ceda.ashoka.edu.in/from-ownership-to-operations-mapping-gender-inequality-in-indias-informal-enterprises/)). The average order on Meesho, the largest value marketplace, was ₹265 in 2025-26 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/meesho-reports-43-growth-to-11371-crore-losses-narrow-sharply-on-ai-led-efficiency-gains)).

The numbers a founder most wants do not exist in reliable form. No independent Indian survey was found on enquiries per day, hours spent replying, or orders lost to slow replies. The figures in circulation (60% of owners losing a customer a week, 84% conversion when replies come within five minutes, 4.5 hours a day on WhatsApp) come from vendor blogs and should not be quoted ([Groweon](https://www.groweon.com/blog/indian-smbs-lose-leads-without-whatsapp-crm/); [Botsense](https://botsenseio.blogspot.com/2026/02/whatsapp-api-provider-business-hyderabad.html)). The only named data point is a testimonial chosen by Meta: one seller handling 60-70 queries a day and closing 6-7 orders ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses-in-india/)). Inference: that implies about one order per ten enquiries, a real time burden, but it is a single hand-picked case.

For local services, discovery runs on paid lead platforms, and provider complaints are consistent: the same lead sold to several providers, junk leads, annual prepaid packages. A Bengaluru consumer commission held Justdial liable for failing to supply leads a customer had paid for ([LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/consumer-cases/bangalore-district-commission-holds-justdial-liable-for-failure-to-provide-customer-leads-despite-receiving-payment-by-coaching-institute-249801)). Indicative prices of ₹15,000-50,000 a year come from comparison blogs, not independent sources ([Vedain](https://vedain.com/blog/indiamart-vs-justdial-which-lead-source-works-better-for-indian-smbs)). Inference: when a lead goes to several providers, the first credible reply with a price tends to win. This is the clearest place where an instant-reply agent has measurable value, and where the seller already spends money. Consumers also have no neutral way to collect comparable quotes from independent providers.

Language is a minimum requirement, not an edge. Meta's agent supports Indian languages, Flipkart launched a voice-enabled WhatsApp ordering bot for small retailers, and Meesho says it is betting on voice ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses-in-india/); [MIT Sloan Management Review Middle East](https://www.mitsloanme.com/?p=9740); [CIO Elets](https://cio.eletsonline.com/?p=75843)). No measurement was found of how much business chat is in Indian languages or by voice note.

## Abuse is the default; safeguards have to sit outside the AI model

**Manipulation (confidence: high).** Every well-known public incident involved a user talking a bot into saying something the business never authorised. A US car dealer's chatbot "agreed" to sell a vehicle for $1; no sale happened because the bot could not conclude deals ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/a-chevy-for-1-car-dealer-chatbots-show-perils-of-ai-for-customer-service)). When Air Canada's chatbot gave a customer wrong refund information, a Canadian tribunal held the airline responsible and rejected the argument that the bot was a separate entity ([American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business%5Flaw/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-information-provided-ai-chatbot/)). Defences inside the model do not hold: a 2025 study tested 12 published defences with adaptive attacks and found attack success above 95% against defences that had looked near-perfect on fixed tests ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09023)). A 2026 benchmark, known here only through press coverage, found hidden instructions in ordinary web content succeeded roughly 42-68% of the time, with attacks aimed at sellers the most successful ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4184455/prompt-injection-breaks-todays-ai-agents-study-warns.html)). No verified case was found of a deployed negotiating agent losing real money this way; the evidence is benchmarks and pranks.

Inference: the safeguards with evidence behind them are price floors and discount caps enforced in ordinary code, a short list of permitted actions, and human confirmation for anything irreversible. Instructions in the prompt ("never go below X") reduce casual abuse and fail under determined attack.

**Leaking the owner's limits (confidence: medium; preprints).** Research in 2026 shows negotiating agents reveal their owner's private limits, both when asked directly and through the pattern of their concessions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30383); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06815)). Inference: keep the floor price out of the model altogether. The model proposes an offer and a rules layer answers accept, reject or counter, so the model cannot reveal what it never saw.

**Fraud in India (confidence: high on scale).** Reported cyber-fraud losses were about ₹22,845 crore in 2024 ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/cyber-fraud-costs-indians-22-845-crore-in-2024-government)); 2025 totals conflict, at ₹22,495 crore or ₹19,813 crore depending on scope ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/cybercrime-saw-24-spike-in-2025-indians-lost-rs-22495-crore-mainly-in-investment-scams/2859930); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/indians-lose-over-rs-52976-crore-to-cyber-frauds-over-six-years-report--20260103154943)). UPI fraud ran at 12.64 lakh incidents and ₹981 crore in 2024-25 ([Lok Sabha reply](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/3018042/1/AU2481_5bIzKk.pdf)). The regulator's habit is to remove features that let a stranger pull money: person-to-person UPI "collect" requests ended on 1 October 2025 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/banking/story/npci-to-tighten-upi-rules-peer-to-peer-collect-feature-to-end-in-october-489244-2025-08-13)). Impersonators have appeared with blue ticks and business badges ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/fraud-alert-big-online-sale-cybercriminals-are-also-shopping-for-victims/article/fraud-alert-sbi-interest-certificates-and-mahanagar-gas-disconnection/80683.html)). No statistics were found on scammers using AI chat agents in India. Inference: a platform that gives anyone a polite, tireless, plausibly branded shopfront is attractive to scammers, so seller verification must come before public reach.

**Disclosure (confidence: medium).** A 2019 field experiment found that telling customers up front they were speaking to a bot cut purchases by more than 79.7%; it involved voice calls at one firm before generative AI, so its present-day relevance is uncertain ([Marketing Science](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:38:y:2019:i:6:p:937-947)). No Indian rule was found that requires a text chatbot to announce itself, but a 2024 government advisory points that way and the EU requires it ([AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/India-AI-Regulatory-Tracker-2nd-Ed-Correct-Final.pdf)). Inference: disclose always, and pair it with signs of competence (a named business, accurate answers, quick handover to the owner).

## Indian law binds the owner; the open question is what the platform is

None of the points below has been tested in an Indian court for AI agents. Treat each as a prediction to put to a lawyer.

**Offers and orders (confidence: medium).** India has no statute on "electronic agents" of the kind the US has, and two 2026 commentaries found no reported Indian case on a contract concluded by a bot ([IJIRL](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/lawof-agency-ai-agent-autonomous-ai-agent-bind-humanprincipal-547909)). The Information Technology Act says a contract is not unenforceable merely because it was formed electronically, and attributes a message to the person whose system was programmed to send it automatically ([IJIRL](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)). The commentary concludes the person who deploys the agent is very likely bound, and the other side protected, particularly through the rule that a counterparty may rely on apparent authority ([IJIRL](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)). Inference: owner confirmation moves the binding act back to a human click, which removes most of the doubt. It does not excuse what the agent said beforehand. A wrong price, stock level or delivery date still exposes the seller, so the agent must never say "confirmed" or "paid" before the owner acts.

**Consumer and e-commerce rules (confidence: medium; the most important question).** The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 define a "marketplace e-commerce entity" as one that provides a technology platform "to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers". The definition has no payment element, so not handling money does not take a platform outside it ([Gazette text](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)). Every e-commerce entity must name a grievance officer who acknowledges complaints within 48 hours and resolves them within a month, and must record consent to buy through an explicit, affirmative action ([Gazette text](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)). An amendment notified on 9 September 2026 takes effect on **1 January 2027**. On a law firm's reading it adds a yearly dark-pattern self-audit with a displayed certificate, mandatory link-up with the National Consumer Helpline, disclosure of ranking criteria with paid placement marked, and display of each seller's GST or Udyam number ([Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)). The gazette text was not opened. Inference: seller-branded pages with no cross-seller search or ranking look more like a software tool; a buyer agent that finds, compares or ranks sellers makes marketplace status hard to avoid. That is a legal reason, on top of the commercial ones, to ship the seller side first.

**Hosting public chats (confidence: high on the rules, medium on how they apply).** A service hosting other people's content keeps legal protection only by following the intermediary rules, which since 20 February 2026 require removal within **3 hours** of a government or court order and labelling of realistic AI-generated audio and images ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/law-policy/centre-amends-it-rules-to-regulate-ai-generated-content-takedown-timelines-reduced)). Text chat falls outside the synthetic-content definition ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/)). Whether a platform's own AI output counts as third-party content is treated as open ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/india/trends-and-developments)). Inference: assume no protection for what the agent itself says, and manage that with grounding, logs and owner sign-off.

**Data protection (confidence: high on staging).** The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified in November 2025. The main duties (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, children's data) apply from May 2027 ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/)). The government consulted in January 2026 on bringing them forward to November 2026; whether that was notified could not be confirmed, although a major firm still wrote "May 2027" in September 2026 ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/Ergo_E-Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules_18%20September%202026.pdf)). The party that decides why data is collected stays accountable even when a vendor processes it ([Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/DPDPA.pdf)). Inference: for a customer's name, address and order, the seller is the accountable party and your app its processor, but micro sellers cannot write notices, so the app must supply them as product features. The app becomes directly accountable for anything it does with that data for itself, such as model training or cross-seller profiles. One commentator argues an agent cannot validly give privacy consent for its user ([IJIRL](https://ijirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/6.-AGENTS-WITHOUT-PERSONS-ATTRIBUTING-AI-MADE-CONTRACTS-UNDER-INDIAN-LAW.pdf)).

**Payments (confidence: medium).** The Reserve Bank's 2025 directions regulate firms that receive and settle customers' money for merchants, and non-bank operators need authorisation ([RBI](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/rbi-issues-the-reserve-bank-of-india-regulation-of-payment-aggregators-directions-2025)). Inference: showing the seller's own UPI ID or QR, so the buyer pays from their own UPI app and money moves bank to bank, should fall outside that definition. This is the standard reading, but no regulator statement confirming it was found. The agent cannot see the seller's bank credit, so it must never claim payment was received.

**Messaging, sector limits and AI policy.** WhatsApp's business terms have barred general-purpose AI assistants since 15 January 2026, while bots for a business's own support, sales and orders remain allowed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform/?source_type=web)). Inference: a seller's order bot on the seller's own number is within policy; a buyer's assistant that shops across sellers inside WhatsApp is not, so that feature belongs in your own app. Telecom rules on commercial calls and SMS do not yet treat AI calling as its own category, and a 2026 draft amendment is in consultation ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/trais-crackdown-on-spam-calls-and-ai-driven-telemarketing)). India has no notified e-pharmacy rules and regulators have acted against unlicensed online medicine sales ([Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/e-pharmacies-on-regulating-online-sale-of-drugs-in-india/)). The fuller list of restricted categories in the notes comes from the researcher's background knowledge and needs a lawyer. India has no AI Act; its November 2025 governance guidelines are voluntary ([JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/?p=55514)).

## A text chat costs under ₹2; pricing power is the constraint

**WhatsApp Business Platform (confidence: high on the model, medium on rupee rates).** Meta charges per delivered message. Since 1 October 2026, free-form replies inside the 24-hour window after a customer writes are no longer free: each business phone number gets **1,000 free service messages a month**, then pays ([Meta pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md)). India rates come from vendor transcriptions of Meta's rate card, which agree with each other: about ₹0.115 per service or utility message and ₹0.8631 per marketing message ([InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-2026-india-october-update)). Higher figures in circulation are mostly reseller markups. A seller can keep an existing WhatsApp Business app number and connect the same number to the platform ([Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users)). Meta's own agent for larger businesses bills at $2 per million tokens, which Meta estimates at 4-5 US cents a message ([Meta pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md)). Reseller subscriptions run about ₹1,500-3,500 a month on third-party figures ([codingclave](https://codingclave.com/blog/interakt-pricing-india-2026)). Inference: platform dependence is high. In 16 months Meta changed its pricing model, began charging for previously free replies, banned a category of bots and launched a competing agent.

**Web chat link or QR.** No per-message platform fee and no platform policy risk. The researchers found no independent data on whether Indian customers will chat on an unfamiliar web page, or how such pages convert against WhatsApp. Inference: the web gives up the ability to message the customer later unless a phone number is collected; a hybrid page that also offers a button to the seller's own WhatsApp number is the practical compromise.

**Voice (confidence: high on list prices).** Sarvam lists ₹3.50 a minute for a voice agent, with speech and language model bundled, plus about ₹0.20 per 30 seconds of telephony ([Sarvam](https://docs.sarvam.ai/voice-agents/pricing.md)); other vendors quote ₹3-6 ([Ringg](https://www.ringg.ai/ai-voice-agent-pricing)). Disclosed traction for Indian voice-AI firms is in banks and enterprises, not small business ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/bolna-nabs-6-3-million-from-general-catalyst-for-its-india-focused-voice-orchestration-platform/?rand=324)).

**Model cost.** Small models list at about $0.10 per million input tokens (Claude Haiku 5.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite) against $2 for mid-range and $8 for the top tier ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Google](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).

The table below is the researchers' own arithmetic, not sourced fact. It assumes a sales chat of about ten agent replies on a small model with caching, and a three-minute call. No production token logs were found, and Meta's own estimate of tokens per message is four to five times higher, so treat these as a lean case.

| Channel | Estimated cost per enquiry | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Web chat link or QR | about ₹0.2-0.6 | No way to re-contact the customer; conversion unknown |
| WhatsApp, seller's own number | about ₹0.2-1.8 | Free while under 1,000 replies a month per number; Meta can change terms |
| Inbound voice call | about ₹11-18 | Roughly ten times text; suits high-value enquiries only |

Inference: at one order per ten enquiries, the cost per order won is about ₹2-18 on text and ₹110-180 on voice.

**Business models (confidence: medium).** A commission on orders is the hardest model to introduce in India now. Flipkart and Amazon have moved to zero commission on items under ₹1,000 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/03/amazon-india-expands-zero-referral-fees-125-crore-products-flipkarts-commission-waiver)), while more than 1,000 Bengaluru restaurant owners threatened to delist from food apps over commissions of 8-28% ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/07/30/wont-take-orders-anymore-why-bengaluru-restaurants-are-threatening-to-boycott-zomato-swiggy.amp.html)). The models Indian small businesses demonstrably pay for are fixed subscriptions for lead flow and prepaid usage. Small monthly plans suffer involuntary churn: a payment firm reports more than 20 million UPI AutoPay mandates revoked each month ([Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/saas-payment-failures-india/)). No disclosed revenue was found for any Indian product selling AI agents to micro sellers, and no evidence either way that ₹199-999 a month works for an answering agent.

Inference: delivery cost is not the problem; a seller with 300 chats a month costs roughly ₹60-540 to serve. The problem is that sellers pay for new customers, not for answering. That points to a free or near-free basic agent, prepaid packs rather than auto-debit, and charging later for quote requests delivered, with "no commission, your customer stays yours" as the pitch.

## Recommendation: a quoting front desk on pages you already build

Everything in this section is this report's judgement, built on the evidence above.

**The first product.** Give sellers among your existing users a public page with a chat that acts as their front desk. Your assistant already builds pages, your gateway server can host the conversation, and the owner already has your app, which is the natural place to approve things. The agent answers only from the price list, stock notes and policies the owner supplied, takes the customer's requirement, prepares a draft quote or order, and sends it to the owner's phone. Nothing is confirmed until the owner taps to confirm. The customer then sees a final summary and pays the seller directly using the seller's own UPI ID. The receive-only mailbox could serve as the inbox for incoming requests; the research did not examine this.

**The wedge.** Start with sellers who quote prices: tailors, caterers, home bakers, tutors, repair and other local services, and made-to-order goods, in one city. Four reasons. These sellers already pay for leads and lose them to whoever replies first. Order values are high enough to bear the cost. Meta's agent is built around a catalogue of fixed-price products, so quoting and owner-set negotiation rules are where a third party can differ. And the eventual buyer feature, collecting comparable quotes, fills a gap consumers face today.

**Sequencing.** Step one is the seller's agent alone, useful the moment any human customer opens the link, with no buyer agent needed. Step two, only after a few hundred active sellers exist in one city and category, is "get me three quotes" inside your own app, sent only to sellers who opted in, exchanged as structured quotes (item, price, terms) and confirmed by a human on each side. Step three, open discovery between agents, waits for evidence from step two and for the legal answers below. Adopt A2A-style agent cards and exportable consent records when you get there instead of inventing a protocol.

**What to avoid.** An open directory or marketplace at launch. Bargaining with no owner in the loop. Holding or routing money, or generating payment requests. A commission on orders. A field sales team. A buyer assistant inside WhatsApp, or many sellers behind one shared WhatsApp number. Outbound AI voice calls. Regulated categories such as medicines, alcohol, tobacco and financial products. Crypto rails. Dependence on ONDC for customers. Paid placement in anything the buyer's agent recommends.

**Safeguards from day one.** These are not optional extras. The agent labels itself as an AI assistant for a named seller and never claims to be human. Owner confirmation precedes every commitment, and the agent cannot say "confirmed" or "paid" on its own. Answers come only from seller-supplied data, with a refusal when the data is silent, and no invented discounts or urgency. Price floors and discount caps live in code and never in the model's instructions. Sellers are verified before their page goes public (phone, then bank or UPI payee name matching the business, then GST, Udyam or food-licence number where relevant), with what was checked shown to buyers. The UPI ID is locked to the verified seller and never taken from chat. New sellers get daily caps on conversations and order value that rise with history. Every chat carries a one-tap report, and you hold a kill switch for any page plus a legal-order inbox watched around the clock. Each customer conversation is isolated from every other. A privacy notice appears before personal details are collected, with deletion tools and stated retention. Agents contact only people who wrote first or sellers who opted in. If agents ever compare offers, batch and randomise them so the fastest reply does not win by default, and give every user the same model quality in negotiation. Keep time-stamped records of who confirmed what.

**Questions for a lawyer.** First, is the app a "marketplace e-commerce entity" for the seller agent, the buyer agent and agent-to-agent dealing respectively, and does seller branding with no cross-seller discovery change the answer? Second, is the app a protected intermediary for what a seller-configured agent says, or the publisher of its own AI output? Third, how is data-protection responsibility split between seller, buyer and platform, what may the platform do with chat data, how should children be handled on public pages, and have the core duties been brought forward from May 2027? Fourth, does displaying a seller's UPI ID, QR or payment link stay outside the payment aggregator rules and NPCI restrictions? Fifth, will terms that place responsibility for agent statements on the seller hold against a consumer? Sixth, what is the platform's food-safety licensing position when sellers sell food, what belongs on the restricted-category list, and what is the GST position? Seventh, does each agent type fit WhatsApp's business policy, and what telecom rules apply to any SMS or voice? Eighth, if agents ever contract without human confirmation, how are attribution, mistake and evidence handled?

**Open questions and cheap tests.**

| Open question | Cheap test |
|---|---|
| Is slow or missed reply a real, felt pain? | Interview 20-30 sellers in one category; ask to see last week's chats and count unanswered or late enquiries |
| Will customers chat on an unfamiliar web page? | Give 20 sellers both a web chat link and a WhatsApp link; compare starts, completions and orders over four weeks |
| Do sellers switch on Meta's Business AI, and what does it lack? | Ask the same sellers to try it; record who is eligible, what it gets wrong, and whether it can quote |
| Do owners confirm fast enough? | Measure time from draft order to owner tap; test voice confirmation in the owner's language |
| Does the agent quote accurately from messy price lists? | Shadow mode: the agent drafts, the owner sends; measure how often the owner edits |
| Will sellers pay, and for what? | Offer prepaid packs at two price points to pilot sellers after a free month; count purchases, not stated intent |
| Do buyers want quote collection? | Run it by hand: staff collect three quotes for app users in one category; measure repeat use before building |
| Does disclosure hurt conversion? | Split-test two honest labels (plain "AI assistant" versus AI assistant plus named owner and handover button) |
| What does abuse look like? | Offer a small bounty to friendly testers to break the pilot agent; log every attempt |

## What the research could not establish

Several gaps bear directly on the decision. There is no independent Indian data on enquiry volumes, time spent replying or orders lost. There is no adoption, pricing or quality data for Meta's Business AI. There is no retail-only ONDC data for 2026. The status of the UPI merchant fee and of NPCI's agent protocol is unconfirmed. Meta's rupee rate card was not opened directly. The specifications of the commerce and payment protocols were not read at source. Whether the data protection timetable was accelerated, and whether a second round of intermediary-rule amendments was finalised after September 2026, could not be confirmed. No field study of agent-to-agent trading among ordinary consumers or small businesses exists, in India or anywhere else, and no documented case was found of an agent commerce network getting started in practice. Startup agent marketplaces, travel and booking agents, and consumer haggling bots were not searched specifically.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Should we build an agent marketplace?" turns out to bundle three bets of very different quality: that small sellers benefit from an instant, accurate first reply (plausible and cheap to test), that buyers want quotes collected for them (reasoned from seller spending, with no buyer data), and that independent agents can bargain fairly without supervision (contradicted by every experiment so far). The same design choice, a human confirming each commitment, turns out to be the answer to three separate problems at once: consumer trust, manipulation, and the unsettled Indian law on contracts made by software. That convergence is the strongest signal in the notes.

The strategic risk is not technology or cost. It is that Meta gives the fixed-price version away inside the app sellers already use, and that sellers pay for customers, not for answers. A product that only answers will be squeezed. One that turns a seller's page into a place where priced quotes are requested, approved and recorded builds the one asset a later network needs: a set of verified sellers who can be reached by an agent and who respond with structured offers. If the cheap tests show sellers will not confirm quotes promptly or buy a prepaid pack, stop there; the marketplace would have failed on the same facts at far greater cost.
